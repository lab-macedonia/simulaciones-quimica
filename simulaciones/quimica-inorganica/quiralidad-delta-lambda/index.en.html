The origin of chirality

en = ethane-1,2-diamine (ethylenediamine), H₂N–CH₂–CH₂–NH₂. It is bidentate: each molecule contributes two donor N to adjacent (cis) positions. Three en ligands occupy the six positions of the octahedron and each forms a five-membered chelate ring with the metal.

The curved bands schematically represent the N–CH₂–CH₂–N bridges; the gray spheres are the two C. The colors help to follow the ligands; they do not mean that they are chemically different. H atoms, real bond lengths and ring conformations (lel/ob) are not modeled.

Nomenclature: Δ designates the right-handed helix and Λ the left-handed one, seen along the C₃ axis (IUPAC). Practical rule: right thumb along the axis; if the chelates turn like the fingers of the right hand, it is Δ.

Ideas for discussion

Is an asymmetric carbon needed?

No. In this example the en ligands are achiral; the chirality arises from their overall arrangement around the metal.

Does Δ mean it rotates light to the right?

No. Δ and Λ describe the spatial configuration. The sign of the optical rotation (+ or −) is determined experimentally, under defined conditions and wavelength. For a pair of enantiomers, the values are opposite under the same conditions.

Does removing the bands make the complex achiral?

No. Hiding them changes the representation, not the molecule. But it shows why looking only at the position of the six N is not enough to identify the chirality: you need to know which N are joined to each other.

Why does the Δ ⇄ Λ path go through a prism?

In this one-parameter model (τ), the trigonal prism has a horizontal mirror plane (σh) and is therefore achiral. On passing through it, the helicity changes sign. This is the geometric idea of the Bailar twist. It is not a statement about energies or about the real mechanism, which may be different.

If Δ and Λ can interconvert in the model, do they mix in the laboratory?

It depends on the kinetics, which this model does not describe. Co(III) complexes are kinetically inert and [Co(en)₃]³⁺ can be resolved into its enantiomers; racemization is slow.
