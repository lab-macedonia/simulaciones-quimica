How to read and use the model

  • Spheres and lines: each outer sphere is a ligand; the line to the metal is its coordination bond.
  • Measure: click two ligands and the angle appears (sky-blue arc).
  • Symmetry: “Symmetry axis” cycles through the calculated axes; “Rotate” turns the structure and it falls onto itself; “Look along the axis” changes the viewpoint.
  • Superimpose: the program tries every possible rigid rotation. If it fails, no rotation achieves it: they are different compounds.

These are idealized models of coordination positions: bond lengths are not shown to scale and the interconversion between isomers is not simulated. The dipole vector adds up only the M→Cl vectors and is a qualitative criterion, not a calculation of the real dipole moment.

Question for discussion

Werner's argument: counting isomers to deduce the geometry

Alfred Werner (coordination theory, 1893) reasoned as follows: if the number of isolated isomers is known, only some geometries are possible. For six ligands, the expected number of isomers is:

Assumed geometryMA₄B₂MA₃B₃
Planar hexagon33
Trigonal prism33
Octahedron22
Observed (e.g. cis/trans and fac/mer)22

Only the octahedron matches the observations. Test in module 05 that two Cl⁻ in an octahedron give exactly two isomers, even though there are 15 ways to place them.
