Epidemics, the immune system and vaccines

3D epidemic simulator: person-to-person transmission with the SIR model, the reproduction number R₀ and effective R, prevention measures, immune defences, vaccines and herd immunity.

Open the simulation

⏱ 45 minLevel: Grades 7 and 11–128 multiple-choice questions · 2 essay questions

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Describe the barriers and the innate and adaptive immune responses.
  • Interpret an epidemic curve with the SIR model and the number R.
  • Explain how vaccines and herd immunity work, and argue for their importance in public health.

Prior knowledge

  • Microorganisms and disease.
  • Percentages.

Suggested activity

How much coverage do we need?

  1. With R₀ = 3, simulate coverages of 0, 30, 60 and 80 % and record the peak number of infected.
  2. Calculate the threshold 1 − 1/R₀ and compare it with what you observed.
  3. Repeat with R₀ = 15 (measles).
  4. Write a short message for your community on why vaccination matters.

Common misconceptions

MisconceptionCorrection
“If almost everyone is vaccinated, I don’t need to be.”If many think the same, coverage drops below the threshold and outbreaks return.
“Antibiotics cure colds.”Colds are viral; antibiotics act only on bacteria.
“Vaccines give you the disease.”They create immunity without causing the disease; side effects are usually mild.

  • Shareable scenario and projector mode: copy a link that reopens the simulation with the same parameters (handy for assignments) and enlarge the text for projection.

B. Essay

1. Explain how a vaccine can protect a newborn who cannot yet receive it.

2. With R₀ = 5 and a 90 % effective vaccine, what coverage is needed?

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. C. the skin — Barriers stop pathogens getting in.
  2. B. the lymphocytes of the adaptive response — Memory B and T lymphocytes.
  3. D. creates antibodies and memory without causing the disease — It presents antigens to the immune system.
  4. D. cases decrease — Each infected person infects, on average, fewer than one person.
  5. C. 75 % — 1 − 1/4 = 0.75.
  6. C. those who cannot be vaccinated — The pathogen cannot find chains of transmission.
  7. A. reduces infections and lowers R — Less transmission per contact.
  8. A. bacteria — They do not act on viruses.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. If enough people around it are immune, the pathogen does not circulate (herd immunity).
  2. (1 − 1/5)/0.9 = 89 %.
