Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Classify amino acids according to their side chain.
  • Calculate the pI and the net charge at a given pH.
  • Relate the peptide bond, α-helix, β-sheet and Ramachandran plot.

Prior knowledge

  • Acids and bases (pKa).
  • Functional groups: amine, carboxylic acid, amide.

Suggested activity

Charge of a peptide

  1. Draw a tripeptide (Gly-Lys-Glu).
  2. Calculate the charge at pH 2, 7 and 12.
  3. Check with the simulation.
  4. Estimate the pI and predict toward which electrode it migrates at pH 7.

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“The peptide bond rotates freely.”It has partial double-bond character: planar and trans.
“pI is the pH where there are no charges.”It is where the net charge is zero; there may be internal charges (zwitterion).
“The α-helix and β-sheet are stabilized by S–S bridges.”By backbone hydrogen bonds.

B. Essay

1. Calculate the pI of alanine (pK₁ 2.34; pK₂ 9.69).

2. Explain why proline breaks α-helices.

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. D. zwitterion (–NH₃⁺ and –COO⁻) — With pK(COOH) ≈ 2.3 and pK(NH₃⁺) ≈ 9.7 the carboxyl is deprotonated and the amino group protonated.
  2. C. 5,97 — pI = (2.34 + 9.60)/2 = 5.97.
  3. B. the N lone pair is delocalized toward the C=O (resonance) — Amide resonance makes the C–N bond short (1.33 Å) and forces trans planarity.
  4. A. i + 4 — One bond every four residues closes rings of 13 atoms (3.6₁₃ helix).
  5. D. aspartate — Asp and Glu have a –COOH group in the side chain (pKa 3.65 and 4.25).
  6. C. steric clashes between backbone and side-chain atoms — Only φ and ψ are free and many combinations bring atoms closer than their van der Waals radii.
  7. B. C=O and N–H of neighboring strands — Adjacent strands pair their backbone C=O and N–H groups.
  8. A. −1 — At pH 12 the α-NH₃⁺ (pK 8.95) and the side-chain NH₃⁺ (pK 10.53) have already lost their proton; only the α-COO⁻ remains: charge −1.
  9. A. low (≈ 3) — Two carboxyl groups.
  10. A. condensation — It loses H₂O.
  11. B. 3.6 residues per turn — Classic value: 3.6.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. pI = (2,34 + 9,69)/2 = 6,02.
  2. Its secondary N lacks the H for the hydrogen bond and its ring restricts φ.
