Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Explain the self-assembly of amphipathic lipids.
  • Relate unsaturations and cholesterol to fluidity.
  • Differentiate passive and active transport.

Prior knowledge

  • Polarity and the hydrophobic effect.
  • Fatty acids.

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“Cholesterol only stiffens.”It buffers: it stiffens the fluid and fluidizes the gel.
“Water crosses any membrane freely.”It crosses by slow diffusion and through aquaporins.
“Active transport does not use energy.”It uses ATP or a gradient.

B. Essay

1. Explain the hydrophobic effect in self-assembly.

2. Compare simple diffusion, facilitated diffusion and active transport.

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. C. has a hydrophilic part and a hydrophobic part — Phospholipids have a polar head and nonpolar tails.
  2. B. it introduces a kink, lowers the Tm and increases fluidity — The kink prevents compact packing of the tails.
  3. A. bilayers and liposomes — Their cylindrical shape favors sheets; they close into vesicles to hide the edges.
  4. D. orders the tails and reduces fluidity — Its rigid ring restricts the movement of neighboring tails.
  5. C. simple diffusion — It is small and nonpolar: it dissolves in the hydrophobic interior.
  6. B. 3 Na⁺ out and 2 K⁺ in — It is electrogenic: it removes one net positive charge per cycle.
  7. A. gel (ordered tails) — Extended and packed tails reduce lateral diffusion.
  8. D. a facilitated transporter (GLUT) — It is polar and large: it needs a protein, but not energy.
  9. B. lowers Tm — It bends the chain and reduces packing.
  10. A. 3 Na⁺ and brings in 2 K⁺ — 3 Na⁺ out, 2 K⁺ in.
  11. B. micelles — Conical geometry.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. Water forms ordered structures around the nonpolar tails; grouping them releases water and increases the entropy of the solvent.
  2. Simple: down the gradient without a protein; facilitated: down the gradient with a channel or carrier; active: against the gradient with energy.
