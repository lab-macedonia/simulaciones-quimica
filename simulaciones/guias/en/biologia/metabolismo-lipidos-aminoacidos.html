Lipid and Amino Acid Metabolism

β-oxidation of fatty acids in 3D (four reactions per cycle, FADH₂, NADH and acetyl-CoA), ATP balance by chain length, carnitine transport, transamination with PLP, the urea cycle and the fate of the carbon skeleton.

Open the simulation

⏱ 55 minLevel: undergraduate, early years9 multiple-choice questions · 2 essay questions

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Describe β-oxidation and calculate the ATP.
  • Explain the role of carnitine.
  • Relate transamination and the urea cycle.

Prior knowledge

  • Energy metabolism.
  • Coenzymes.

Suggested activity

ATP from a fatty acid

  1. For palmitate (C16) and stearate (C18) calculate the cycles of β-oxidation.
  2. Count NADH, FADH₂ and acetyl-CoA.
  3. Convert to ATP with 7n − 6.
  4. Compare with glucose per gram.

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“Fatty acids enter the mitochondrion freely.”They require carnitine.
“β-oxidation occurs in the cytosol.”In the mitochondrial matrix.
“NH₄⁺ is safe to accumulate.”It is toxic; it is converted to urea.

B. Essay

1. Calculate the net ATP of palmitate and compare it with glucose per mole.

2. Describe transamination.

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. B. 7 — n/2 − 1 = 7.
  2. A. 106 — 7·16 − 6.
  3. D. acyl groups into the matrix — Acyl-carnitine shuttle.
  4. C. PLP (vitamin B6) — Pyridoxal phosphate.
  5. B. urea — Urea cycle in the liver.
  6. A. acetyl-CoA — Leucine and lysine.
  7. A. 7 — n/2 − 1.
  8. B. PLP — Derived from B₆.
  9. B. liver — Hepatocytes.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. 7 × 16 − 6 = 106 ATP; glucose: ≈ 32 ATP.
  2. The amino group of an amino acid is passed to α-ketoglutarate forming glutamate; the amino acid becomes an α-keto acid (PLP as coenzyme).
