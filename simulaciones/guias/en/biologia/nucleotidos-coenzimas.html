Nucleotides and Coenzymes

ATP, NAD⁺/NADH, FAD/FADH₂ and coenzyme A in 3D: modular structure (base, ribose, phosphates), precursor vitamins, ATP hydrolysis, hydride transfer and coupled energy.

Open the simulation

⏱ 45 minLevel: undergraduate, early years8 multiple-choice questions · 2 essay questions

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Describe the parts of a nucleotide.
  • Explain the role of ATP, NAD⁺, FAD and CoA.
  • Relate B vitamins to coenzymes.

Prior knowledge

  • Functional groups: phosphates, amides.
  • Redox reactions.

Suggested activity

Coenzyme cards

  1. Make cards with structure, function and precursor vitamin.
  2. With the simulation identify the transfer site (C4 of NAD⁺).
  3. Relate each card to a metabolic reaction.
  4. Present one to the class.

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“ATP stores energy in the bond.”Energy is released because of the stability of the products (ADP + Pᵢ).
“NADH and NADPH serve the same purpose.”NADH, catabolic; NADPH, biosynthetic.
“The vitamin is the coenzyme.”It is its precursor.

B. Essay

1. Compare NAD⁺ and FAD in redox reactions.

2. Explain why ATP hydrolysis is exergonic.

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. D. base + pentose + phosphate — Nucleoside + phosphate(s).
  2. C. niacin (B3) — Pellagra by deficiency.
  3. B. a hydride ion at C4 — Reduction to 1,4-dihydropyridine.
  4. A. thioesters — With its –SH group.
  5. D. −30.5 kJ/mol — Exergonic reaction.
  6. B. C4 — C4 of the nicotinamide ring.
  7. B. thioester — High transfer potential.
  8. B. FAD — FMN and FAD.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. NAD⁺ accepts a hydride (2 e⁻ + 1 H⁺); FAD accepts 2 e⁻ and 2 H⁺ and can pass through a semiquinone.
  2. It relieves charge repulsion, and stabilizes Pᵢ by resonance and the products by solvation.
