Populations and ecosystems

3D ecology simulator: levels of organisation, exponential and logistic growth, carrying capacity, the predator–prey cycle (culpeo fox and degu), competition with an invasive species and the effects of habitat loss and hunting.

Open the simulation

⏱ 45 minLevel: Grade 98 multiple-choice questions · 2 essay questions

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Distinguish individual, population, community and ecosystem.
  • Explain the factors that regulate population size (resources, predation, competition).
  • Evaluate the effects of human activity and invasive species on a Chilean ecosystem.

Prior knowledge

  • Food chains and food webs.
  • Reading graphs.

Suggested activity

What happens if we remove the fox?

  1. Run the predator–prey cycle and record the degu and fox peaks.
  2. Turn on fox hunting and record what happens to the degus and the vegetation.
  3. Repeat with 50 % habitat loss.
  4. Write a recommendation for managing a protected area.

Common misconceptions

MisconceptionCorrection
“Without predators, prey would be better off.”They grow until food runs out and then crash; predation regulates the population.
“Populations always grow.”Limited resources level them off at the carrying capacity.
“An introduced species increases biodiversity.”Invasives usually displace native species and reduce it.

Classroom tools

The simulation’s «Class» button gathers these tools. None needs an account or a connection: everything stays in the student’s browser.

  • Practice: problems with new values or cases on every attempt, instant feedback and a worked solution. Types: Exponential growth; Growth rate; Logistic growth; Level of organisation; Type of interaction. Use it as a lesson wrap-up (10 minutes) or homework: ask the class for a streak of 5 correct answers.
  • Predict → observe → explain: the guided lesson pauses at stages 3 and 5 and asks for a prediction before showing the result. Project it and take a show of hands before continuing.
  • Report: records practice, the challenge and predictions with time and result, plus a screenshot and the student’s notes. It can be printed or saved as PDF to hand in. It survives closing the tab and clears itself after 12 hours of inactivity; on shared computers, ask students to use «Start over» when they finish.
  • Shareable scenario and projector mode: copy a link that reopens the simulation with the same parameters (handy for assignments) and enlarge the text for projection.

B. Essay

1. Explain why the fox peak comes after the degu peak.

2. Suggest two measures to protect the degu from the European rabbit.

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. C. a population — Same species, same place and time.
  2. B. the community and the abiotic factors — Biotic + abiotic.
  3. D. resources are plentiful and nothing limits growth — J curve.
  4. D. the maximum number of individuals the environment can sustain — Set by food, space, water…
  5. C. after the prey peak — First the prey become abundant, then the predators that eat them.
  6. C. an invasive species — Introduced and without natural control, it displaces native species.
  7. A. increase until the food runs out — Without predation they grow to the carrying capacity or beyond.
  8. A. food shortage — It has more effect the denser the population.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. Foxes need time to breed while food is plentiful; when they are numerous, prey are already declining.
  2. Control of the rabbit population and conservation or restoration of native habitat.
