Membrane potential and action potential

Nernst and Goldman equations for K⁺ and Na⁺, resting potential, Hodgkin–Huxley-type model with adjustable stimulus, threshold, refractory period and blockers (TTX, TEA), with a 3D axon where channels open and the impulse propagates.

⏱ 60 minLevel: undergraduate, early years9 multiple-choice questions · 2 essay questions

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Calculate equilibrium potentials with Nernst.
  • Explain the resting potential (Goldman) and the action potential.
  • Describe the threshold, the refractory period and the effect of TTX and TEA.

Prior knowledge

  • Ion gradients.
  • Membrane channels.

#StageCore idea
1Equilibrium potential (Nernst)E = 61.5 mV · log([ion]ext/[ion]int)
2Resting potential (Goldman)Vm depends on the relative permeability of each ion
3Action potentialDepolarization (Na⁺ enters) → repolarization (K⁺ exits)
4Threshold and refractory periodAfter a spike, Na⁺ channels are inactive: it cannot fire again
5Blockers: TTX and TEATTX blocks Na⁺ channels; TEA blocks K⁺ channels

Suggested activity

Fire the neuron

  1. Calculate E(K⁺) and E(Na⁺) with your concentrations.
  2. Apply a subthreshold stimulus and then one above threshold.
  3. Block Na⁺ with TTX and observe.
  4. Block K⁺ with TEA and compare the repolarization.

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“The resting potential is E(K⁺).”It is close to E(K⁺) but is set by Goldman: it depends on several ions.
“A larger stimulus produces a larger potential.”It is all-or-none above the threshold.
“TTX blocks K⁺ channels.”It blocks the voltage-gated Na⁺ channels.

B. Essay

1. Calculate E(K⁺) with 5 mM outside and 140 mM inside at 37 °C.

2. Explain why the action potential is all-or-none.

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. C. less negative — E = 61.5·log(ext/int): rises.
  2. B. Na⁺ entry — Voltage-gated Na⁺ channels.
  3. A. Na⁺ channels — It eliminates the depolarization phase.
  4. D. the Na⁺ channels are inactivated — Another potential cannot be generated.
  5. C. the membrane is much more permeable to K⁺ — Goldman weights by permeability.
  6. B. a local depolarization without an action potential — It is all-or-none.
  7. A. Na⁺ influx — Open Na⁺ channels.
  8. A. becomes less negative — Smaller gradient.
  9. A. inactivation of Na⁺ channels — They must recover.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. 61.5 · log(5/140) ≈ −89 mV.
  2. Positive feedback of the Na⁺ channels once the threshold is exceeded.
