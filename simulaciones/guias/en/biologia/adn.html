Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Describe nucleotides, base pairing and the double helix.
  • Explain semiconservative replication and transcription.
  • Use the genetic code to translate an mRNA.

Prior knowledge

  • Hydrogen bonds.
  • Structure of sugars and bases.

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“A pairs with G.”A–T (2 hydrogen bonds) and G–C (3).
“Replication is conservative.”It is semiconservative.
“RNA contains thymine.”It contains uracil.

B. Essay

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. B. 3 — G≡C has three; A=T only two, which is why more GC increases Tm.
  2. A. 5′-GCAT-3′ — Complement 3′-TACG-5′; read 5′→3′ it is GCAT (reverse complement).
  3. D. 10 — 10 bp per turn, pitch 3.4 nm (0.34 nm per pair).
  4. C. each daughter molecule has one original strand and one new strand — Demonstrated by Meselson and Stahl with heavy nitrogen (¹⁵N).
  5. B. uracil — RNA uses uracil instead of thymine: A (template) → U (RNA).
  6. A. AUG (Met) — AUG codes for methionine and marks the start; UAA, UAG and UGA are stop codons.
  7. D. A = T y G = C — Each purine pairs with a specific pyrimidine.
  8. C. a higher Tm — More G–C pairs (3 H bonds) stabilize the duplex.
  9. A. 20 % — A = T = 30 %; G + C = 40 % → G = 20 %.
  10. B. 3′→5′ — It synthesizes 5′→3′.
  11. B. 3 bases — Triplets.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. DNA polymerase only synthesizes 5′→3′, and the fork advances in the opposite direction for that strand.
  2. 1000/10.5 ≈ 95 turns.
