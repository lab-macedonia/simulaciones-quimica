Cell signaling: receptor → second messenger

Cascade of a G protein-coupled receptor: ligand, receptor occupancy, G protein, adenylyl cyclase, cAMP, PKA and phosphodiesterase, with amplification, dose–response curves (Hill, EC50), partial agonists, antagonists, cholera toxin and comparison with a receptor tyrosine kinase.

Open the simulation

⏱ 55 minLevel: undergraduate, early years9 multiple-choice questions · 2 essay questions

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Relate receptor occupancy and response.
  • Describe the GPCR → G protein → cAMP → PKA cascade.
  • Distinguish agonists, antagonists and the RTK signal.

Prior knowledge

  • Membrane proteins.
  • Enzymes.

Suggested activity

Dose–response curve

  1. Vary the ligand concentration.
  2. Note the occupancy and the active PKA.
  3. Add a competitive antagonist.
  4. Describe the shift of the curve.

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“A receptor activates a single molecule.”One activation can recruit many G proteins (amplification).
“The cAMP signal lasts indefinitely.”Phosphodiesterase degrades it.
“A competitive antagonist reduces the maximal response.”It shifts the curve to the right; the maximum is reached with more ligand.

B. Essay

1. Calculate θ with [L] = 3 nM and Kd = 1 nM.

2. Compare GPCR and RTK.

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. C. 0,5 — θ = K/(K + K) = 0,5.
  2. B. several G proteins — First amplification.
  3. A. degrades cAMP and shuts off the signal — cAMP → AMP.
  4. D. on the right — More agonist is needed.
  5. C. excess cAMP from permanently active Gα — It keeps adenylyl cyclase active.
  6. B. dimerization and autophosphorylation — Then Ras → MAPK.
  7. A. cAMP — Produced by adenylyl cyclase.
  8. A. 0,5 — [L]/([L]+Kd).
  9. A. degrades cAMP — It terminates the signal.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. 3/(3+1) = 0,75.
  2. GPCR: G protein and second messenger; RTK: dimerization and autophosphorylation.
