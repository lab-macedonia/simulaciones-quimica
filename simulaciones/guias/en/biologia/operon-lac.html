The lac operon and gene regulation

Regulation of the E. coli lac operon: LacI repressor, operator, promoter and lacZYA genes, effect of lactose (allolactose) and glucose (cAMP–CAP), β-galactosidase expression level and lacI⁻ and lacOᶜ mutants in 3D.

Open the simulation

⏱ 55 minLevel: undergraduate, early years9 multiple-choice questions · 2 essay questions

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Identify the components of the lac operon.
  • Predict expression according to lactose and glucose.
  • Interpret the effect of lacI⁻ and lacOᶜ mutants.

Prior knowledge

  • Transcription and translation.
  • Promoter and RNA polymerase.

Simulation walkthrough

Use the lesson (bottom player) for the guided walkthrough and the Lab tab to explore freely; “Back to the lesson” resumes the script.

#StageCore idea
1Operon componentslacI encodes the repressor; P is the promoter; O the operator; lacZ, lacY and lacA the structural genes
2Without lactose: repressionThe repressor blocks the operator and RNA polymerase does not advance
3With lactose: inductionAllolactose binds to the repressor and releases it from the operator
4Glucose and cAMP–CAPWith glucose, cAMP is low, CAP–cAMP does not form and the promoter is weak
5Mutants (challenge)lacI⁻ and lacOᶜ express without lactose

Suggested activity

Four-condition table

  1. Complete a table with ± lactose and ± glucose.
  2. Predict the expression level in each case.
  3. Check with the simulation.
  4. Repeat with lacI⁻ and lacOᶜ.

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“Lactose binds to the operator.”Allolactose binds to the repressor, which is released from the operator.
“With glucose the operon is repressed by the repressor.”With glucose, cAMP is low and the promoter is weak (catabolite repression).
“lacI⁻ is a mutation in the promoter.”It affects the repressor gene.

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. A. binds to the operator and blocks transcription — Negative control.
  2. D. allolactose — It binds to the repressor and releases it.
  3. C. low — Catabolite repression: CAP inactive.
  4. B. is expressed without lactose — The repressor does not recognize the operator.
  5. A. β-galactosidase — It hydrolyzes lactose into glucose and galactose.
  6. D. an activator (positive control) — It helps recruit RNA polymerase.
  7. A. low — The repressor blocks O.
  8. A. allolactose — It binds to the repressor.
  9. A. expresses without lactose — The repressor does not recognize O.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. No repressor at O and with CAP–cAMP activating the promoter.
  2. Constitutive expression (no functional repressor).
