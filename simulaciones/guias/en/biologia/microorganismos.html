Microorganisms and biotechnology

3D microorganism simulator: structure and size of bacteria, yeasts and viruses, the bacterial growth curve, a virus’s lytic cycle, fungi, antibiotic resistance and biotechnology: fermentation, yoghurt and copper biomining in Chile.

Open the simulation

⏱ 45 minLevel: Grade 78 multiple-choice questions · 2 essay questions

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Compare the structure and size of bacteria, fungi and viruses.
  • Explain bacterial growth, how antibiotics act and resistance.
  • Describe biotechnological uses of bacteria and fungi, including biomining in Chile.

Prior knowledge

  • The cell: prokaryotic and eukaryotic.
  • Powers of 2 and the idea of a logarithmic scale.

Suggested activity

Responsible use of antibiotics

  1. Grow bacteria without antibiotic and identify the four phases of the curve.
  2. Repeat applying the antibiotic and record the percentage of resistant bacteria.
  3. Try a low and a high dose: which leaves more resistant bacteria?
  4. Write three recommendations for the responsible use of antibiotics.

Common misconceptions

MisconceptionCorrection
“All microorganisms are harmful.”Most are harmless or useful: digestion, food, cycling of matter.
“Antibiotics work against viruses.”They only act on bacteria.
“A bacterium becomes resistant because it gets used to the antibiotic.”Resistant bacteria already existed; the antibiotic selects them.

B. Essay

1. Explain why bread rises and why the alcohol disappears when it is baked.

2. A bacterium doubles every 30 min. How many will there be after 5 h, starting from 10?

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. C. are not cells and only multiply inside one — They are obligate parasites.
  2. B. prokaryotic, with no nucleus — Their DNA is in the nucleoid.
  3. D. 64 — 6 generations: 2⁶ = 64.
  4. D. the number of bacteria holds steady because resources run out — Births and deaths balance.
  5. C. bacterial infections — They do not act on viruses.
  6. C. are used needlessly or the course is stopped early — Resistant bacteria are selected.
  7. A. the CO₂ yeast produces when it ferments — Alcoholic fermentation.
  8. A. release copper from ores (bioleaching) — Biomining with Acidithiobacillus.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. Yeast ferments glucose producing CO₂ (bubbles in the dough) and ethanol, which evaporates in the heat.
  2. 10 generations: 10·2¹⁰ = 10,240.
