Mendelian genetics and Hardy–Weinberg

Monohybrid and dihybrid Punnett squares with phenotypic ratios, χ² test against a simulated sample of offspring, and Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium with selection against the recessive and genetic drift.

Open the simulation

⏱ 55 minLevel: undergraduate, early years9 multiple-choice questions · 2 essay questions

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Construct and interpret Punnett squares for monohybrid and dihybrid crosses.
  • Apply the χ² test to observed proportions.
  • Calculate allele and genotype frequencies with Hardy–Weinberg.

Prior knowledge

  • Dominant and recessive alleles.
  • Meiosis.

Simulation walkthrough

Use the lesson (bottom player) for the guided walkthrough and the Lab tab to explore freely; “Back to the lesson” resumes the script.

#StageCore idea
1Monohybrid Punnett squareAa × Aa → 3 dominant : 1 recessive (1 AA : 2 Aa : 1 aa)
2Dihybrid crossAaBb × AaBb → 9 : 3 : 3 : 1
3χ² testCompare observed with expected: χ² = Σ(O − E)²/E
4Hardy–Weinberg equilibriump² + 2pq + q² = 1 if no selection, drift, mutation or migration
5Selection and driftSelection changes q predictably; random drift, especially in small populations

Suggested activity

Crosses and equilibrium

  1. Cross Aa × Aa and note the proportions.
  2. Do the dihybrid cross AaBb × AaBb.
  3. Calculate χ² with the simulated sample.
  4. In Hardy–Weinberg, vary q and compare genotypes.

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“An exact 3:1 must be seen in every sample.”Samples vary: χ² is used.
“Dominant means more frequent.”Not necessarily: it depends on the allele frequencies.
“Hardy–Weinberg always holds.”It requires no selection, drift, mutation or migration.

B. Essay

1. In a population with 16 % aa, what is q and the frequency of heterozygotes?

2. Calculate χ² for 290 : 110 versus 3 : 1 (n = 400).

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. B. 3 : 1 — 1 AA : 2 Aa : 1 aa.
  2. A. 9 : 3 : 3 : 1 — 16 cells.
  3. D. 0,09 — q² = 0,09.
  4. C. the data are consistent with the expected ratio — The difference may be due to chance.
  5. B. small populations — Gamete sampling varies more.
  6. A. homozygous recessive — It reveals the unknown genotype.
  7. A. 9:3:3:1 — Independent assortment.
  8. A. 0,09 — q².
  9. A. 0,42 — 2pq.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. q = 0,4; p = 0,6; 2pq = 0,48.
  2. E = 300 and 100; χ² = 100/300 + 100/100 = 1.33 (df = 1) → consistent with 3:1.
