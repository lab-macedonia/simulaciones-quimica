PCR and gel electrophoresis

Thermocycler with denaturation, primer annealing and extension, 2ⁿ exponential amplification, effect of annealing temperature (Tm) and reading of bands on an agarose gel with a molecular-weight ladder.

Open the simulation

⏱ 55 minLevel: undergraduate, early years9 multiple-choice questions · 2 essay questions

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Describe the three stages of a PCR cycle.
  • Calculate the expected amplification and relate Ta to Tm.
  • Interpret an agarose gel with ladder and controls.

Prior knowledge

  • DNA structure and replication.
  • Concept of enzyme.

Suggested activity

Optimize a PCR

  1. Set 30 cycles and vary the annealing temperature between 45 and 70 °C.
  2. Observe the efficiency and the nonspecific bands.
  3. Choose the optimal Ta for the primer in the simulation.
  4. Predict the position of the 500 bp band relative to the ladder.

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“PCR copies DNA indefinitely.”It reaches a plateau due to lack of reagents.
“The higher the annealing temperature, the better.”Too high prevents the primers from binding.
“DNA migrates to the negative pole.”It is negatively charged and migrates to the positive pole.

B. Essay

1. Explain why a no-template control is included.

2. With 90 % efficiency, how many times does the DNA increase in 10 cycles?

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. A. denaturation, annealing, extension — 94 °C, ~55 °C, 72 °C.
  2. D. 1024 times more DNA — 2¹⁰.
  3. C. nonspecific bands — The primers bind to partial sites.
  4. B. the positive pole — Negative charge from the phosphates.
  5. A. travel a greater distance — They pass through the mesh more easily.
  6. D. detect contamination — It should show no band.
  7. A. it withstands 94 °C without denaturing — It comes from Thermus aquaticus.
  8. A. ~10⁶-fold — 2²⁰ ≈ 10⁶.
  9. A. largest — Large fragments advance less.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. It detects DNA contamination: no band should appear.
  2. 1.9¹⁰ ≈ 614-fold.
