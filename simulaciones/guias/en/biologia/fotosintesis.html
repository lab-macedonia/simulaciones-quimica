Photosynthesis: light reactions and the Calvin cycle

3D simulator of photosynthesis: chloroplast structure, light absorption, water photolysis, electron transport chain, H⁺ gradient and ATP synthase, NADPH production and the Calvin cycle with CO₂ fixation, reduction and regeneration.

Open the simulation

⏱ 55 minLevel: undergraduate, early years11 multiple-choice questions · 2 essay questions

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Describe electron transport in the light reactions.
  • Explain the H⁺ gradient and ATP synthesis.
  • Follow the Calvin cycle and its balance.

Prior knowledge

  • Cellular respiration.
  • Redox.

Simulation walkthrough

Use the lesson (bottom player) for the guided walkthrough and the Lab tab to explore freely; “Back to the lesson” resumes the script.

#StageCore idea
1The chloroplastStacked thylakoids (grana) in the stroma: the light reactions happen in the membrane, the Calvin cycle in the stroma
2Light and water photolysisPhotosystem II: light excites chlorophyll P680 and water supplies electrons
3Electron transport and H⁺ gradientPSII → plastoquinone → cytochrome b₆f → plastocyanin → PSI; b₆f pumps H⁺ into the lumen
4ATP synthase and NADPHH⁺ return to the stroma through ATP synthase (ADP + Pᵢ → ATP); PSI reduces NADP⁺ to NADPH
5Calvin cycle: fixation and reductionRuBisCO fixes CO₂ onto RuBP (5C) → 2 × 3-PGA; then ATP and NADPH reduce it to G3P
6Regeneration and balanceOf 6 G3P: 1 leaves as product and 5 regenerate 3 RuBP (3 ATP)

Suggested activity

Inputs and outputs

  1. Draw the chloroplast and label compartments.
  2. Note the reactants and products at each stage.
  3. Use the simulation to inhibit PSII with DCMU.
  4. Predict effects on O₂, ATP and NADPH.

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“O₂ comes from CO₂.”It comes from water.
“The Calvin cycle does not depend on light.”It depends on ATP and NADPH from the light reactions.
“Plants are green because they absorb green.”They reflect green and absorb blue and red.

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. C. From water — Water photolysis in PSII releases O₂.
  2. B. the thylakoid membrane — The Calvin cycle occurs in the stroma.
  3. A. the H⁺ gradient between lumen and stroma — Proton-motive force: the H⁺ leave the lumen into the stroma.
  4. D. RuBisCO — Ribulose-1,5-bisphosphate carboxylase/oxygenase.
  5. C. 3 ATP and 2 NADPH — 2 ATP + 2 NADPH in the reduction and 1 ATP in the regeneration.
  6. B. the flow from PSII — It blocks the plastoquinone site in PSII.
  7. A. 1 is net product and 5 regenerate RuBP — Carbon balance: 5 × 3 C = 15 C = 3 RuBP.
  8. D. green — They absorb blue and red.
  9. A. CO₂ fixation — CO₂ + RuBP.
  10. B. 3 ATP, 2 NADPH — Classic stoichiometry.
  11. B. NADP⁺ — NADPH is formed.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂. Water gives up electrons, which end up in NADPH and reduce CO₂.
  2. RuBisCO fixes O₂ instead of CO₂; carbon and energy are lost, more so at high T.
