Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Interpret catalysis as a lowering of the activation energy.
  • Apply the Michaelis–Menten and Lineweaver–Burk equations.
  • Distinguish competitive and noncompetitive inhibition.

Prior knowledge

  • Chemical kinetics.
  • Equilibrium.

  1. With the simulation measure v at different [S].
  2. Plot v versus [S] and 1/v versus 1/[S].
  3. Obtain Km and Vmax.
  4. Repeat with an inhibitor and classify it.

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“The enzyme changes the ΔG of the reaction.”It only lowers Ea; it does not change the equilibrium.
“A high Km means high affinity.”A high Km indicates lower affinity.
“The competitive inhibitor lowers Vmax.”It raises Km and leaves Vmax unchanged.

B. Essay

1. With Vmax = 100 µM/min and Km = 5 µM, calculate v at [S] = 20 µM.

2. Explain why an enzyme denatures at high temperatures.

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. C. lowers the activation energy — It stabilizes the transition state; it does not alter ΔG or the equilibrium and it is regenerated.
  2. B. the [S] at which v = Vmax/2 — A lower Km indicates higher affinity for the substrate.
  3. A. increases Km and does not change Vmax — It competes for the active site; with a lot of substrate Vmax is reached anyway.
  4. D. 1/Vmax — 1/v = (Km/Vmax)(1/[S]) + 1/Vmax; the slope is Km/Vmax.
  5. C. destroys its structure and the active site — The weak interactions that maintain the folding are broken; the sequence (primary structure) does not change.
  6. B. approaches Vmax (zero order with respect to S) — All enzymes are saturated: v ≈ Vmax = kcat[E]ₜ.
  7. A. the enzyme changes shape on binding the substrate — Substrate binding reshapes the active site to optimize catalysis.
  8. D. to a site other than the active one — It deforms the enzyme and lowers Vmax; Km (affinity) does not change.
  9. B. Vmax/2 — Definition of Km.
  10. B. lowers Vmax — It binds outside the active site and reduces Vmax.
  11. A. 1/Vmax — 1/v at 1/[S] = 0.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. v = 100 × 20/(5 + 20) = 80 µM/min.
  2. Weak interactions (hydrogen bonds, hydrophobic) that maintain the 3D structure are broken, and the active site is lost.
