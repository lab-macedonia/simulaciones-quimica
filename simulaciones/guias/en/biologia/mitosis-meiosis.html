Mitosis and Meiosis

3D simulator of cell division: cell cycle and interphase, phases of mitosis, meiosis I and II, synapsis and crossing over, independent assortment of homologous chromosomes, DNA content per cell and comparison of both processes.

Open the simulation

⏱ 50 minLevel: undergraduate, early years11 multiple-choice questions · 2 essay questions

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Order the phases of mitosis and meiosis.
  • Differentiate the genetic outcomes of both.
  • Calculate the variability from independent assortment.

Prior knowledge

  • Chromosomes, chromatids and ploidy.
  • DNA and the cell cycle.

  3. Identify crossing over.
  4. Calculate the possible combinations in humans (n = 23).

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“Meiosis I separates chromatids.”It separates homologs; meiosis II separates chromatids.
“Mitosis produces haploid cells.”It produces cells identical to the mother cell.
“DNA is duplicated in every division.”Only in the preceding S phase.

B. Essay

1. Calculate the possible chromosome combinations in human gametes without crossing over.

2. Explain why meiosis is a source of variability.

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. B. the homologous chromosomes — Each homolog (with its two chromatids) goes to a pole.
  2. A. 2 identical 2n cells — Clonal reproduction.
  3. D. prophase I — During synapsis of the homologs.
  4. C. DNA replication — The DNA content goes from 2C to 4C.
  5. B. 1C — Haploid cells with single-chromatid chromosomes.
  6. A. 8 combinations — 2³ = 8.
  7. D. chromosomes aligned on the equatorial plate — All the kinetochores attached to the spindle at the equator.
  8. C. a mitosis, without prior replication — Sister chromatids separate.
  9. B. 4 n cells — Two divisions.
  10. A. prophase I — Pachytene.
  11. C. 8 — 2³.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. 2²³ ≈ 8.4 million.
  2. Through crossing over and independent assortment of homologs, in addition to random fertilization.
