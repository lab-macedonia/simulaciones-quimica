Energy metabolism 3D

Glycolysis, Krebs cycle, respiratory chain and ATP synthase, aerobic balance versus fermentation, and inhibitors (rotenone, cyanide, oligomycin, DNP).

⏱ 55 minLevel: undergraduate, early years11 multiple-choice questions · 2 essay questions

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Follow the flow glucose → pyruvate → acetyl-CoA → CO₂.
  • Count ATP, NADH and FADH₂ at each stage.
  • Interpret the action of inhibitors and uncouplers.

Prior knowledge

  • Enzymes and coenzymes.
  • Redox reactions.

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“Glycolysis needs O₂.”It is anaerobic.
“The Krebs cycle produces most of the ATP directly.”Most comes from oxidative phosphorylation.
“O₂ is converted into CO₂.”CO₂ comes from acetyl-CoA; O₂ is reduced to water.

B. Essay

1. Calculate the total ATP from one glucose under aerobic conditions.

2. Explain the Pasteur effect.

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. B. 2 ATP + 2 NADH — 2 ATP are spent and 4 are produced; in addition 2 NADH (G3P-DH).
  2. A. the mitochondrial matrix — The enzymes of the cycle are in the matrix (except succinate DH, in the inner membrane).
  3. D. O₂ — Complex IV reduces ½O₂ to H₂O with 2 H⁺.
  4. C. complex IV — It binds to the Fe of cytochrome c oxidase: the chain stops.
  5. B. dissipates the H⁺ gradient: more O₂ consumed, less ATP — It carries H⁺ across the membrane; the chain works at full speed and the energy is released as heat.
  6. A. blocks the H⁺ channel of ATP synthase — The gradient becomes maximal and brakes the pumping, so O₂ consumption ceases.
  7. D. 2 CO₂ — Isocitrate DH and α-ketoglutarate DH decarboxylate; the 2 C of the acetyl leave (in the following turn).
  8. C. regenerate NAD⁺ — Without NAD⁺ glycolysis would stop.
  9. B. 2 — 4 produced − 2 consumed.
  10. B. dissipates the H⁺ gradient — Heat is lost, not ATP.
  11. B. 2.5 ATP — Modern estimate.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. 2 ATP (glycolysis) + 2 GTP (Krebs) + 10 NADH × 2.5 + 2 FADH₂ × 1.5 = approximately 32 ATP.
  2. In the presence of O₂ fermentation is inhibited and the more efficient aerobic metabolism is used.
