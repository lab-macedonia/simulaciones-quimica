Drugs and Receptors

Drug–receptor interaction in 3D: occupancy according to Kd, agonists, partial agonists and antagonists, dose–response curves (EC₅₀), competitive antagonism with curve shift and Lipinski's rules for oral absorption.

Open the simulation

⏱ 50 minLevel: undergraduate, early years8 multiple-choice questions · 2 essay questions

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Calculate receptor occupancy with Kd.
  • Distinguish agonists, partial agonists and antagonists.
  • Apply Lipinski's rule.

Prior knowledge

  • Equilibrium and Kd.
  • Logarithms.

Suggested activity

Dose–response curves

  1. With the simulation plot the curve of an agonist.
  2. Add a competitive antagonist and observe the shift.
  3. Calculate the dose ratio and the Schild plot.
  4. Evaluate a candidate with Lipinski.

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“An antagonist activates the receptor.”It binds without activating it (efficacy 0).
“EC₅₀ is the lethal dose.”It is the concentration that gives 50 % of the maximum effect.
“A competitive antagonist reduces Emax.”It only shifts the curve to the right.

B. Essay

1. A drug has Kd = 10 nM. What concentration gives 90 % occupancy?

2. Evaluate with Lipinski a molecule with MW 620 and logP 6.

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. B. 50 % — [L]/([L] + Kd) = ½.
  2. A. zero — It binds without activating.
  3. D. to the right, with the same Emax — It increases the EC₅₀.
  4. C. does not reach the Emax of the full one — e < 1.
  5. B. 500 g/mol — For oral absorption.
  6. C. 75 % — 3/(3+1).
  7. A. activates less than a full one — Lower efficacy.
  8. A. ≤ 500 — For good oral bioavailability.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. θ = [L]/(Kd + [L]) = 0,9 → [L] = 9 Kd = 90 nM.
  2. It violates two criteria (MW > 500 and logP > 5): poor oral bioavailability is likely.
