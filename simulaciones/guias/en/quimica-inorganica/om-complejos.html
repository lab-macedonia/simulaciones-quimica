Molecular orbitals in octahedral complexes

3D simulator of molecular orbital theory in octahedral ML6 complexes: σ bonding, a1g, t1u, eg and t2g orbitals, Δo splitting, π effect (donor and acceptor), spectrochemical series, high and low spin, magnetism and color.

⏱ 60 minLevel: undergraduate, early years11 multiple-choice questions · 2 essay questions

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Build the MO diagram of an octahedral ML₆ complex with σ bonding.
  • Explain the effect of π-donor and π-acceptor ligands on Δo.
  • Predict spin, color and magnetism.

Prior knowledge

  • Crystal field theory.
  • Molecular orbitals of diatomics.

Suggested activity

From CFT to MO

  1. Compare the crystal field diagram with the MO diagram in the simulation.
  2. Identify the nonbonding t₂g and the antibonding eg*.
  3. Add a π-acceptor ligand and observe the change in Δo.
  4. Explain the spectrochemical series in terms of π bonding.

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“The t₂g are always nonbonding.”They are only with σ-only ligands; with π ligands they interact.
“π-donors increase Δo.”They decrease it: the t₂g are raised.
“Back-donation weakens the M–L bond.”It strengthens it, and weakens C≡O.

B. Essay

1. Explain the order I⁻ < F⁻ < H₂O < NH₃ < CN⁻ < CO in terms of π bonding.

2. Calculate the absorbed λ for Δo = 25,000 cm⁻¹.

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. A. nonbonding — No ligand points toward them.
  2. D. a large Δo — Back-donation stabilizes t₂g.
  3. C. decrease — The t₂g rises on interacting with filled π orbitals.
  4. B. low spin — It is cheaper to pair than to go up to e_g*.
  5. A. 0 — t₂g⁶: all paired.
  6. D. 500 nm — λ = 10⁷/20,000 = 500 nm.
  7. C. 12 — One pair per ligand.
  8. B. a₁g, t₁u y e_g — The 3 σ SALCs combine with a₁g (s), t₁u (p) and e_g (d).
  9. B. π back-donation — π-acceptor.
  10. B. ligand — They are closer in energy to the ligand AOs.
  11. C. 15 — 15 MOs in total.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. I⁻ and F⁻ are π-donors (raise t₂g, lower Δo); H₂O and NH₃ are almost σ-only; CN⁻ and CO are π-acceptors (lower t₂g, raise Δo).
  2. λ = 10⁷/25,000 = 400 nm (violet); it appears yellow.
