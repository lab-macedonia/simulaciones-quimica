Molecular symmetry and point groups

Symmetry elements and operations (E, Cn, σ, i, Sn) applied in 3D to H₂O, NH₃, CH₄, BF₃, SF₆, CO₂, HCl, trans-dichloroethene, benzene and a chiral molecule; point-group assignment with a decision diagram and relation to polarity and chirality.

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Identify symmetry elements and operations.
  • Assign the point group using the decision flowchart.
  • Relate the group to polarity and chirality.

Prior knowledge

  • Molecular geometry (VSEPR).

Suggested activity

Assign groups

  1. Choose H₂O and apply each operation.
  2. Follow the decision flowchart.
  3. Repeat with NH₃, CH₄ and BF₃.
  4. Indicate which are polar.

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“Symmetry element and operation are the same thing.”The element is the axis/plane; the operation is the action.
“Every molecule with a C₂ axis is polar.”It depends on whether there are perpendicular C₂ axes and planes.
“If it is symmetric it cannot be chiral.”It can be if it lacks improper axes (C₂, D₃).

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. B. C₂v — One C₂ and two σv planes.
  2. A. rotation by 360°/n followed by reflection in the perpendicular plane — It is an improper rotation.
  3. D. Td — Td has zero dipole by symmetry.
  4. C. C₁ — No planes, center or improper axes.
  5. B. D∞h — Linear with an inversion center.
  6. A. (−x, −y, −z) — Reflection through the center.
  7. A. C₂v — One C₂ and two planes.
  8. A. Td — Tetrahedral.
  9. A. polar or chiral — It has an improper axis.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. C₃v: one C₃ and three vertical planes.
  2. D₃h; nonpolar.
