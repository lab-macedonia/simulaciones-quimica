Crystal field theory 3D

d splitting in octahedral, tetrahedral and square-planar fields, spectrochemical series, high/low spin, CFSE and complex color from Δ.

⏱ 55 minLevel: undergraduate, early years11 multiple-choice questions · 2 essay questions

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Build d-orbital splitting diagrams in octahedral, tetrahedral and square-planar geometries.
  • Predict high or low spin by comparing Δo and P.
  • Calculate the CFSE and relate Δo to color.

Prior knowledge

  • Electron configuration of transition metals.
  • Oxidation states.

Suggested activity

  1. With the simulation compare [Fe(H₂O)₆]²⁺ and [Fe(CN)₆]⁴⁻.
  2. Place the ligands in the spectrochemical series.
  3. Determine spin, unpaired electrons and CFSE.
  4. Relate to color and magnetism.

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“The eg orbitals point between the ligands.”In the octahedron the eg point toward the ligands and rise in energy.
“Δt is greater than Δo.”Δt ≈ 4/9 Δo; tetrahedral complexes are almost always high spin.
“The color is the one the complex absorbs.”The complement of the absorbed color is seen.

B. Essay

1. Determine the spin and magnetism of [CoF₆]³⁻ and [Co(NH₃)₆]³⁺.

2. A complex absorbs at 500 nm (green). What color is observed and what is Δo in cm⁻¹?

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. A. d_z² y d_x²−y² (e_g) — The e_g point directly toward the ligands and suffer more repulsion.
  2. D. CN⁻ — CN⁻ is strong-field (π acceptor); I⁻ is very weak-field.
  3. C. 0 unpaired electrons — Δo > P: t₂g⁶, low spin and diamagnetic.
  4. B. −1,2 Δo — Three electrons in t₂g: 3 × (−0.4) = −1.2 Δo.
  5. A. Δt = 4/9 Δo — With only 4 ligands and no orbitals pointing at them, the splitting is smaller.
  6. D. violet / blue — The observed color is the complement of the absorbed one: yellow → violet.
  7. C. it is square planar, low-spin d⁸ — In square planar, d_x²−y² is empty and the 8 electrons are paired.
  8. B. t₂g³ e_g² (high spin, 5 unpaired) — With Δo < P it is cheaper to occupy the e_g than to pair.
  9. C. −2,4 Δo + 2P — t₂g⁶: 6 × (−0.4Δo) = −2.4Δo, with 2 extra pairings.
  10. D. CO — Strong field (π-acceptor).
  11. B. square planar — Example: [Ni(CN)₄]²⁻.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. Co³⁺ d⁶. F⁻ weak field: high spin (4 unpaired e⁻, paramagnetic). NH₃ strong field: low spin (0 unpaired, diamagnetic).
  2. Complementary: red–magenta. Δo = 1/λ = 1/(500×10⁻⁷ cm) = 20,000 cm⁻¹.
