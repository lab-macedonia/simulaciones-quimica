Organometallics: the 18-electron rule

3D simulator of organometallic chemistry: the 18-electron rule, electron counting by the neutral method, metal carbonyls, ferrocene, 16-electron square planar complexes and the Wilkinson catalytic cycle (oxidative addition, coordination, migratory insertion and reductive elimination).

Open the simulation

⏱ 55 minLevel: undergraduate, early years11 multiple-choice questions · 2 essay questions

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Count valence electrons with the neutral method.
  • Apply the 18 e⁻ rule to carbonyls and ferrocene.
  • Describe elementary steps of a catalytic cycle.

Prior knowledge

  • d configuration of metals.
  • Complexes and ligands.

Suggested activity

Electron counting

  1. Count electrons in Cr(CO)₆, Fe(CO)₅, Ni(CO)₄ and ferrocene.
  2. Check it in the simulation.
  3. Identify the 16 e⁻ complex.
  4. Follow the Wilkinson cycle and mark the count at each intermediate.

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“CO contributes 4 electrons.”It contributes 2 (σ donor).
“Cp always contributes 6 e⁻.”In the neutral method η⁵-Cp contributes 5 (6 as Cp⁻).
“Every complex obeys 18 e⁻.”There are 16 e⁻ (square-planar d⁸) and 17 e⁻ complexes.

B. Essay

1. Count the electrons of [Mn(CO)₆]⁺.

2. Explain why square-planar d⁸ complexes (16 e⁻) are reactive in oxidative addition.

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. C. 18 — 6 (Cr) + 6 × 2 (CO) = 18.
  2. B. 8 + 2 × 5 — Fe (8) plus two Cp (5 e⁻ each, neutral method).
  3. A. 16 e⁻ — The d_{x²−y²} is left empty.
  4. D. I to III — H₂ contributes two hydride ligands and oxidizes the metal by 2 units.
  5. C. σ-donor and π-acceptor — M → π*(CO) back-donation.
  6. B. tetrahedral and 18 e⁻ — 10 + 8 = 18 and d¹⁰ → tetrahedral.
  7. A. lowers the oxidation state of the metal by 2 — A bond forms between two ligands that leave the metal.
  8. D. it has 17 e⁻ — 5 + 12 = 17: stable radical.
  9. C. 18 e⁻ — 8 + 5×2.
  10. C. +2 — X–Y is broken and two bonds, M–X and M–Y, are formed.
  11. B. Rh — RhCl(PPh₃)₃.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. Mn 7 + 6 × 2 = 19; minus 1 for the charge: 18 e⁻.
  2. They can reach 18 e⁻ by accepting two ligands, changing the oxidation state by +2.
