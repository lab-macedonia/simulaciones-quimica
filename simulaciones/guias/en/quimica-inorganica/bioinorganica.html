Bioinorganic chemistry: metals in life

Active site of the heme group with Fe²⁺, histidine, O₂ and CO; cooperativity (Hill), the Bohr effect and CO poisoning; Zn in carbonic anhydrase, Mg in chlorophyll, Cu in azurin and Fe in cytochrome c; toxicity of Pb²⁺, Hg and Cd and chelation.

⏱ 55 minLevel: undergraduate, early years9 multiple-choice questions · 2 essay questions

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Describe the heme group and the binding of O₂ and CO.
  • Interpret cooperativity (Hill n) and the Bohr effect.
  • Recognize functions and toxicity of metals in biological systems.

Prior knowledge

  • Coordination chemistry.
  • Binding equilibrium.

Suggested activity

Saturation curves

  1. Compare the hemoglobin curve with that of myoglobin.
  2. Lower the pH and observe P₅₀.
  3. Add CO and compare the affinity.
  4. Relate toxic metals to –SH groups.

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“Hemoglobin binds O₂ without cooperativity.”The sigmoidal curve indicates cooperativity (n ≈ 2.8).
“CO displaces O₂ because it is smaller.”It binds much more strongly to Fe²⁺ (~200×).
“A lower pH increases the affinity for O₂.”It decreases it (Bohr effect).

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. B. protruding from the plane of the porphyrin — High spin, larger radius.
  2. A. cooperativity between subunits — Each O₂ makes the next one easier.
  3. D. increases — Bohr effect: lower affinity.
  4. C. 210 times greater than O₂ — Strong π-acceptor ligand.
  5. B. Lewis acid — It polarizes the coordinated H₂O.
  6. A. –SH groups — Soft acid ↔ soft S base.
  7. A. Fe²⁺ — Fe³⁺ does not bind O₂.
  8. A. sigmoidal — Cooperativity.
  9. A. it binds to Fe²⁺ with higher affinity than O₂ — It blocks transport.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. Lower pH decreases the affinity for O₂ and favors its release in active tissues.
  2. They bind to –SH groups and replace essential metals.
