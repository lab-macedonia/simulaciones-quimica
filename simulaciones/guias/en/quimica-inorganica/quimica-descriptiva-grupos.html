Descriptive Chemistry of the Groups

Trends and reactivity of alkali metals, alkaline earth metals, halogens and noble gases, and the acid–base character of period 3 oxides in 3D: radius, ionization energy, reaction with water and states of matter.

Open the simulation

⏱ 45 minLevel: undergraduate, early years9 multiple-choice questions · 2 essay questions

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Explain periodic trends using radius, ionization energy and electronegativity.
  • Compare the reactivity of alkali metals, alkaline earth metals and halogens.
  • Relate the acid–base character of oxides to position in the period.

Prior knowledge

  • Electron configuration.
  • Basic periodic trends.

#StageCore idea
1Trends in the tableRadius, ionization energy and electronegativity change in a regular way
2Alkali metalsOne valence electron: strong reducing agents that react with water ever more violently
3Alkaline earth metalsTwo valence e⁻: M²⁺; reactivity increasing downward
4HalogensSeven valence e⁻: strong oxidants; oxidizing power decreases downward
5Noble gasesComplete valence shell: very unreactive
6Period 3 oxidesFrom basic (Na₂O) to acidic (SO₃, Cl₂O₇) across the period

Suggested activity

Reactivity of alkali metals with water

  1. Observe Li, Na and K with water in the simulation.
  2. Relate the rate to ionization energy.
  3. Write the equations.
  4. Repeat for Mg, Ca and Ba.

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“Noble gases never form compounds.”Xe and Kr form fluorides and oxides.
“The oxidizing power of the halogens increases going down the group.”It decreases: F₂ > Cl₂ > Br₂ > I₂.
“All metal oxides are acidic.”Those of metals are basic; those of nonmetals, acidic.

B. Essay

1. Explain why potassium is more reactive than lithium with water.

2. Write SO₃ + H₂O and Na₂O + H₂O and classify the products.

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. C. increases — Lower ionization energy.
  2. B. F₂ — E° = 2,87 V.
  3. A. amphoteric — It reacts with acids and bases.
  4. D. solid — Greater London forces.
  5. C. xenon — XeF₂, XeF₄, XeO₃.
  6. B. H₂SO₄ — Acidic oxide.
  7. A. 2NaOH + H₂ — It produces hydroxide and hydrogen.
  8. C. amphoteric — It reacts with acids and bases.
  9. B. Br₂ — Bromine.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. K has a lower ionization energy (more distant and more shielded electron); although Li has a more negative E°, kinetics favors K.
  2. SO₃ + H₂O → H₂SO₄ (acid); Na₂O + H₂O → 2NaOH (base).
