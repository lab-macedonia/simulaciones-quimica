Δ / Λ chirality in complexes

Origin of chirality in coordination complexes and distinction between the Δ and Λ configurations in 3D models.

Open the simulation

⏱ 40 minLevel: undergraduate, early years3 multiple-choice questions · 2 essay questions

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Recognize chiral complexes.
  • Assign Δ and Λ.
  • Relate chirality to optical activity.

Prior knowledge

  • R/S stereochemistry.
  • Bidentate ligands.

Suggested activity

Assign Δ/Λ

  1. Observe [Co(en)₃]³⁺ in the simulation.
  2. Look along the C₃ axis and follow the helix.
  3. Assign Δ or Λ.
  4. Predict the sign in the polarimeter (it is not deduced from Δ/Λ alone).

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“Δ is equivalent to dextrorotatory.”They are not directly related.
“Every octahedral complex is chiral.”Only with chelates or specific arrangements.
“Δ and Λ are diastereomers.”They are enantiomers.

Question bank

A. Multiple choice

1. Δ is the helix that is…

  1. left-handed
  2. right-handed (clockwise)
  3. planar
  4. square

2. Which is chiral?

  1. [Co(NH₃)₆]³⁺
  2. [Co(en)₃]³⁺
  3. [CoCl₆]³⁻
  4. [Co(CN)₆]³⁻

3. Δ and Λ are…

  1. enantiomers
  2. diastereomers
  3. identical
  4. tautomers

B. Essay

1. Explain why [Co(en)₃]³⁺ is chiral and [Co(NH₃)₆]³⁺ is not.

2. Describe the resolution of the enantiomers of a complex.

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. B. right-handed (clockwise) — Helix rule.
  2. B. [Co(en)₃]³⁺ — Three chelates form a helix.
  3. A. enantiomers — Mirror images.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. The three en chelates form a helix; the hexaammine has a plane of symmetry.
  2. A chiral counterion is used that forms diastereomeric salts that can be separated by crystallization.
