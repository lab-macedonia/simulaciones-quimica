Nomenclature and formulas of complexes

3D simulator to name and write formulas of coordination compounds: central atom, monodentate ligands and chelates, coordination number, geometry, charge and oxidation number, alphabetical order, prefixes, anionic complexes (-ate) and salts.

Open the simulation

⏱ 45 minLevel: undergraduate, early years11 multiple-choice questions · 2 essay questions

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Identify central atom, ligands, coordination number and oxidation number.
  • Name cationic, neutral and anionic complexes.
  • Write formulas from names.

Prior knowledge

  • Ionic charges.
  • Names of common ligands.

Simulation walkthrough

Use the lesson (bottom player) for the guided walkthrough and the Lab tab to explore freely; “Back to the lesson” resumes the script.

#StageCore idea
1Central atom and ligandsA complex = central metal + ligands bound by coordinate bonds
2Types of ligandsMonodentate (1 donor atom) and polydentate or chelates (2 or more)
3Charge and oxidation numberCharge of the complex = oxidation no. of the metal + sum of the ligand charges
4How to name a complexLigands in alphabetical order + metal with oxidation no. in Roman numerals
5Anionic complexes and saltsIf the complex is an anion, the metal ends in -ate
6Formula and practiceFormula: metal first, then anionic and neutral ligands in alphabetical order

Suggested activity

From name to formula and vice versa

  1. Each student receives three complexes and names them.
  2. They swap with a classmate who writes the formula.
  3. They check in the simulation.
  4. They correct errors in alphabetical order and prefixes.

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“Ligands are ordered by number of atoms.”They are ordered alphabetically without considering numerical prefixes.
“A complex anion keeps the name of the metal.”The ending -ate is used (ferrate, cuprate).
“The oxidation number is the coordination number.”They are different concepts.

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. C. hexaamminecobalt(III) chloride — The anion (chloride) is named last, after the cationic complex.
  2. B. potassium hexacyanoferrate(II) — Anionic complex: -ate; Fe²⁺ because 4− = x + 6(−1).
  3. A. 6 — 3 bidentate ligands = 6 donors.
  4. D. tris(ethylenediamine) — Complex ligands use bis-, tris- with parentheses.
  5. C. alphabetical — Ignoring the numerical prefixes.
  6. B. cyano — As a ligand: cyano.
  7. A. 0 — Pt²⁺ + 2(−1) = 0.
  8. D. tetracarbonylnickel(0) — Neutral complex: no -ate ending.
  9. A. tetraamminecopper(II) sulfate — Complex cation, sulfate anion.
  10. B. tris — For ligands with a compound name.
  11. B. aqua — Aqua.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. Potassium hexacyanoferrate(III) and potassium hexacyanoferrate(II).
  2. [PtCl₂(NH₃)₂] (cisplatin if cis).
