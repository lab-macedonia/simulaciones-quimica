Hard and soft acids and bases (HSAB)

Classification of cations and bases as hard, borderline or soft according to charge, radius and polarizability; qualitative pair-stability score, prediction of the direction of exchange reactions and mineral examples (Al₂O₃, HgS, Cu₂S).

Open the simulation

⏱ 50 minLevel: undergraduate, early years9 multiple-choice questions · 2 essay questions

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Classify acids and bases as hard, borderline or soft.
  • Predict the stability of acid–base pairs.
  • Explain the distribution of metals in minerals.

Prior knowledge

  • Ionic radius and polarizability.
  • Lewis acids and bases.

1Hard and softHard: small, highly charged, weakly polarizable. Soft: large, weakly charged, highly polarizable
2ClassificationThree categories: hard, borderline and soft
3PairingHard with hard, soft with soft
4Exchange reactionsAB + CD ⇌ AD + CB: favors hard–hard and soft–soft pairs
5MineralsAl₂O₃ in oxides; HgS and Cu₂S in sulfides

Suggested activity

Predict exchanges

  1. Choose a cation and a base and record the score.
  2. Predict the direction of AB + CD ⇌ AD + CB.
  3. Check it in the simulation.
  4. Explain why Hg appears as HgS.

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“Hard means strong.”It refers to polarizability, not strength.
“A hard acid prefers a soft base.”Hard with hard, soft with soft.
“The classification is exact.”It is a qualitative criterion.

B. Essay

1. Predict whether Al³⁺ prefers O²⁻ or S²⁻.

2. Explain why CN⁻ forms stable complexes with Cu⁺.

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. D. hard — Hard with hard and soft with soft.
  2. C. Hg²⁺ — Large, polarizable and of moderate charge.
  3. B. I⁻ — Larger size, more deformable cloud.
  4. A. sulfide (HgS) — Soft Hg²⁺ ↔ soft S²⁻.
  5. D. ionic — Hard–hard: high charge/radius.
  6. C. the reactants — The original pairs are hard–hard and soft–soft.
  7. A. soft — Large and polarizable.
  8. A. hard — Small and weakly polarizable.
  9. A. HgS — Soft with soft.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. O²⁻ (hard with hard).
  2. Both are soft.
