Mechanical energy, work and power

3D energy simulator: work done by a force, gravitational potential and kinetic energy on a track, conservation of mechanical energy, dissipation by friction and the power of a crane.

Open the simulation

⏱ 45 minLevel: Grade 108 multiple-choice questions · 2 essay questions

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Calculate work, potential energy and kinetic energy.
  • Apply conservation of mechanical energy and recognise dissipation by friction.
  • Relate work, time and power.

Prior knowledge

  • Force and weight (W = m·g).
  • Powers and square roots.

Simulation walkthrough

Use the lesson (bottom player) for the guided walkthrough and the Lab tab to explore freely; “Back to the lesson” resumes the script.

#StageCore idea
1WorkW = F·d: a force that moves its point of application transfers energy
2Gravitational potential energyEp = m·g·h: it depends on mass and height
3Kinetic energyEk = ½·m·v²: it depends on the square of the speed
4Conservation of mechanical energyWithout friction, Em = Ep + Ek stays constant
5Friction and dissipationWith friction, part of the mechanical energy turns into heat
6PowerP = W/t: how fast work is done, in watts

Suggested activity

The unpowered roller coaster

  1. On the frictionless track, release the ball from 2, 4 and 6 m and record the speed at the bottom.
  2. Compare with v = √(2gh).
  3. Repeat with masses of 1 kg and 8 kg: does the speed change?
  4. Add friction 0.1 and estimate how much energy is dissipated on the first run.

Common misconceptions

MisconceptionCorrection
“A heavier body falls faster.”Without air resistance, the speed after falling h does not depend on mass.
“With friction energy is lost.”It turns into thermal energy; the total is conserved.
“More power means more work.”Power is work per unit time; the same work can be done with different power.

B. Essay

1. A 0.5 kg ball is thrown upwards at 12 m/s. What maximum height does it reach without friction?

2. An 800 kg lift rises 30 m in 20 s. Calculate the work and the minimum power of the motor.

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. C. 98 J — W = m·g·h = 5·9.8·2 = 98 J.
  2. B. quadruples — Ek = ½mv² depends on v².
  3. D. does not depend on its mass — v = √(2gh): the mass cancels out.
  4. D. Ek is maximum and Ep minimum — All the Ep has become Ek.
  5. C. part of the energy turns into heat — Total energy is conserved, but part of it is no longer mechanical.
  6. C. 200 W — P = W/t = 600/3.
  7. A. has twice the power and does the same work — Work depends on F and d; power depends on time.
  8. A. joule — 1 J = 1 N·m; the watt is J/s.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. h = v²/(2g) = 144/19.6 = 7.3 m.
  2. W = 800·9.8·30 = 235,200 J; P = 11,760 W ≈ 11.8 kW.
