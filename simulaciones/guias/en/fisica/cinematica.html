Kinematics: uniform and accelerated motion

3D kinematics simulator: position, displacement, uniform and uniformly accelerated linear motion, x–t and v–t graphs, braking distance and two objects meeting.

Open the simulation

⏱ 45 minLevel: Grade 108 multiple-choice questions · 2 essay questions

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Distinguish position, displacement and distance travelled.
  • Describe uniform and accelerated motion with equations and x–t and v–t graphs.
  • Calculate braking distance and the meeting time of two objects.

Prior knowledge

  • Solving first-degree equations.
  • Reading Cartesian graphs.

Suggested activity

How far does a car need to stop?

  1. In «Lab» set v₀ = 10 m/s and a = −4 m/s²; record where the car stops.
  2. Repeat with v₀ = 20 m/s and v₀ = 30 m/s.
  3. Build a table of v₀ against braking distance and plot it.
  4. Explain why the distance does not grow in proportion to speed and link it to urban speed limits.

Common misconceptions

MisconceptionCorrection
“If the velocity is zero, so is the acceleration.”At the top of a throw or when reversing, v = 0 but a ≠ 0.
“A negative velocity means the object is slowing down.”The sign gives the direction; it slows down when v and a have opposite signs.
“The x–t graph shows the path.”It shows how position changes with time; the path here is a straight line.

B. Essay

1. A car at 90 km/h brakes with a = −5 m/s². Calculate the braking time and distance.

2. Two cyclists start from the same point: A at 6 m/s and B, 4 s later, at 9 m/s in the same direction. When and where does B catch up?

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. C. a sloping straight line — x = x₀ + v·t is linear in t; its slope is the velocity.
  2. B. 20 m/s — 72 / 3.6 = 20 m/s.
  3. D. 10 m/s — v = 0 + 2·5 = 10 m/s.
  4. D. 25 m — x = ½·2·5² = 25 m.
  5. C. the acceleration — a = Δv/Δt.
  6. C. becomes four times longer — d = v₀²/(2|a|) depends on the square of v₀.
  7. A. accelerated — Each second it covers more distance: the velocity increases.
  8. A. 5 s — They close in at 22 m/s: 110/22 = 5 s.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. v₀ = 25 m/s; t = 25/5 = 5 s; d = 25²/(2·5) = 62.5 m.
  2. 6t = 9(t − 4) → t = 12 s after A starts; x = 72 m.
