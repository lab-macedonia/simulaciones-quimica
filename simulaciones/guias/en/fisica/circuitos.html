Series and parallel electric circuits

3D circuit simulator: open and closed circuits, Ohm’s law, series and parallel connection of bulbs, equivalent resistance, a burnt-out bulb and electric power.

Open the simulation

⏱ 45 minLevel: Grade 88 multiple-choice questions · 2 essay questions

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Identify the parts of a circuit and the condition for a closed circuit.
  • Apply Ohm’s law.
  • Compare series and parallel circuits in current, voltage, brightness and behaviour when a part fails.

Prior knowledge

  • Electric charge and conductors.
  • Working with fractions.

Simulation walkthrough

Use the lesson (bottom player) for the guided walkthrough and the Lab tab to explore freely; “Back to the lesson” resumes the script.

#StageCore idea
1Simple circuitCurrent flows only if the circuit is closed
2Ohm’s lawI = V/R: more voltage, more current; more resistance, less current
3Series circuitA single path: the same current flows through every bulb
4Parallel circuitSeveral paths: each bulb gets the full voltage
5If a bulb burns outIn series they all go out; in parallel the others stay on
6Electric powerP = V·I: the energy a load uses every second

Suggested activity

How would you wire the lights in your home?

  1. Set up 3 bulbs in series and record the brightness, current and voltage of each one.
  2. Repeat in parallel.
  3. Burn out bulb 1 in each case.
  4. Write three reasons for using parallel wiring in a home.

Common misconceptions

MisconceptionCorrection
“Current is used up in the bulb.”The current going in equals the current coming out; energy is transformed, not charge.
“In parallel each bulb gets less voltage.”Each branch gets the full cell voltage.
“More bulbs in parallel increase the total resistance.”They decrease it: there are more paths for the current.

B. Essay

1. Two 6 Ω bulbs are connected to 12 V, first in series and then in parallel. Calculate the total current and the power of each bulb in both cases.

2. Explain why a short circuit can cause a fire.

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. C. closed — Electrons need a complete path.
  2. B. 0.5 A — I = V/R = 6/12.
  3. D. is the same in every load — There is only one path.
  4. D. the full cell voltage — Each branch is connected directly to the cell.
  5. C. 30 Ω — In series they add up.
  6. C. 5 Ω — 1/R = 1/10 + 1/10 → R = 5 Ω.
  7. A. parallel — In parallel each branch is independent.
  8. A. ≈ 9.1 A — I = P/V = 2000/220.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. Series: R = 12 Ω, I = 1 A, P = 6 W each. Parallel: R = 3 Ω, I = 4 A, P = 24 W each.
  2. With no resistance the current is very large and the wires heat up (P = I²R); that is why fuses and circuit breakers exist.
