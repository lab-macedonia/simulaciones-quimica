The expanding universe

3D simulator of the expanding universe: galaxy redshift, Hubble’s law, expansion without a centre, the age of the universe, its history since the Big Bang and astronomy at Chile’s observatories.

Open the simulation

⏱ 45 minLevel: Grades 9 and 11–128 multiple-choice questions · 2 essay questions

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Interpret redshift as evidence that galaxies are moving away.
  • Apply Hubble’s law and estimate the age of the universe.
  • Describe the history of the universe and the role of Chile’s observatories.

Prior knowledge

  • The electromagnetic spectrum and wavelength.
  • Direct proportionality.

Suggested activity

Measure the Hubble constant yourself

  1. Select five galaxies at different distances and record d and v.
  2. Plot v against d and draw the line of best fit.
  3. Find H₀ as the slope and estimate the age 1/H₀.
  4. Change the observer and repeat: do you get a different value? Explain.

Common misconceptions

MisconceptionCorrection
“The Big Bang was an explosion at a point in space.”It was the expansion of space itself, everywhere at once.
“The Milky Way is the centre of the universe.”Any observer sees the other galaxies moving away.
“Galaxies move away by racing through space.”It is the space between them that stretches.

B. Essay

1. A galaxy recedes at 14,000 km/s. With H₀ = 70 km/s/Mpc, how far away is it and how long ago did its light leave?

2. Explain why northern Chile is a privileged place for astronomy.

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. C. are moving away from us — The observed wavelength increases.
  2. B. twice as fast — v = H₀·d is proportional.
  3. D. 7000 km/s — 70 · 100 = 7000.
  4. D. No: every galaxy sees the same thing — Space itself expands.
  5. C. 13.8 billion years — 4.6 billion years is the age of the Solar System.
  6. C. the first atoms formed — ≈ 380,000 years after the Big Bang.
  7. A. the north has dry, high, dark skies — The Atacama Desert is ideal for observing.
  8. A. as it was 1 billion years ago — The light took that long to arrive.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. d = 200 Mpc ≈ 652 million light-years: its light left ≈ 652 million years ago.
  2. Very dry, stable air, high altitude and dark skies, with many clear nights a year.
