Waves and sound

3D sound wave simulator: air particles forming compressions and rarefactions, amplitude and intensity, frequency and pitch, speed in different media, echoes and how the ear works, with an audible tone.

Open the simulation

⏱ 45 minLevel: Grade 98 multiple-choice questions · 2 essay questions

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Describe sound as a longitudinal mechanical wave.
  • Relate amplitude to intensity and frequency to pitch, and apply v = λ·f.
  • Explain echoes and the basic workings of the ear.

Prior knowledge

  • The idea of vibration and energy.
  • Rearranging simple formulas.

Simulation walkthrough

Use the lesson (bottom player) for the guided walkthrough and the Lab tab to explore freely; “Back to the lesson” resumes the script.

#StageCore idea
1Sound is a mechanical waveThe particles of the medium oscillate and pass on compressions and rarefactions
2Amplitude and intensityGreater amplitude, louder sound (more decibels)
3Frequency and pitchHigher frequency, higher pitch
4Speed and wavelengthv = λ·f: speed depends on the medium, not on the frequency
5Reflection: the echoSound bounces off surfaces; d = v·t/2
6The ear and hearingThe eardrum vibrates with the wave; the cochlea tells frequencies apart

Suggested activity

How long is the wave of your favourite note?

  1. Choose three notes (for example 262, 440 and 880 Hz) and listen to them.
  2. Calculate λ in air and compare it with the simulation’s value.
  3. Switch the medium to water and steel: what changes and what does not?
  4. Use the echo set-up to estimate the distance to a wall that returns the sound in 0.5 s.

Common misconceptions

MisconceptionCorrection
“In sound, air travels from the source to the ear.”The particles only oscillate; energy is what travels.
“A louder sound is higher-pitched.”Loudness depends on amplitude; pitch on frequency.
“Sound travels faster in air than in water.”It is the other way round: faster in liquids and solids.

B. Essay

1. Explain why an explosion could not be heard in space.

2. A dolphin sends a 100 kHz pulse and receives the echo 0.02 s later. How far away is the fish? (v = 1480 m/s)

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. C. it needs particles to vibrate — It is a mechanical wave.
  2. B. oscillate along the direction of travel — It is a longitudinal wave.
  3. D. a higher frequency — Pitch depends on frequency.
  4. D. 1 m — λ = v/f.
  5. C. steel — In solids the particles are more strongly bound.
  6. C. 340 m away — d = v·t/2 = 340·2/2.
  7. A. the amplitude — More amplitude, more energy.
  8. A. ultrasound and echoes — Echolocation with frequencies up to ≈ 110 kHz.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. There is no material medium whose particles can vibrate to carry the wave.
  2. d = 1480·0.02/2 = 14.8 m.
