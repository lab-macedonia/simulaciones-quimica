Momentum and collisions

3D momentum simulator: impulse and average force (airbag), elastic, inelastic and partially elastic collisions, different masses and recoil on an air track.

Open the simulation

⏱ 45 minLevel: Grade 108 multiple-choice questions · 2 essay questions

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Calculate momentum and impulse.
  • Apply conservation of momentum to collisions and explosions.
  • Distinguish elastic and inelastic collisions through kinetic energy.

Prior knowledge

  • Velocity as a vector (sign).
  • Newton’s second law.

Simulation walkthrough

Use the lesson (bottom player) for the guided walkthrough and the Lab tab to explore freely; “Back to the lesson” resumes the script.

#StageCore idea
1Momentump = m·v: it depends on mass and velocity
2ImpulseJ = F·Δt = Δp: the same change of momentum with less force if it lasts longer
3Elastic collisionBoth momentum and kinetic energy are conserved
4Perfectly inelastic collisionThe bodies stick together; momentum is conserved, kinetic energy is not
5Different massesA light body bounces off a heavy one
6Recoil and explosionsIf the system starts at rest, the total momentum stays zero

Suggested activity

Design a safety system

  1. In the «Impulse» set-up, compare the force when the cart stops with Δt = 0.05 s and 0.5 s.
  2. Explain why the product F·Δt does not change.
  3. Link it to airbags, helmets and crumple zones.
  4. In «Collision», measure the Ek lost with e = 0 and e = 0.5 for equal masses.

Common misconceptions

MisconceptionCorrection
“Kinetic energy is conserved in a collision.”Momentum is always conserved; Ek only in elastic collisions.
“The heavier body exerts more force in a collision.”The forces are equal (3rd law); what differs is the acceleration.
“Momentum is a number without direction.”It is a vector: opposite velocities have opposite signs.

Question bank

  1. 7 kg·m/s
  2. 0.75 kg·m/s
  3. 12 kg·m/s

  2. they stick together
  3. both stop
  4. they swap velocities

  2. 6 m/s
  3. 3 m/s
  4. 1.5 m/s

  3. the total momentum
  4. the shape of the bodies

  2. recoils at the same speed
  3. recoils, more slowly than the ball
  4. moves forwards

7. A 50 N force acts for 0.2 s. The impulse is…

  1. 10 N·s
  2. 250 N·s
  3. 0.004 N·s
  4. 50.2 N·s

8. In a perfectly inelastic collision, kinetic energy…

  1. decreases
  2. doubles
  3. increases
  4. is conserved

B. Essay

1. A 0.4 kg ball hits a wall at 10 m/s and bounces back at 8 m/s. Calculate the impulse it receives.

2. A 60 kg skater at rest throws a 5 kg backpack at 6 m/s. With what velocity does the skater recoil?

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. C. 12 kg·m/s — p = m·v = 3·4.
  2. B. it lengthens the stopping time and reduces the force — Δp is the same; with a longer Δt the force is smaller.
  3. D. they swap velocities — p and Ek are conserved: the first one stops.
  4. D. 1.5 m/s — 2·3 = 4·v → v = 1.5 m/s.
  5. C. the total momentum — The internal forces are equal and opposite (3rd law).
  6. C. recoils, more slowly than the ball — M·V = −m·v; since M ≫ m, V is small.
  7. A. 10 N·s — J = F·Δt.
  8. A. decreases — Part of it becomes deformation and heat.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. Δp = 0.4·(−8 − 10) = −7.2 kg·m/s (opposite to the initial direction).
  2. V = −5·6/60 = −0.5 m/s.
