Electrostatics: charging and electric force

3D electrostatics simulator: electric charge, charging by friction with the triboelectric series, by contact and by induction on an electroscope, grounding and Coulomb’s law.

Open the simulation

⏱ 45 minLevel: Grade 88 multiple-choice questions · 2 essay questions

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Explain charging as a transfer of electrons.
  • Compare charging by friction, contact and induction.
  • Describe qualitatively and calculate the force between charges.

Prior knowledge

  • Atomic model: protons, neutrons and electrons.
  • The idea of force.

Simulation walkthrough

Use the lesson (bottom player) for the guided walkthrough and the Lab tab to explore freely; “Back to the lesson” resumes the script.

#StageCore idea
1Electric chargeNeutral bodies have equal numbers of protons and electrons
2Charging by frictionWhen two materials are rubbed, electrons pass from one to the other
3Charging by contactA charged body shares its charge when it touches a conductor
4Charging by inductionWithout touching, a nearby charge separates the charges in a conductor
5GroundingWith the rod nearby, the ground gives or takes electrons: an opposite charge remains
6Electric force: Coulomb’s lawF = k·q₁·q₂ / r²: like charges repel, opposite charges attract

Suggested activity

Positive or negative?

  1. Use the triboelectric series to predict the sign of each material when rubbing glass with silk, a balloon with hair and PVC with wool.
  2. Check your predictions in the «Friction» set-up.
  3. Charge the electroscope by contact and by induction with grounding, and compare the final sign.
  4. Explain why a rubbed balloon sticks to the wall (induction in an insulator).

Common misconceptions

MisconceptionCorrection
“Rubbing creates charges.”Charges are transferred; total charge is conserved.
“Protons move when objects are charged.”Only electrons move.
“A neutral body has no charges.”It has equal amounts of positive and negative charge.
“With induction the electroscope stays charged even without grounding.”Without grounding the charges only separate; when the rod moves away it returns to the initial state.

B. Essay

1. Explain, step by step, how to charge an electroscope positively using only a negative rod.

2. Two charges of 2 μC and −5 μC are 30 cm apart. Calculate the force and say whether it is attractive or repulsive.

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. C. gains electrons — Only electrons are transferred; an excess of e⁻ is negative charge.
  2. B. positive, with the same amount of charge — Charge is conserved: the electrons gained by the balloon are lost by the wool.
  3. D. negative — Contact shares charge of the same sign.
  4. D. the charges in the metal separate — Induction: the electroscope stays neutral, but its charge is redistributed.
  5. C. positive — The repelled electrons escape to ground.
  6. C. attract — Opposite signs attract.
  7. A. drops to a quarter — F ∝ 1/r².
  8. A. because electrons move freely in a conductor — In a conductor charge spreads over the whole body.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. Bring the rod close without touching, ground it (electrons leave), disconnect the ground and then remove the rod: it stays positive.
  2. F = 9·10⁹·2·10⁻⁶·5·10⁻⁶/0.09 = 1 N, attractive.
