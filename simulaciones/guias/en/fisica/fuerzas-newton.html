Forces and Newton’s laws

3D simulator of forces: weight and gravity on different bodies, elastic force and Hooke’s law, static and kinetic friction, and Newton’s three laws with a free-body diagram.

Open the simulation

⏱ 50 minLevel: Grades 7 and 108 multiple-choice questions · 2 essay questions

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Represent the forces on a body with a free-body diagram.
  • Distinguish mass and weight, and apply W = m·g and F = k·x.
  • Explain static and kinetic friction and apply Newton’s three laws.

Prior knowledge

  • The idea of force as a push or a pull.
  • Decimal operations and direct proportionality.

Simulation walkthrough

Use the lesson (bottom player) for the guided walkthrough and the Lab tab to explore freely; “Back to the lesson” resumes the script.

#StageCore idea
1Forces in balanceWith no net force, a body at rest stays at rest (1st law)
2Weight and gravityW = m·g: mass does not change, weight depends on the body you stand on
3Elastic force: Hooke’s lawF = k·x: the stretch is proportional to the force
4FrictionStatic friction up to μₛ·N; when sliding, kinetic friction μ_k·N
5Second law: F = m·aAcceleration is proportional to the net force and inversely proportional to the mass
6Third law: action and reactionForces come in equal and opposite pairs, acting on different bodies

Suggested activity

Calibrating a spring balance

  1. In «Lab» choose «Spring» and set k = 100 N/m.
  2. Hang masses of 1, 2, 4 and 8 kg and record the stretch.
  3. Plot force (weight) against stretch and find the slope.
  4. Repeat on the Moon: does the slope change or only the stretch? Explain.

Common misconceptions

MisconceptionCorrection
“Mass and weight are the same thing.”Mass (kg) does not change; weight (N) depends on g.
“If a body moves, a force must act in the direction of motion.”With no net force a body keeps moving at constant velocity (1st law).
“Action and reaction cancel out.”They act on different bodies, so they do not cancel.
“Friction is always μ·N.”Static friction is whatever is needed to prevent sliding, up to μₛ·N.

  4. 24 m/s²

  4. does not change

  1. equal to that of the car on the truck
  2. greater
  3. zero
  4. smaller

8. Why is it harder to start sliding than to keep sliding?

  1. μₛ is greater than μ_k
  2. the normal force disappears
  3. the weight changes

B. Essay

1. A 10 kg box on a floor with μₛ = 0.4 and μ_k = 0.3 is pushed with 30 N and then 50 N. Describe what happens in each case and find the acceleration where relevant.

2. Using the third law, explain why a boat moves back when a person jumps from it onto the pier.

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. C. zero — Weight and normal force balance: it does not accelerate.
  2. B. is still 70 kg — Mass does not depend on location; weight does (W = m·g).
  3. D. 10 cm — x = F/k = 20/200 = 0.1 m.
  4. D. equal to your force — While it does not slide, static friction equals the applied force.
  5. C. 5 m/s² — a = F/m = 20/4.
  6. C. halves — a = F/m.
  7. A. equal to that of the car on the truck — Third law: the pair of forces has equal magnitude.
  8. A. μₛ is greater than μ_k — Maximum static friction exceeds kinetic friction.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. Maximum static friction 39.2 N: with 30 N it does not move (friction = 30 N). With 50 N it slides: a = (50 − 29.4)/10 = 2.06 m/s².
  2. The person pushes the boat backwards and the boat pushes the person forwards with an equal force; both accelerate in opposite directions, the lighter one more.
