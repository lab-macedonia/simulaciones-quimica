Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Relate c = λf and E = hf.
  • Locate spectrum regions and their effects on matter.
  • Distinguish linear and circular polarization.

Prior knowledge

  • Waves: λ, f, amplitude.
  • Scientific notation.

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“Longer λ, more energy.”E is inversely proportional to λ.
“EM waves need a medium.”They propagate in vacuum.
“Infrared breaks bonds.”It excites vibrations; breaking bonds requires UV or beyond.

B. Essay

1. Calculate the energy in kJ/mol of 500 nm light.

2. Explain the difference between linearly and circularly polarized light.

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. B. c = λ·f — The speed of light is the product of λ and f.
  2. A. increases — E = hc/λ: inversely proportional to λ.
  3. D. 2.25 eV — E = 1239.84/550 ≈ 2.25 eV.
  4. C. bond vibrations — IR spectroscopy detects vibrations (≈ 4000–400 cm⁻¹).
  5. B. are in phase and perpendicular to each other and to the propagation — They are transverse, in phase, and |E| = c·|B|.
  6. A. the tip of E describing a helix — Two orthogonal components with a 90° phase shift and the same amplitude.
  7. D. ≳ 10 eV — It must exceed the ionization energy (H: 13.6 eV).
  8. C. 1/λ (cm⁻¹) — It is the inverse of the wavelength, usual in IR.
  9. D. γ rays — Smaller λ, greater E.
  10. A. microwaves — Low energies.
  11. A. 5 × 10¹⁴ Hz — c/λ = 3×10⁸/6×10⁻⁷.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. E = hc/λ = 3.97×10⁻¹⁹ J; × N_A = 239 kJ/mol.
  2. In linear light E oscillates in one plane; in circular light its vector rotates with constant amplitude, clockwise or counterclockwise.
