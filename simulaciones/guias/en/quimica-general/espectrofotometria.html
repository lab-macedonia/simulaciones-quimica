Spectrophotometry and the Beer–Lambert law

3D UV-Vis spectrophotometer: transmittance and absorbance, Beer–Lambert law, spectrum and λmax, calibration curve with linear regression and determination of an unknown sample.

Open the simulation

⏱ 50 minLevel: undergraduate, early years9 multiple-choice questions · 2 essay questions

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Convert T and A.
  • Apply Beer–Lambert and build a calibration curve.
  • Determine the concentration of a sample.

Prior knowledge

  • Light and color.
  • Linear regression.

Simulation walkthrough

Use the lesson (bottom player) for the guided walkthrough and the Lab tab to explore freely; “Back to the lesson” resumes the script.

#StageCore idea
1Light, transmittance and absorbanceT = I/I₀ ; A = −log₁₀ T
2Beer–Lambert lawA = ε·b·c : linear with concentration and path length
3Absorption spectrum and λmaxMeasure at the wavelength of maximum absorption: greater sensitivity
4Calibration curveStandards of known concentration are measured and A = m·c + n is fitted
5Unknown samplec = (A − n)/m, always within the calibration range

Suggested activity

Quantify a sample

  1. Prepare standards in the simulation and measure A.
  2. Fit the line and check R².
  3. Measure the sample and calculate c.
  4. Check that it is within the range.

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“A is directly proportional to T.”A = −log T.
“Beer–Lambert always holds.”It stops being linear at high concentrations.
“A blank is not needed.”It is needed to subtract solvent and cuvette.

B. Essay

1. A = 0.500, ε = 2500 M⁻¹cm⁻¹, b = 1.00 cm. Calculate c.

2. Explain why A > 1.5 is avoided.

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. D. 10 % — T = 10⁻¹ = 0,1.
  2. C. it doubles — A is proportional to c.
  3. B. there is greater sensitivity — maximum ε.
  4. A. violet/magenta — Complementary color.
  5. D. set A = 0 with the solvent — It removes the contribution of the cuvette and solvent.
  6. C. within the range of the standards — It is not extrapolated.
  7. B. 1 — −log 0.1.
  8. A. λmax — Greater sensitivity.
  9. B. orange–red — Complementary color.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. c = A/(εb) = 2,0 × 10⁻⁴ M.
  2. Too little light reaches the detector: noise and deviations from Beer–Lambert.
