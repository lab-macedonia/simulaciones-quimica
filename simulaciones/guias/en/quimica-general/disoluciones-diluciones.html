Solutions and dilutions

Molar, percent and molal concentration with a 3D volumetric flask, preparing solutions from a solid, dilution with C₁V₁ = C₂V₂ and mixing of solutions, with visible solute particles.

Open the simulation

⏱ 50 minLevel: undergraduate, early years9 multiple-choice questions · 2 essay questions

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Calculate the molarity and the mass of solute for a given solution.
  • Apply C₁V₁ = C₂V₂ in dilutions.
  • Calculate the concentration of a mixture of solutions.

Prior knowledge

  • Mole and molar mass.
  • Concept of solution, solute and solvent.

Suggested activity

Prepare and dilute

  1. Prepare 250 mL of 0.40 M CuSO₄ and calculate the mass needed.
  2. Check with the simulation.
  3. Dilute 50 mL of that solution to 200 mL and predict the concentration.
  4. Explain why the moles of solute do not change.

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“Molarity is calculated with the volume of water added.”The final volume of solution is used.
“Diluting decreases the moles of solute.”Only the volume increases; the moles stay the same.
“When mixing, the concentrations are averaged.”It is a volume-weighted average: the moles are added.

B. Essay

1. Calculate the molarity of 5.85 g of NaCl in 500 mL.

2. You mix 200 mL of 0.10 M with 300 mL of 0.40 M of the same solute. What concentration results?

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. A. 5,84 g — 0,20 × 0,500 × 58,44.
  2. D. 0,50 M — 2,0 × 25 / 100.
  3. C. do not change — Only solvent is added.
  4. B. 0,60 M — (0,10 + 0,020)/0,200.
  5. A. the solution — Liters of solution.
  6. D. dilute 10 times — Dilution factor = C₁/C₂ = 10.
  7. A. 0,50 M — 3,0 × 10/60.
  8. A. 2,0 g — 0,5 × 0,100 × 40.
  9. A. mol/L — Moles per liter of solution.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. 0.100 mol / 0.500 L = 0.200 M.
  2. (0,020 + 0,120)/0,500 = 0,28 M.
