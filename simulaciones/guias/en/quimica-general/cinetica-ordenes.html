Kinetics: reaction orders 3D

Integrated rate laws of order 0, 1 and 2, half-life, linearization ([A], ln[A], 1/[A]) and a mystery experiment to determine the order from initial rates.

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Determine the order of reaction from data.
  • Apply the integrated laws and t½.
  • Choose the linear plot according to the order.

Prior knowledge

  • Concentration and rate.
  • Logarithms.

Suggested activity

Identify the order

  1. With the simulation obtain [A] versus t tables.
  2. Plot [A], ln[A] and 1/[A] versus t.
  3. Identify the straight line and calculate k.
  4. Calculate t½ and compare with theory.

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“The order is read from the stoichiometric coefficients.”It is determined experimentally.
“The first-order t½ depends on [A]₀.”It is constant: ln2/k.
“k has no units.”They depend on the order: M^(1−n)·s⁻¹.

B. Essay

1. A 1st-order reaction has k = 0.035 min⁻¹. How long does it take for 90 % to be consumed?

2. When [A] is doubled the initial rate is multiplied by 4; when tripled, by 9. Deduce the order with respect to A and write the rate law.

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. B. 30,0 s — t½ = ln 2 / k = 0.693 / 0.0231 = 30.0 s (independent of the initial concentration).
  2. A. 2 — 2ⁿ = 4 → n = 2.
  3. D. 40 s — [A] = [A]₀ − kt = 0 → t = 0.80 / 0.020 = 40 s (the half-life would be 20 s).
  4. C. 10 s — t½ = 1/(k[A]₀) = 1/(0,50 × 0,20) = 10 s.
  5. B. ln[A] versus t — ln[A] = ln[A]₀ − kt: a line of slope −k.
  6. A. M⁻¹·s⁻¹ — v (M/s) = k [A]² (M²) → k = M⁻¹·s⁻¹.
  7. D. 25 % — t½ = 10 s: 20 s is 2 half-lives → (1/2)² = 25 %. (Also e^(−0.0693·20) = 0.25.)
  8. C. 2 — 3ⁿ = 9 → n = 2.
  9. B. 1 — First order.
  10. B. M·s⁻¹ — v = k.
  11. C. 2 — Second order: t½ = 1/(k[A]₀).

Essay (marking guide)

  1. t = ln(10)/k = 2.303/0.035 = 65.8 min.
  2. v ∝ [A]²: order 2, v = k[A]². Check that 2² = 4 and 3² = 9.
