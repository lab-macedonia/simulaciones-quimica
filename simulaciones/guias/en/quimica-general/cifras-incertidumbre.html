Significant Figures and Uncertainty

Precision versus accuracy with a 3D target, random and systematic error, laboratory instruments with their uncertainty, significant figures in sums and products and propagation of uncertainties.

Open the simulation

⏱ 40 minLevel: undergraduate, early years9 multiple-choice questions · 2 essay questions

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Distinguish accuracy and precision.
  • Apply the rules for significant figures.
  • Propagate uncertainties in sums and products.

Prior knowledge

  • Scientific notation.
  • Laboratory instruments.

#StageCore idea
1Accuracy and precisionAccuracy: close to the true value. Precision: results close to each other
2Instruments and uncertaintyEach instrument has an uncertainty: the reading is written to that decimal place
3Significant figuresAll the certain digits plus the first uncertain one
4Operating with significant figuresSum: decimals. Product: figures
5Propagation of uncertaintySum: absolute uncertainties in quadrature. Product: relative ones in quadrature

Suggested activity

Measure with different instruments

  1. With the simulation measure the same volume with a graduated cylinder, a pipette and a burette.
  2. Record with the correct figures.
  3. Calculate the relative uncertainty.
  4. Discuss which instrument is most suitable.

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“More decimals = more accurate.”The decimals must respect the instrument.
“A trailing zero does not count.”In 2.50 the zero counts.
“Precision and accuracy are synonyms.”Precision is repeatability; accuracy is closeness to the true value.

B. Essay

1. Calculate 4.56 × 1.4 with significant figures.

2. Propagate: (10.0 ± 0.1) + (5.00 ± 0.02).

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. B. 3 — 4, 5 and the final zero with a decimal point.
  2. A. 1 decimal — 15.75 → 15.8 (3.4 rules).
  3. D. 2 significant figures — Smallest number of figures: 2 → 8.0.
  4. C. accuracy — It shifts all the measurements.
  5. B. the burette has the lower relative uncertainty — 0,02/20 ≪ 0,5/20.
  6. A. are not significant — They only fix the position of the decimal point.
  7. B. 3 figures — 4, 5, 0.
  8. B. 12,4 — It is limited to the smallest number of decimals.
  9. A. accuracy — It shifts all values.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. 6.384 → 6.4 (2 figures).
  2. Sum 15.0; uncertainty √(0.1² + 0.02²) = 0.10 → 15.0 ± 0.1.
