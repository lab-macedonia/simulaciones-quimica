Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“Hydrogen bonds are covalent bonds.”They are intermolecular interactions that are much weaker.
“London forces exist only in nonpolar molecules.”They exist in all molecules.
“Greater mass always means higher bp.”It depends on the forces; water boils higher than H₂S.

B. Essay

1. Explain why ice floats.

2. Compare pentane (36 °C) and neopentane (9.5 °C).

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. D. 1/r — U = q₁q₂/(4πε₀r): it is the longest-range interaction.
  2. C. the polarizability (size of the cloud) — It exists between all molecules and grows with α′ (which is why Xe boils at a higher T than Ne).
  3. B. repel each other — The energy depends on the orientation: head-to-tail attracts, head-to-head repels.
  4. A. a directional X–H···A interaction — Directional electrostatics (≈ 180° at X–H···A, H···A ≈ 1.8–2.0 Å).
  5. D. a halogen bond with a lone pair — The positive zone opposite C–I attracts lone pairs in a very directional way.
  6. C. thermal agitation separates the pair — k_BT ≈ 2.5 kJ/mol at 298 K: always compare the well with that energy.
  7. B. Keesom: ∝ 1/(T·r⁶) — The Boltzmann average over orientations gives an r⁻⁶ term that decreases with T.
  8. A. ion–ion > H bond > dipole–dipole > London (small atoms) — Na⁺···Cl⁻ ≈ −500; H₂O···H₂O ≈ −27; HCl···HCl ≈ −4; Ar···Ar ≈ −1 kJ/mol.
  9. B. hydrogen bond — It has O–H.
  10. D. I₂ — More electrons and a larger cloud.
  11. A. ion–dipole — Hydration.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. In ice the H bonds form an open network that is less dense than liquid water.
  2. Same masses; neopentane is more spherical, with less contact area and weaker London forces.
