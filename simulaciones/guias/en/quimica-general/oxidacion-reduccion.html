Oxidation and reduction: electron transfer

3D simulator that explains what it means to be oxidized and reduced: electrons moving from the reducing to the oxidizing agent, changing oxidation number, size of cations and anions, half-reactions and oxidizing and reducing agents in six reactions.

⏱ 50 minLevel: undergraduate, first years8 multiple-choice questions · 2 essay questions

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Define oxidation and reduction as loss and gain of electrons and relate them to the change in oxidation number.
  • Write the oxidation and reduction half-reactions of a simple redox reaction.
  • Identify the oxidizing and reducing agents and justify that the electrons lost equal the electrons gained.

Prior knowledge

  • Atomic structure, ions, cations and anions.
  • Rules for assigning oxidation numbers.

Suggested activity

Who gives and who receives the electrons?

  1. With Zn + Cu²⁺, move the progress slider and note when the oxidation number of each species changes.
  2. Write the two half-reactions and check that the electrons match.
  3. Repeat with 2 Al + 3 Cu²⁺ and explain why 2 Al and 3 Cu²⁺ are needed.
  4. With Mg + O₂ and with Cl₂ + Br⁻, identify the reducing and oxidizing agents and justify your choice using electrons, not oxygen.

Common misconceptions

MisconceptionCorrection
“To be oxidized is to combine with oxygen.”That is the historical definition. Today it is defined by electrons: Zn is oxidized in Zn + Cu²⁺ even though there is no oxygen.
“The oxidizing agent is the one that is oxidized.”It is the other way around: the oxidizing agent is reduced (gains e⁻) and causes the other species to be oxidized.
“Electrons are lost and disappear.”The e⁻ lost by the reducing agent are exactly those gained by the oxidizing agent: total charge is conserved.
“Reduction means the species gets smaller.”Being reduced means gaining electrons: the oxidation number goes down. Size depends on the case (Cu²⁺ grows when reduced to Cu).

B. Essay

1. For 2 Al + 3 Cu²⁺ → 2 Al³⁺ + 3 Cu write the two half-reactions, state how many electrons are transferred and which is the reducing agent.

2. Explain why the combustion of magnesium, 2 Mg + O₂ → 2 MgO, is a redox reaction even though the product is ionic and no “electrons” are visible.

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. A. Zn — Zn goes from 0 to +2: it loses 2 e⁻.
  2. C. gaining electrons — By gaining e⁻ the oxidation number decreases.
  3. D. is oxidized — It gives up e⁻ and thus reduces the other species.
  4. B. 0 to −1 — Each Cl gains 1 e⁻ and ends up as Cl⁻.
  5. A. 4 — Each Mg gives up 2 e⁻ and there are 2 Mg: 2 × 2 = 4 e⁻.
  6. A. Cl₂ — Cl₂ takes up e⁻ and oxidizes Br⁻.
  7. A. Zn + Cu²⁺ → Zn²⁺ + Cu — There is e⁻ transfer: the oxidation numbers of Zn and Cu change.
  8. D. 6 e⁻ — The e⁻ lost are exactly the e⁻ gained.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. Oxidation: 2 Al → 2 Al³⁺ + 6 e⁻. Reduction: 3 Cu²⁺ + 6 e⁻ → 3 Cu. 6 e⁻ are transferred; the reducing agent is Al.
  2. Mg goes from 0 to +2 (loses 2 e⁻ per atom) and O from 0 to −2 (gains 2 e⁻ per atom): 4 e⁻ are transferred in total, with Mg as the reducing agent and O₂ as the oxidizing agent.
