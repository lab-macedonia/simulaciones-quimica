Acid–base, pH and titration 3D

Burette and beaker with indicator: strong, weak and polyprotic (H₃PO₄) acids and bases, titration curve, buffer region (Henderson–Hasselbalch), species distribution, equivalence point and indicator choice.

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Calculate the pH of strong and weak acids/bases.
  • Interpret a titration curve and the buffer region.
  • Select the appropriate indicator.

Prior knowledge

  • Chemical equilibrium.
  • Logarithms.

Suggested activity

Titrate acetic acid with NaOH

  1. In the simulation prepare 25 mL of 0.100 M CH₃COOH.
  2. Titrate with 0.100 M NaOH.
  3. Identify initial pH, half-equivalence and equivalence.
  4. Choose an indicator and justify.

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“The equivalence pH is always 7.”Only for strong acid–strong base.
“A buffer does not change pH.”It changes little when acid or base is added.
“A large Ka means a weak acid.”It means a strong acid.

B. Essay

1. Calculate the pH of 0.100 M CH₃COOH (Ka = 1.8×10⁻⁵).

2. Calculate the pH of a mixture of 0.10 M CH₃COOH / 0.20 M CH₃COO⁻.

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. A. pKa — [A⁻] = [HA].
  2. C. > 7 — The conjugate base hydrolyzes.
  3. B. near the equivalence pH — Within the jump of the curve.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. [H⁺] = √(Ka·C) = 1,34×10⁻³ M → pH = 2,87.
  2. pH = 4.74 + log 2 = 5.04.
