Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Apply PV = nRT and the laws of Boyle, Charles and Gay-Lussac.
  • Interpret the Maxwell–Boltzmann speed distribution.
  • Identify when a real gas departs from ideal behavior.

Prior knowledge

  • Mole and molar mass.
  • Temperature scales and pressure units.

Suggested activity

Three laws, three graphs

  1. In the simulation keep two variables constant and vary a third.
  2. Record (P, V), (P, T) and (V, T) pairs.
  3. Plot the data and obtain the form of each law.
  4. Calculate R from your data and compare it with 0.0821 atm·L/(mol·K).

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
Using °C in PV = nRT.The absolute temperature in kelvin is always used.
“The greater the mass, the greater the kinetic energy.”At the same T, the average kinetic energy is the same; heavy molecules are slower.
“Real gases behave as ideal at low T.”They deviate most at low T and high P.

B. Essay

1. A 2.0 L balloon at 25 °C is cooled to 0 °C at constant pressure. Calculate the new volume.

2. Explain the van der Waals correction for P and V.

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. C. 22,4 L — V = nRT/P = 1.00 × 0.08206 × 273.15 / 1.00 = 22.41 L (molar volume at STP).
  2. B. 4.0 atm — Boyle's law: P₁V₁ = P₂V₂ → P₂ = 1.0 × 2.0 / 0.50 = 4.0 atm.
  3. A. 4,5 L — Charles's law (T in kelvin): V₂ = V₁ · T₂/T₁ = 3.0 × 450/300 = 4.5 L.
  4. D. H₂, about 4 times faster — v_rms = √(3RT/M): v(H₂)/v(O₂) = √(32/2.016) ≈ 4.
  5. C. Equal — The mean translational kinetic energy is 3/2 kT and depends only on T, not on mass.
  6. B. High pressure and low temperature — At high P the molecules are close together (their volume matters) and at low T the intermolecular attractions are relevant compared with the kinetic energy.
  7. A. 0.406 mol — n = PV/RT = (2.00 × 5.00) / (0.08206 × 300) = 10.0 / 24.6 = 0.406 mol.
  8. D. 2.0 atm — Dalton's law: p(N₂) = x(N₂) · P = (2/3) × 3.0 = 2.0 atm.
  9. B. it doubles — Boyle: P·V = constant.
  10. B. He — Lower molar mass, higher speed.
  11. B. 22,4 L — RT/P a 273,15 K.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. V₂ = V₁T₂/T₁ = 2,0 × 273/298 = 1,83 L.
  2. a corrects for the attraction between molecules (lowers the measured pressure); b corrects for the molecules' own volume.
