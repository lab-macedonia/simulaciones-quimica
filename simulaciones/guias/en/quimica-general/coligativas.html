Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Calculate molality, mole fraction and % m/m.
  • Apply the four colligative properties with the van 't Hoff factor.
  • Predict the direction of solvent flow in osmosis.

Prior knowledge

  • Mole and molar concentration.
  • Vapor pressure and phase changes.

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“Colligative properties depend on the identity of the solute.”They depend only on the number of particles.
“The i of NaCl is always 2.”It is ≈ 1.9 because of ion association at high concentration.
“The solvent flows toward the more dilute solution.”It flows from the dilute solution toward the one more concentrated in solute.

B. Essay

1. Calculate the boiling point of a 0.50 m NaCl solution (Kb = 0.512 °C/m, i = 2).

2. Calculate π for 0.100 M glucose at 25 °C.

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. A. 2.0 mol/kg — m = mol of solute / kg of solvent = 0.50 / 0.250 = 2.0 mol/kg.
  2. D. 3,72 °C — m = 1.00 mol/kg, i = 2: ΔT_f = i·K_f·m = 2 × 1.86 × 1.00 = 3.72 °C; it freezes at −3.72 °C.
  3. C. CaCl₂ — It depends on i·m: CaCl₂ (i = 3) > NaCl (i = 2) > glucose (i = 1).
  4. B. Lower than that of the pure solvent — Raoult's law: P = x_solvent·P° and x < 1, hence P < P°.
  5. A. 100,51 °C — ΔT_b = 1 × 0,512 × 1,00 = 0,512 °C → 100,51 °C.
  6. D. 2.45 atm — π = M·R·T = 0.100 × 0.08206 × 298.15 = 2.45 atm (i = 1).
  7. C. From the dilute side to the concentrated side — Water passes through the membrane toward the more concentrated solution, tending to equalize the concentrations.
  8. B. 5 — Al₂(SO₄)₃ → 2 Al³⁺ + 3 SO₄²⁻: 5 particles per formula unit.
  9. C. 0.1 m CaCl₂ — CaCl₂ contributes i = 3.
  10. B. decreases — Raoult: P = x·P°.
  11. C. greater than π — It overcomes the osmotic pressure.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. ΔTb = 2 × 0,512 × 0,50 = 0,512 °C → 100,51 °C.
  2. π = MRT = 0.100 × 0.0821 × 298 = 2.45 atm.
