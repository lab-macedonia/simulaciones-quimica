Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Identify anode, cathode and electron flow.
  • Calculate the E° of a cell and apply Nernst.
  • Relate E°, ΔG° and K.

Prior knowledge

  • Redox reactions and half-reactions.
  • Chemical equilibrium.

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“The anode is positive in every cell.”In the galvanic cell the anode is negative.
“The salt bridge conducts electrons.”It conducts ions and maintains neutrality.
“E° is multiplied by the coefficients.”E° is intensive; it is not multiplied.

B. Essay

1. Calculate E of the cell Zn|Zn²⁺(0.010 M)‖Cu²⁺(1.0 M)|Cu at 25 °C (E° = 1.10 V).

2. Calculate K for the Daniell cell.

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. B. Zn is oxidized: Zn → Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻ — The anode is always the site of oxidation; zinc (lower E°) gives up electrons.
  2. A. +1,10 V — E°cell = E°cathode − E°anode = 0.34 − (−0.76) = +1.10 V (spontaneous).
  3. D. +0,46 V — 0.80 − 0.34 = +0.46 V; silver is reduced and copper is oxidized.
  4. C. 1,04 V — Q = 1/0.010 = 100; E = 1.10 − (0.0592/2)·log 100 = 1.10 − 0.0592 = 1.04 V.
  5. B. −212 kJ — ΔG° = −nFE° = −2 × 96,485 × 1.10 = −212 kJ/mol.
  6. A. From the anode to the cathode — The electrons released in the oxidation (anode) travel through the wire to the cathode, where they are consumed.
  7. D. It maintains charge neutrality and closes the circuit — It allows ions to flow between half-cells and prevents the buildup of charge that would stop the reaction.
  8. C. E = 0 y Q = K — At equilibrium ΔG = 0, so E = 0 and the reaction quotient equals the constant: Q = K.
  9. B. oxidation — Always oxidation.
  10. B. decreases — Nernst: E = E° − (RT/nF) ln Q.
  11. B. equilibrium — Q = K.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. E = 1.10 − (0.0592/2) log(0.010/1.0) = 1.10 + 0.059 = 1.16 V.
  2. log K = nE°/0.0592 = 2 × 1.10/0.0592 = 37.2 → K ≈ 1.6 × 10³⁷.
