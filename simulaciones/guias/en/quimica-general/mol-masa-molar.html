The mole and molar mass

Molar mass from the periodic table, Avogadro's number, conversions among grams, moles and particles, molar volume of gases at STP and percent composition, with molecules and piles of particles in 3D.

Open the simulation

⏱ 45 minLevel: undergraduate, early years9 multiple-choice questions · 2 essay questions

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Calculate molar masses from the periodic table.
  • Convert between grams, moles and particles using Avogadro's number.
  • Use the molar volume of an ideal gas at STP and percent composition.

Prior knowledge

  • Atomic mass and chemical formulas.
  • Scientific notation.

#StageCore idea
1Molar massMolar mass is the sum of the atomic masses of the formula, in g/mol
2The mole and Avogadro1 mole = 6.022×10²³ entities, whether atoms, molecules or ions
3Grams ↔ moles ↔ particlesn = m / M · N = n · N_A
4Molar volume of a gasAt STP (0 °C, 1 atm) 1 mole of ideal gas occupies 22.4 L
5Percent composition% of each element = (n atoms · atomic mass) / M × 100

Suggested activity

From grams to particles

  1. Choose water and set 36 g in the simulation.
  2. Calculate n = m/M and compare it with the value shown.
  3. Calculate the molecules with N = n·N_A.
  4. Repeat with CO₂ and with iron and compare how many particles there are in 10 g of each.

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“One mole of any substance weighs the same.”It weighs its molar mass; what is equal is the number of entities.
“The molar volume of 22.4 L applies to solids.”Only for ideal gases at STP.
“Molar mass and atomic mass are the same thing.”They have the same numerical value but different units (g/mol versus u).

B. Essay

1. Calculate how many moles and molecules there are in 90 g of glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆, 180.16 g/mol).

2. What volume do 11 g of CO₂ occupy at STP?

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. D. 18.015 g/mol — 2·1,008 + 15,999.
  2. C. 1 mol — n = 44/44.
  3. B. 1.2×10²⁴ molecules — 2 × 6,02×10²³.
  4. A. 11,2 L — 0,5 × 22,4.
  5. D. 6.022×10²³ entities per mole — Entities in one mole.
  6. C. Equal in molecules/atoms: 1 mole of entities of each — A mole is a number of entities, not a mass.
  7. A. 44.0 g/mol — 12,011 + 2·15,999.
  8. A. 1,5×10²³ — 0,25 × 6,02×10²³.
  9. A. 11,2 % — 2,016/18,015.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. n = 0.50 mol; N = 3.0×10²³ molecules.
  2. n = 0.25 mol; V = 5.6 L.
