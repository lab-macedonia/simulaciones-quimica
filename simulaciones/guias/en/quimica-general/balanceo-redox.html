Redox balancing: the ion-electron method

Balance redox reactions in acidic and basic media step by step: oxidation numbers, half-reactions, balancing of O, H and charge with electrons, and the final sum, with the electron flow in 3D.

Open the simulation

⏱ 60 minLevel: undergraduate, early years9 multiple-choice questions · 2 essay questions

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Identify the species that is oxidized and the one that is reduced using oxidation numbers.
  • Balance reactions by the ion-electron method in acidic and basic media.
  • Check the balance of atoms and charge.

Prior knowledge

  • Oxidation numbers.
  • Balancing equations by inspection.

#StageCore idea
1Oxidation numbersWhatever increases its oxidation number is oxidized; whatever decreases it is reduced
2Half-reactionsSeparate the oxidation and the reduction
3Balance atoms, O and HAcidic medium: O with H₂O and H with H⁺. Basic medium: use OH⁻ and H₂O
4Balance the chargeAdd electrons to equalize the charges
5Equalize electrons and addThe electrons gained must equal those lost

Suggested activity

Balance step by step

  1. Choose MnO₄⁻ + Fe²⁺ and try to write the half-reactions before revealing them.
  2. Reveal each step and compare with your result.
  3. Switch to the reaction in basic medium and explain what changes.
  4. Verify that the final sum has equal charge on both sides.

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“Electrons always go on the left.”In oxidation they appear on the right; in reduction, on the left.
“You can multiply the coefficients without equalizing the e⁻.”The electrons given up must equal those gained.
“In basic medium H⁺ is used.”OH⁻ and H₂O are used.

B. Essay

1. Balance Cr₂O₇²⁻ + Fe²⁺ → Cr³⁺ + Fe³⁺ in acidic medium.

2. Identify the oxidizing and reducing agents in Cu + NO₃⁻ → Cu²⁺ + NO.

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. D. +7 — Mn + 4(−2) = −1 → Mn = +7.
  2. C. H₂O — Then the H with H⁺.
  3. B. on the right — They are lost and are a product.
  4. A. 5 — +7 → +2.
  5. D. 6 e⁻ (3Cu and 2NO₃⁻) — The LCM of 2 and 3 is 6.
  6. C. it is oxidized — It reduces another by giving up e⁻.
  7. A. 5 e⁻ — +7 a +2.
  8. A. is reduced — It oxidizes another by gaining electrons.
  9. A. H₂O — Then H⁺ for the H.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. Cr₂O₇²⁻ + 6 Fe²⁺ + 14 H⁺ → 2 Cr³⁺ + 6 Fe³⁺ + 7 H₂O.
  2. Oxidizing agent: NO₃⁻ (N +5 → +2); reducing agent: Cu (0 → +2).
