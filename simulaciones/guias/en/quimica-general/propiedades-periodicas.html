Periodic Properties

Seven properties with reference data (Pauling, ionization, affinity, radii) and an interactive plot to compare trends.

Open the simulation

⏱ 50 minLevel: undergraduate, early years3 multiple-choice questions · 2 essay questions

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Describe the periodic trends of radius, IE, EN and EA.
  • Explain the trends with Zeff and shielding.
  • Justify exceptions such as Be/B or N/O.

Prior knowledge

  • Electron configuration.
  • Structure of the table.

Suggested activity

Rank without a table

  1. Rank Na, Mg, Al, Si, Cl by radius and IE.
  2. Compare with the simulation.
  3. Plot IE₁ for period 2 and explain the jumps.
  4. Write an explanation using Zeff.

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“Radius increases across a period.”It decreases because of greater Zeff.
“IE always decreases across the period.”It increases with exceptions (Be→B, N→O).
“Anions are smaller than their atom.”They are larger because of repulsion.

B. Essay

1. Explain why the IE₁ of O is lower than that of N.

2. Define effective nuclear charge.

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. B. F — Upper right corner, excluding noble gases.
  2. B. smaller — It loses a shell.
  3. D. He — Period 1 noble gases.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. In O the paired 2p electron repels its partner and is easier to remove.
  2. Zeff = Z − σ, the charge that a valence electron actually feels.
