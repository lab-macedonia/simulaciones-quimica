Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Calculate heat with q = m·c·ΔT.
  • Distinguish exothermic and endothermic processes.
  • Apply Hess's law and enthalpies of formation.

Prior knowledge

  • Energy, heat and temperature.
  • Balancing equations.

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“Exothermic means that heat is added.”The system releases heat: ΔH < 0.
“ΔH does not change when the reaction is reversed.”It changes sign.
“Heat and temperature are the same thing.”Heat is energy transfer; T measures the average kinetic energy.

B. Essay

1. Calculate ΔH° for CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O(l) with ΔH°f = −74.8; −393.5; −285.8 kJ/mol.

2. Explain Hess's law with an example.

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. B. 4.18 kJ — q = m·c·ΔT = 100 × 4.18 × 10 = 4180 J = 4.18 kJ.
  2. A. 26,7 °C — Tf = (100·0.385·100 + 100·4.18·20)/(100·0.385 + 100·4.18) = 12,210/456.5 = 26.7 °C.
  3. D. ΔH < 0 and the surroundings warm up — The system releases heat (q < 0, ΔH < 0) and gives it to the surroundings, which warm up.
  4. C. 6,8 °C — q = 57.1 × 0.050 = 2.855 kJ = 2855 J; ΔT = 2855/(100 × 4.18) = 6.8 °C.
  5. B. −393.5 kJ — Hess's law: the equations and their ΔH are added: −110.5 + (−283.0) = −393.5 kJ.
  6. A. −890.3 kJ — ΔH° = [−393.5 + 2(−285.8)] − [−74.8 + 0] = −965.1 + 74.8 = −890.3 kJ/mol.
  7. D. Changes sign and doubles — ΔH is extensive (proportional to the amounts) and changes sign when the direction is reversed.
  8. C. Cools down — Endothermic process: it absorbs heat from the solution (and the surroundings), which cools down.
  9. A. it doubles — ΔH is extensive.
  10. C. zero — By definition.
  11. A. 4,18 J/g·°C — High value: a good thermal regulator.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. ΔH° = (−393.5 + 2(−285.8)) − (−74.8) = −890.3 kJ/mol.
  2. ΔH depends only on the initial and final states: C→CO (−110.5) + CO→CO₂ (−283.0) = C→CO₂ (−393.5).
