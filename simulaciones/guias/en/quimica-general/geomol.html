Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Predict geometry with VSEPR from AXₙEₘ.
  • Determine ideal bond angles and their distortions.
  • Decide whether a molecule is polar using the vector sum.

Prior knowledge

  • Lewis structures.
  • Electronegativity.

Suggested activity

Geometry and polarity

  1. Predict the geometry and polarity of CO₂, H₂O, NH₃, BF₃ and SF₄.
  2. Build them in the builder.
  3. Compare with the sum of dipoles.
  4. Explain what changes when lone pairs are added.

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“Every polar bond makes the molecule polar.”The vector sum can be zero (CO₂, CCl₄).
“Lone pairs do not affect the angles.”They repel more and close the bond angles.
“Electron geometry = molecular geometry.”Molecular geometry excludes lone pairs.

Question bank

A. Multiple choice

1. The geometry of NH₃ is…

  1. tetrahedral
  2. trigonal pyramidal
  3. planar
  4. bent

2. The ideal angle in octahedral geometry is…

  1. 90°
  2. 109,5°
  3. 120°
  4. 180°

3. Which is nonpolar?

  1. H₂O
  2. NH₃
  3. CCl₄
  4. CHCl₃

B. Essay

1. Predict the shape of XeF₄ and its polarity.

2. Explain why H₂O has an angle smaller than 109.5°.

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. B. trigonal pyramidal — AX₃E.
  2. A. 90° — Six electron groups.
  3. C. CCl₄ — Tetrahedral symmetry.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. AX₄E₂: square planar; the dipoles cancel: nonpolar.
  2. The two lone pairs repel more than the bonding pairs: 104.5°.
