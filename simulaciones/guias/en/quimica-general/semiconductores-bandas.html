Semiconductors and Band Theory

Band theory in 3D: metals, insulators and semiconductors according to the width of the band gap, thermal population of electrons, n- and p-type doping, p–n junction and LED color from the gap energy.

Open the simulation

⏱ 50 minLevel: undergraduate, early years8 multiple-choice questions · 2 essay questions

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Classify metals, semiconductors and insulators by their gap.
  • Explain the effect of T and doping.
  • Relate Eg to the color of an LED.

Prior knowledge

  • Molecular orbitals.
  • Photon energy.

Suggested activity

Choosing a material for an LED

  1. With the simulation choose Eg to emit red, green and blue light.
  2. Calculate λ = 1240/Eg.
  3. Compare with GaAs, GaN and Si.
  4. Explain why Si is not a good LED.

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“Semiconductors do not conduct.”They conduct depending on T and doping.
“A metal conducts more when heated.”It conducts less; in semiconductors it conducts more.
“p-type doping adds positive charges.”It creates holes; the material remains neutral.

B. Essay

1. Explain the p–n junction as a rectifier.

2. Calculate the relative fraction of carriers at 600 K versus 300 K for Eg = 1.1 eV.

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. D. increases — More electrons in the CB.
  2. C. n-type — P contributes e⁻.
  3. B. Eg ≈ 5.5 eV — Very wide band gap.
  4. A. 500 nm — 1240/2.48 = 500 nm.
  5. D. overlap — Eg = 0.
  6. B. p-type — B has 3 valence e⁻.
  7. B. 650 nm — 1240/1,9.
  8. C. > 4 eV — Large gap.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. In forward bias carriers cross the junction; in reverse bias the depletion zone widens and almost no current flows.
  2. nᵢ ∝ T^1.5 exp(−Eg/2kT). The ratio ≈ 2.8 × exp(1.1/(2×8.617×10⁻⁵)×(1/300 − 1/600)) ≈ 10⁵: it grows by several orders of magnitude.
