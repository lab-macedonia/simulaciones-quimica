Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Read regions, curves, triple point and critical point.
  • Predict phase changes when varying T or P.
  • Explain why water and CO₂ differ.

Prior knowledge

  • States of matter.

  3. Do the same with CO₂.
  4. Explain the sublimation of dry ice.

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“The triple point is a range.”It is a single (T, P) pair.
“Above the critical point there is liquid.”There is a supercritical fluid.
“In all substances the solid–liquid curve has a positive slope.”In water it is negative.

B. Essay

1. Explain, using the water diagram, why it is possible to skate on ice.

2. Define supercritical fluid and give a use.

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. C. Solid, liquid and gas coexist in equilibrium — The triple point is the only (T, P) at which the three phases are in equilibrium (F = 0).
  2. B. 273.16 K and 611.66 Pa — The triple point of water is 273.16 K and ≈0.006 atm. 647.1 K and 22.06 MPa is the critical point.
  3. A. a supercritical fluid — There is no liquid–gas interface: the supercritical fluid has intermediate properties.
  4. D. ice is less dense than liquid water — ΔVfus < 0; by Clapeyron, dP/dT = ΔH/(TΔV) < 0: more pressure favors the liquid.
  5. C. sublimes (there is no liquid) — The triple point of CO₂ is at 5.1 atm; below that pressure it goes directly from solid to gas (−78.5 °C).
  6. B. decrease — It boils when vapor P = external P; with lower external P it is reached at lower T (pressure cookers: the opposite).
  7. A. 1 — F = C − P + 2 = 1 − 2 + 2 = 1: T can be varied and P is fixed (or vice versa).
  8. D. ΔHvap (Clausius–Clapeyron) — d ln P/dT = ΔHvap/(RT²): the greater the ΔHvap, the steeper the curve.
  9. B. 0,01 °C — 273,16 K, 611 Pa.
  10. B. sublimes — Below its triple pressure (5.1 atm).
  11. A. 0 — F = C − P + 2 = 1 − 3 + 2.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. The solid–liquid curve of water has a negative slope, but the pressure of a skate lowers the melting point by only a fraction of a degree. Gliding is now attributed mainly to the nearly liquid surface layer and to frictional heat.
  2. Above Tc and Pc there is no liquid–gas distinction; supercritical CO₂ decaffeinates coffee and extracts natural products.
