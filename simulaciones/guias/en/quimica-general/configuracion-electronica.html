Electron Configuration

Orbitals, full and condensed notation, orbital diagrams and ions.

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Write full and condensed configurations.
  • Draw orbital diagrams applying Aufbau, Pauli and Hund.
  • Obtain the configuration of ions and recognize exceptions.

Prior knowledge

  • Quantum numbers.
  • Periodic table by blocks.

Suggested activity

Configurations and magnetism

  1. Write the configuration of Fe, Fe²⁺ and Fe³⁺.
  2. Draw diagrams and count unpaired electrons.
  3. Check with the simulation.
  4. Predict which is more paramagnetic.

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“The 4s electrons are lost after the 3d.”When a transition metal is ionized the 4s are lost first.
“Hund says orbitals are filled with opposite spins.”They are first occupied with parallel spin.
«Cr es [Ar]4s²3d⁴».It is [Ar]4s¹3d⁵ (half-filled).

Question bank

A. Multiple choice

1. The configuration of Na⁺ is…

  1. 1s²2s²2p⁶3s¹
  2. 1s²2s²2p⁶
  3. 1s²2s²2p⁶3s²
  4. 1s²2s²2p⁵

2. Number of unpaired electrons in N:

  1. 1
  2. 2
  3. 3
  4. 0

3. The Pauli exclusion principle states that…

  1. two e⁻ cannot have all four quantum numbers equal
  2. the lowest-energy level is filled first
  3. degenerate orbitals are filled with parallel spin
  4. there is no limit of e⁻ per orbital

B. Essay

1. Write the condensed configuration of Cu and explain the exception.

2. Draw the orbital diagram of O and O²⁻.

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. B. 1s²2s²2p⁶ — It loses the 3s electron.
  2. C. 3 — 2p³.
  3. A. two e⁻ cannot have all four quantum numbers equal — Maximum 2 e⁻ per orbital, opposite spins.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. [Ar]4s¹3d¹⁰: the filled d subshell is more stable than 4s²3d⁹.
  2. O: 2p⁴ (one pair and two unpaired). O²⁻: 2p⁶ (three pairs).
