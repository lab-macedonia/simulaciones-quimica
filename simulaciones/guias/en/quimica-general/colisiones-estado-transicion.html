  • Explain rate with collision theory.
  • Interpret the reaction coordinate diagram.
  • Calculate Ea from an Arrhenius plot.

Suggested activity

Obtain Ea

  1. Measure k at several T in the simulation.
  2. Plot ln k versus 1/T.
  3. Calculate Ea from the slope (−Ea/R).
  4. Repeat with a catalyst and compare.

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“All collisions produce a reaction.”Only those with energy ≥ Ea and proper orientation.
“The catalyst changes ΔH.”It only lowers Ea.
“At higher T, Ea increases.”Ea is almost constant; the fraction of energetic molecules increases.

B. Essay

1. With k₁ = 0.010 at 300 K and k₂ = 0.040 at 320 K, calculate Ea.

2. State the Hammond postulate.

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. A. k = A·e^(−Ea/RT) — Exponential dependence.
  2. B. decreases Ea — New path with a lower barrier.
  3. B. an energy maximum — Maximum of the profile.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. Ea = R·ln(k₂/k₁)/(1/T₁ − 1/T₂) = 8.314×1.386/(2.083×10⁻⁴) = 55 kJ/mol.
  2. The transition state resembles the species closest in energy: early in exothermic reactions, late in endothermic ones.
