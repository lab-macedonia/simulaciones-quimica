Environmental chemistry and green chemistry

Ozone layer and CFCs, greenhouse effect (radiative forcing of CO₂), acid rain and green chemistry in 3D: atom economy, E-factor and twelve principles with comparison of synthetic routes.

Open the simulation

⏱ 50 minLevel: undergraduate, early years9 multiple-choice questions · 2 essay questions

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Explain the catalytic cycle of ozone destruction.
  • Relate CO₂ and radiative forcing.
  • Calculate atom economy and E factor.

Prior knowledge

  • Radical reactions and catalysis.
  • pH and acids.

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“The greenhouse effect is always bad.”It is natural and necessary; the problem is its intensification.
“High yield implies a green process.”It can generate a lot of waste if AE is low.
“Normal rain is neutral.”It has pH ≈ 5.6 because of CO₂.

B. Essay

1. Calculate the AE of CH₃CH₂Br + NaOH → CH₃CH₂OH + NaBr.

2. Calculate ΔF for CO₂ = 560 ppm.

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. A. 100 % — All atoms go into the product.
  2. D. Cl· acting as a catalyst — Cl· is regenerated.
  3. C. 5,6 — Dissolved CO₂ brings it to 5.6.
  4. B. kg of waste per kg of product — Sheldon's indicator.
  5. A. SO₂ — It is oxidized to H₂SO₄.
  6. D. ≈ 3,7 W/m² — 5.35·ln2.
  7. B. CFCs — Ozone-depleting substances.
  8. C. 100 % — All atoms go into the product.
  9. B. a lot of waste — kg waste/kg product.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. M(EtOH) = 46.07; sum = 108.97 + 40.00 = 148.97; AE = 30.9 %.
  2. ΔF = 5,35 ln(560/280) = 3,71 W/m².
