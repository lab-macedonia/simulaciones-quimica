Solubility and solubility product (Ksp)

3D simulator of the solubility of sparingly soluble salts: Ksp, molar solubility, common-ion effect, pH effect, quotient Q versus Ksp and selective precipitation (AgI/AgCl).

Open the simulation

⏱ 55 minLevel: undergraduate, early years11 multiple-choice questions · 2 essay questions

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Write Ksp and calculate solubility.
  • Analyze the common-ion and pH effects.
  • Predict precipitation by comparing Q and Ksp.

Prior knowledge

  • Chemical equilibrium.
  • Acids and bases.

Simulation walkthrough

Use the lesson (bottom player) for the guided walkthrough and the Lab tab to explore freely; “Back to the lesson” resumes the script.

#StageCore idea
1Dissolution equilibriumMₐXᵦ(s) ⇌ aMⁿ⁺ + bXᵐ⁻ → Ksp = [M]ᵃ[X]ᵇ
2Molar solubilityWith s = mol/L dissolved: Ksp = aᵃ·bᵇ·s^(a+b)
3Common-ion effectAdding an ion of the salt reduces its solubility (Le Châtelier)
4Effect of pHBasic anions (F⁻, CO₃²⁻, OH⁻) make the salt more soluble in acidic medium
5Q versus KspQ > Ksp: precipitates · Q = Ksp: saturated · Q < Ksp: no solid
6Selective precipitationWith 0.1 M I⁻ and Cl⁻ and increasing Ag⁺, AgI precipitates first

Suggested activity

Selective precipitation

  1. With 0.1 M Cl⁻ and I⁻ add Ag⁺ in the simulation.
  2. Determine which salt precipitates first.
  3. Calculate [I⁻] when AgCl begins to precipitate.
  4. Discuss quantitative separation.

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“A larger Ksp always means greater solubility.”Only if they have the same stoichiometry.
“The common ion increases solubility.”It decreases it.
“pH does not affect salts.”It affects basic anions.

B. Essay

1. Calculate the molar solubility of CaF₂ (Ksp = 3.5×10⁻¹¹).

2. Calculate the solubility of AgCl in 0.10 M NaCl (Ksp = 1.8×10⁻¹⁰).

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. A. [Ca²⁺]³[PO₄³⁻]² — Each concentration is raised to its coefficient; the solid does not appear.
  2. D. 1,3×10⁻⁵ M — s = √Ksp = 1.3×10⁻⁵ M.
  3. C. decreases (common ion) — Common-ion effect: the equilibrium shifts toward the solid.
  4. B. CaF₂ — F⁻ is the conjugate base of a weak acid (HF); Cl⁻, SO₄²⁻ (almost) and I⁻ do not react significantly.
  5. A. solid precipitates — The solution is supersaturated and the excess precipitates until Q = Ksp.
  6. D. decreases — More OH⁻ in solution: common-ion effect.
  7. C. AgI (lower Ksp) — With the same anion concentration and 1:1 stoichiometry, the one with the lower Ksp precipitates first.
  8. B. by their molar solubility s, not by Ksp — Ksp = aᵃbᵇ·s^(a+b) depends on the stoichiometry.
  9. B. [Mg][OH]² — It is raised to the coefficient.
  10. B. more dissolves — CO₃²⁻ + H⁺.
  11. B. there is no precipitate — Unsaturated solution.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. Ksp = 4s³ → s = (3.5×10⁻¹¹/4)^(1/3) = 2.1×10⁻⁴ M.
  2. s = Ksp/[Cl⁻] = 1.8×10⁻⁹ M.
