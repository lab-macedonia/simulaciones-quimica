Lipids, saponification and soaps

Triglycerides with saturated and unsaturated fatty acids (melting point), saponification with NaOH and calculation of the saponification value, micelles that emulsify grease and the effect of hard water on soap.

Open the simulation

⏱ 50 minLevel: undergraduate, early years9 multiple-choice questions · 2 essay questions

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Relate unsaturation and melting point.
  • Calculate the saponification value.
  • Explain the action of soap and the effect of hard water.

Prior knowledge

  • Esters and hydrolysis.
  • Polarity.

  3. Calculate the NaOH needed per kg.
  4. Increase the hardness and describe what happens to the micelles.

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“Soap is an ester.”It is the sodium salt of a fatty acid.
“The tails point toward the water.”The tails are hydrophobic and face the interior.
“Detergents precipitate with Ca²⁺ just like soap.”Synthetic detergents usually do not.

B. Essay

1. Calculate the saponification value of a triglyceride with M = 885 g/mol.

2. Write the reaction of soap with Ca²⁺.

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. D. glycerol and soap — Basic hydrolysis of the ester.
  2. C. lower the melting point — The kink hinders packing.
  3. B. toward the interior — They are hydrophobic.
  4. A. it forms insoluble salts with Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺ — (RCOO)₂Ca precipitates.
  5. D. 3 — One for each ester.
  6. C. short-chain — Lower M ⇒ more esters per gram.
  7. A. glycerol and 3 soaps — Saponification.
  8. A. liquid — Less packing.
  9. A. the concentration at which micelles form — Critical micelle concentration.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. 3 × 56,108 / 885 ≈ 190 mg KOH/g.
  2. 2 RCOO⁻ + Ca²⁺ → (RCOO)₂Ca↓.
