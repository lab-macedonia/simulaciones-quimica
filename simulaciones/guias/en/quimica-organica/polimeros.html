Polymers: addition and condensation

Chains of polyethylene, polypropylene, polystyrene, PVC, nylon 6,6 and PET in 3D: addition polymerization (initiation, propagation, termination) and condensation polymerization, degree of polymerization, Carothers equation, average molar mass and dispersity.

Open the simulation

⏱ 55 minLevel: undergraduate, early years9 multiple-choice questions · 2 essay questions

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Distinguish addition polymerization from condensation polymerization.
  • Calculate average molar mass and degree of polymerization.
  • Relate the structure of the repeating unit to properties such as Tg.

Prior knowledge

  • Alkenes and addition.
  • Formation of amides and esters.

Suggested activity

Compare six polymers

  1. Go through PE, PP, PS, PVC, nylon 6,6 and PET.
  2. Classify them according to the mechanism.
  3. Note the substituent or functional group of each one.
  4. Relate the size of the substituent to Tg.

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“Addition polymers lose atoms.”The repeating unit keeps the formula of the monomer.
“A polymer has a single molar mass.”It is a distribution; Mn, Mw and Đ are reported.
“A high Xn requires low conversion.”In condensation, p close to 1 is needed (Carothers).

B. Essay

1. Calculate Mn of a polyethylene with n = 1000.

2. How much H₂O is released on forming one mole of amide bonds in nylon?

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. D. polyethylene without releasing by-products — The C=C opens.
  2. C. a small molecule (H₂O) — Each amide/ester bond releases H₂O.
  3. B. 100 — 1/(1 − 0,99).
  4. A. all chains of the same size — Mw = Mn.
  5. D. polystyrene — Styrene = vinylbenzene.
  6. C. a diacid and a diamine — Amide bonds are formed.
  7. A. vinyl chloride — CH₂=CHCl.
  8. A. amide — Acid + amine.
  9. A. 20 — 1/(1 − 0,95).

Essay (marking guide)

  1. 1000 × 28.05 = 28,050 g/mol.
  2. 1 mol of H₂O per bond, 18.015 g.
