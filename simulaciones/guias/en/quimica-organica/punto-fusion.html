Melting Point and Purity

Capillary and melting-point apparatus in 3D: melting range, depression by impurities (eutectic), heating rate and mixed melting point to identify compounds.

Open the simulation

⏱ 35 minLevel: undergraduate, early years8 multiple-choice questions · 2 essay questions

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Use the melting range as a criterion of purity and identity.
  • Explain the melting-point depression caused by impurities.
  • Interpret a solid–liquid diagram with a eutectic.

Prior knowledge

  • Phase changes.
  • Mixtures and solutions.

#StageCore idea

Suggested activity

Mixed melting point

  1. Two samples that melt at 122 °C are handed out (benzoic acid and another compound).
  2. Mix 1:1 in the simulation.
  3. Interpret whether there is depression.
  4. Conclude whether they are the same compound.

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“An impure compound melts at a higher T.”It melts at a lower T and over a wider range.
“Heating quickly gives a better result.”It broadens and shifts the range; near melting, heat at 1–2 °C/min.
“The eutectic is the purest solid.”It is the composition of lowest melting point of a mixture.

B. Essay

1. A sample melts between 118 and 124 °C and the tabulated value is 122 °C. Interpret.

2. Explain the concept of a eutectic.

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. C. over a narrow range — Typical range 0.5–1 °C.
  2. B. it lowers and broadens the range — Melting point depression.
  3. A. 1–2 °C/min — To maintain thermal equilibrium.
  4. D. they are the same compound — A mixture of different compounds melts lower.
  5. C. the minimum melting temperature of the mixture — The solidus line.
  6. A. ≤ 2 °C — Narrow and sharp.
  7. B. the same compound — There is no depression.
  8. B. a lower chemical potential of the liquid because of the solute — The solute stabilizes the liquid.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. Wide 6 °C range: the sample is impure; it should be recrystallized.
  2. Composition of a mixture that melts like a pure compound at the lowest temperature of the system.
