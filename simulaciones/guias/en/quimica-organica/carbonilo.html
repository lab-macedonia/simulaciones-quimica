Nucleophilic addition to the carbonyl 3D

Aldehydes and ketones with different nucleophiles: Bürgi–Dunitz angle, tetrahedral intermediate, hemiacetal/acetal and nucleophilic acyl substitution.

Open the simulation

⏱ 50 minLevel: undergraduate, early years11 multiple-choice questions · 2 essay questions

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Explain the electrophilicity of the carbonyl carbon and the Bürgi–Dunitz trajectory.
  • Predict addition products of hydrides, Grignard reagents, cyanide and alcohols.
  • Distinguish nucleophilic addition (aldehydes/ketones) from acyl substitution (acid derivatives).

Prior knowledge

  • Polarity of the C=O bond and resonance.
  • Structure of aldehydes, ketones and carboxylic acids.

Suggested activity

  1. Build a table with nucleophile, product and conditions for each reaction seen.

  3. Compare the rate of hemiacetal formation in an aldehyde and in a ketone.

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“The nucleophile attacks the oxygen.”It attacks the δ+ carbon; the oxygen receives the π electrons and is protonated afterwards.
“Ketones are more reactive because they have more carbons.”The two alkyl groups donate density and hinder: ketones are less reactive than aldehydes.
“The acetal is formed with a single equivalent of alcohol.”The hemiacetal forms with one; the acetal requires two and acid catalysis.

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. D. The carbon — The O pulls electrons; the C becomes δ+ and is attacked by nucleophiles.
  2. C. planar sp² to tetrahedral sp³ — The π bond breaks: the tetrahedral intermediate forms.
  3. B. 107° — The nucleophile approaches at ≈ 107° to the C=O bond.
  4. A. Less hindrance and greater δ+ on C — A single R group: less electron donation and less hindrance.
  5. D. propan-2-ol — H⁻ adds to the C=O; after protonation: secondary alcohol.
  6. C. 2-metilpropan-2-ol — CH₃⁻ forms a C–C bond: tertiary alcohol (tert-butanol).
  7. B. 2-hidroxipropanonitrilo — CN⁻ adds to the C=O and the O⁻ is protonated: CH₃CH(OH)CN.
  8. A. acyl chloride — Cl⁻ is the best leaving group (pKa of HCl ≈ −7).
  9. B. benzyl alcohol — An aldehyde is reduced to a primary alcohol.
  10. B. HCN/CN⁻ — Nucleophilic addition of cyanide.
  11. C. sp³ — It goes from trigonal planar to tetrahedral.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. The intermediate alkoxide can expel the nucleophile if it is a good leaving group (weakly basic), regenerating the C=O; with carbanions or hydrides it is irreversible.
  2. In addition, the tetrahedral intermediate is protonated (there is no leaving group). In acyl substitution, the intermediate expels a leaving group L⁻ and regenerates the C=O.
