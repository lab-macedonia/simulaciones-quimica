Radicals and chain halogenation

3D simulator of radical halogenation of alkanes: radical stability, initiation, propagation and termination, chlorination versus bromination selectivity (Hammond postulate) and product distribution for propane.

⏱ 45 minLevel: undergraduate, early years11 multiple-choice questions · 2 essay questions

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Describe initiation, propagation and termination.
  • Explain the selectivity of bromination compared with chlorination.
  • Predict the major product of radical halogenation.

Prior knowledge

  • Homolytic and heterolytic cleavage.
  • Stability of alkyl radicals.

Suggested activity

Chlorination vs. bromination

  1. Predict the products of chlorination and bromination of propane.
  2. Calculate the expected ratio with relative reactivities (3.8 : 1 for Cl, 97 : 1 for Br).
  3. Check it in the simulation.
  4. Explain the Hammond postulate.

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“Light breaks C–H.”Light breaks the weak X–X bond, starting the chain.
“Cl• and Br• are equally selective.”Br• is much more selective.
“Termination is the most frequent step.”It is rare because of the low radical concentration.

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. A. 3° > 2° > 1° > methyl — More hyperconjugation = greater stability.
  2. D. two radicals — Each fragment keeps one electron.
  3. C. X• + R–H → HX + R• — H abstraction.
  4. B. its TS is late — Endothermic bromination: TS similar to the radical.
  5. A. 2-bromopropano — 97 % through the secondary H.
  6. D. combination of two radicals — Radicals are consumed without being regenerated.
  7. C. R–H + X₂ → R–X + H–X — The intermediate radicals cancel out.
  8. B. planar (sp²) — The unpaired e⁻ occupies a p orbital.
  9. B. 2-bromo-2-methylpropane — The tertiary radical is more stable.
  10. A. O₂ — Oxygen traps radicals.
  11. B. late — Endothermic reaction.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. Cl₂ → 2Cl•; Cl• + CH₄ → HCl + CH₃•; CH₃• + Cl₂ → CH₃Cl + Cl•; termination: 2Cl• → Cl₂, etc.
  2. It is very exothermic and poorly selective.
