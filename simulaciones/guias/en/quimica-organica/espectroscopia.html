Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Relate IR bands to functional groups.
  • Interpret chemical shift, integration and multiplicity in ¹H NMR.
  • Propose a structure from IR and NMR data.

Prior knowledge

  • Basic functional groups.
  • Concept of chemical equivalence of hydrogens.

Suggested activity

Mystery spectrum

  1. Give the molecular formula C₄H₈O₂ and two IR and NMR data points.
  2. Calculate the degrees of unsaturation (DBE).
  3. Propose at least two isomers and choose the correct one using the data.
  4. Verify the result with the “mystery spectrum” in the simulation.

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“Integration gives the exact number of H.”It gives the relative ratio; it must be scaled to the molecular formula.
“A triplet has 3 neighboring H.”n + 1 rule: a triplet indicates 2 neighboring H.
“Any IR band will do.”First look at the diagnostic bands (C=O, O–H, N–H, C≡N) above 1500 cm⁻¹.

B. Essay

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. A. a carbonyl group (C=O) — The C=O stretch is very intense and appears at 1650–1750 cm⁻¹.
  2. D. alcohol O–H (H bonding) — Hydrogen bonds broaden the O–H stretching band.
  3. C. quartet — It has 3 neighboring H (CH₃): 3+1 = 4 lines.
  4. B. 1 — The six H atoms are equivalent by symmetry: a single signal (singlet).
  5. A. an aldehyde H — The CHO hydrogen is strongly deshielded (9–10 ppm).
  6. D. CH₃, CH₂ and OH have 3, 2 and 1 H — The area of each signal is proportional to the number of H atoms that give rise to it.
  7. C. As a reference: δ = 0 — Tetramethylsilane: 12 equivalent, highly shielded H atoms; it defines δ = 0 ppm.
  8. B. 2250 cm⁻¹ — The 2100–2260 cm⁻¹ region corresponds to triple bonds (C≡N and C≡C).
  9. D. 4 — DBE = (2·6 + 2 − 6)/2 = 4.
  10. B. it gives a single signal at δ = 0 ppm — It is the reference standard with 12 equivalent H.
  11. C. C≡N — Nitrile triple bond.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. Propanone: ketone C=O and six equivalent H (two CH₃) in a singlet.
  2. They are strongly deshielded by the electronegativity of O and the anisotropy of the C=O bond.
