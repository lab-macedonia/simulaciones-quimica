Synthesis of aspirin

Acetylation of salicylic acid with acetic anhydride: step-by-step mechanism, limiting and excess reagent, theoretical and percent yield, effect of temperature, time and catalyst, and a purity test with FeCl₃.

Open the simulation

⏱ 55 minLevel: undergraduate, early years9 multiple-choice questions · 2 essay questions

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Describe the mechanism of acetylation of salicylic acid.
  • Calculate the limiting reagent and the yield.
  • Interpret the FeCl₃ test.

Prior knowledge

  • Nucleophilic acyl substitution.
  • Stoichiometry.

Suggested activity

Optimize the yield

  1. Start from 2.0 g of salicylic acid, 5 mL of anhydride, 75 °C and 15 min.
  2. Reduce the time to 3 min and observe FeCl₃.
  3. Remove the catalyst and compare.
  4. Propose the best conditions.

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“The anhydride is the limiting reagent because it is the liquid.”It is in excess; salicylic acid is limiting.
“The yield can exceed 100 % if more is weighed.”It indicates a wet or impure product.
“FeCl₃ detects aspirin.”It detects free phenols.

B. Essay

1. With 3.00 g of salicylic acid, what is the theoretical yield of aspirin?

2. You obtained 3.10 g. What is the percent yield?

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. D. the O of the phenolic –OH — It forms the ester.
  2. C. salicylic acid — The anhydride is in excess.
  3. B. 69 % — 1,8/2,6 × 100.
  4. A. unreacted salicylic acid — Presence of free phenol.
  5. D. acetic acid — It leaves as a leaving group.
  6. C. increases the rate — Arrhenius dependence.
  7. A. 180.16 g/mol — C₉H₈O₄.
  8. A. salicylic acid present — Free phenol.
  9. A. acetic acid — From the anhydride.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. 3,00/138,12 × 180,16 = 3,91 g.
  2. 3,10/3,91 × 100 = 79 %.
