Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Order acid derivatives by reactivity according to the leaving group.
  • Describe the addition–elimination mechanism.
  • Explain the stability of the amide by resonance.

Prior knowledge

  • Carbonyl and nucleophilic addition.
  • Acidity and basicity (pKa).

Suggested activity

Reactivity cascade

  1. Draw a ladder: chloride > anhydride > ester ≈ acid > amide.
  2. With the simulation, convert an acyl chloride into an ester and an amide; then try the reverse path.
  3. Explain which conversions are favorable and why.
  4. Describe how an ester is saponified.

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“The best leaving group is the one that donates the most.”It is the one that best stabilizes the negative charge (weakest base).
“The amide is more reactive than the ester.”It is the least reactive: N→C=O resonance is strong.
“Acid hydrolysis of an ester is irreversible.”Saponification is; the acid one is an equilibrium.

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. B. Acyl chloride > anhydride > ester > amide — Reactivity follows the quality of the leaving group and the lower donation from L: Cl⁻ is the best, NH₂⁻ the worst.
  2. A. N donates its pair to the C=O and weakens the double bond — N⁺=C–O⁻ resonance gives the C=O single-bond character.
  3. D. Addition–elimination with a tetrahedral intermediate — The nucleophile adds to the C=O and then L⁻ leaves from the tetrahedral intermediate.
  4. C. The acid formed is deprotonated to the carboxylate (pKa 4.76 versus 15.9 for the alcohol) — The final acid–base step drives the equilibrium completely toward the carboxylate.
  5. B. Acyl chloride + amine → amide — One can only go from a more reactive derivative to a less reactive one.
  6. A. Cl⁻ (pKa of HCl = −7) — The best leaving group is the weakest base, that is, the one with the strongest conjugate acid.
  7. D. its partial double-bond character — Resonance makes the O=C–N group planar and restricts rotation.
  8. C. The O⁻ regenerates the C=O and expels Cl⁻ — The O⁻ pair rebuilds the double bond and expels the best leaving group.
  9. B. amide — Substitution of Cl by NH₂.
  10. B. carboxylate and alcohol — The carboxylate is not reconverted in basic medium.
  11. B. HO⁻ — Hydroxide attacks the carbonyl.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. Cl⁻ is a better leaving group than RO⁻: the conversion would be uphill. It is done via the acid and SOCl₂.
  2. CH₃COCl + 2 CH₃NH₂ → CH₃CONHCH₃ + CH₃NH₃⁺Cl⁻ (the second equivalent neutralizes the HCl).
