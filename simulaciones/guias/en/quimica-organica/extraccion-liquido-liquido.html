Liquid–liquid extraction

3D separatory funnel: partition coefficient, phases by density, multiple extractions with small portions and acid–base extraction according to pH and pKa.

Open the simulation

⏱ 45 minLevel: undergraduate, early years9 multiple-choice questions · 2 essay questions

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Apply the partition coefficient.
  • Compare one large extraction with several small ones.
  • Use pH to extract acids and bases.

Prior knowledge

  • Miscibility and polarity.
  • Acid–base equilibrium.

#StageCore idea
1Principle of extractionA solute is distributed between two immiscible solvents according to its affinity
2Partition coefficientK = [solute]organic / [solute]aqueous at equilibrium
3Several small extractionsWith the same total volume, n portions extract more than one large one
4Acid–base extractionAn acid or base becomes water-soluble when it ionizes: the pH controls which phase it is in
5Laboratory techniqueShake, vent, let it separate and drain the lower phase

Suggested activity

Separate acid, base and neutral

  1. Mixture of benzoic acid, aniline and naphthalene in ether.
  2. Design the sequence with HCl and NaOH.
  3. Simulate each extraction.
  4. Indicate the phase where each compound ends up.

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“The top phase is always the organic solvent.”It depends on density (dichloromethane ends up at the bottom).
“One large extraction = three small ones.”Several small ones extract more with the same total volume.
“The funnel is stoppered without venting.”You must vent to relieve pressure.

B. Essay

1. Calculate the fraction remaining in water after two extractions with 50 mL (K = 3, V_aq = 100 mL).

2. Describe how to recover aniline from the acidic aqueous phase.

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. A. bottom — Denser than water.
  2. D. several small extractions — (1/(1+Kr/n))ⁿ decreases as n increases.
  3. C. greater than its pKa — It ionizes to the carboxylate.
  4. B. dilute HCl — It forms the water-soluble ammonium salt.
  5. A. vent the funnel — Ether is volatile and the pressure rises.
  6. D. 6/7 ≈ 86 % — E = Kr/(1 + Kr).
  7. B. aqueous NaHCO₃ — It forms the soluble carboxylate.
  8. C. 80 % — 4/(4 + 1) = 0,8.
  9. B. saturated NaCl solution — It increases the ionic strength.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. Fraction remaining per stage = 1/(1 + K·Vo/Va) = 1/(1 + 1.5) = 0.4; after two: 0.16.
  2. Basify with NaOH to deprotonate the anilinium and extract with ether.
