Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Predict the mechanism (SN1, SN2, E1, E2) according to substrate, nucleophile/base and solvent.
  • Relate the mechanism to the stereochemistry of the product and the kinetics.
  • Interpret energy profiles and transition states.

Prior knowledge

  • Nucleophiles, electrophiles and leaving groups.
  • Carbocation stability.

Suggested activity

Decision table

  1. Complete a table with 1°, 2°, 3° substrate versus strong/weak nucleophile and bulky base.
  2. Use the predictor to check each cell.
  3. Explain the cases in which SN and E compete.
  4. Predict the stereochemistry of the product in each case.

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“SN2 occurs in tertiary substrates.”Steric hindrance prevents it; tertiary substrates go by SN1/E1/E2.
“SN1 inverts the configuration.”It gives a (partial) racemic mixture because of the planar carbocation.
“A strong base always gives SN2.”A strong, bulky base favors E2.

B. Essay

1. Predict the mechanism and product of 2-bromo-2-methylpropane in hot ethanol.

2. Explain the Walden inversion in SN2.

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. B. substrate and nucleophile — v = k[RX][Nu].
  2. B. anti-periplanar — Optimal orbital overlap.
  3. B. polar protic — It stabilizes the carbocation and the leaving group.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. Tertiary substrate, protic solvent: SN1/E1. Mixture of ether (2-ethoxy-2-methylpropane) and 2-methylpropene.
  2. The nucleophile attacks from behind the leaving group; the configuration of the carbon is inverted.
