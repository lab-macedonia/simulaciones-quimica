Aromatic Heterocycles

Pyrrole, furan, thiophene, pyridine, imidazole and pyrimidine in 3D: lone pair in the π system or in the plane, basicity (pKa of the conjugate acid), reactivity and regioselectivity toward electrophiles and presence in biomolecules.

Open the simulation

⏱ 50 minLevel: undergraduate, early years9 multiple-choice questions · 2 essay questions

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Distinguish pyrrole-type and pyridine-type lone pairs.
  • Predict basicity and sites of electrophilic and nucleophilic attack.
  • Recognize heterocycles in biomolecules.

Prior knowledge

  • Aromaticity and Hückel.
  • SEAr.

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“Pyrrole is basic because it has N.”Its pair is part of the aromatic system.
“Pyridine reacts quickly with electrophiles.”It is electron-poor; it reacts with nucleophiles at C2/C4.
“Furan is not aromatic.”It is, although with a lower resonance energy than benzene.

B. Essay

1. Explain why the pKaH of pyridine is 5.2 and that of pyrrole ≈ −3.8.

2. Draw the resonance forms of the σ complex for attack at C2 of pyrrole.

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. C. is part of the π sextet — It is in the p orbital.
  2. B. is in the plane (sp²) — It does not take part in the sextet.
  3. A. C2 — The cation has 3 resonance forms.
  4. D. much less reactive than benzene — The N withdraws electron density.
  5. C. histidine — pKa of the ring ≈ 6.
  6. B. C2 y C4 — The charge ends up on N.
  7. A. one pyrrole-type N and one pyridine-type N — That is why it is amphoteric.
  8. A. C2 — Most stable σ complex.
  9. B. bicyclic system of pyrimidine and imidazole — Two fused rings.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. In pyridine the lone pair is in the plane and available; in pyrrole it is part of the aromatic sextet and protonating it destroys it.
  2. Three resonance forms (charge on C3, C5 and N); at C3 only two.
