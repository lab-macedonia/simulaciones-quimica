Diels–Alder reaction

3D simulator of the Diels–Alder [4+2] reaction: s-cis conformation of the diene, concerted mechanism, frontier orbitals, substituent effect, endo rule and thermodynamics (ΔG versus T).

Open the simulation

⏱ 50 minLevel: undergraduate, early years11 multiple-choice questions · 2 essay questions

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Identify diene and dienophile and predict the resulting cyclohexene.
  • Apply frontier orbitals and the endo rule.
  • Relate reversibility to temperature (ΔG = ΔH − TΔS).

Prior knowledge

  • π molecular orbitals of butadiene and ethene.
  • Basic thermodynamics.

Suggested activity

Design an adduct

  1. Choose a diene (cyclopentadiene) and a dienophile (maleic anhydride).
  2. Predict the product and the endo rule.
  3. Confirm the HOMO–LUMO overlap in the simulation.
  4. Discuss why it is favored at low temperature.

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“The diene can be in any conformation.”It must be s-cis; s-trans does not cyclize.
“It is a stepwise reaction with intermediates.”It is concerted: a single transition state.
“Donor groups on the dienophile speed it up.”With an electron-rich diene, electron-withdrawing groups on the dienophile speed it up.

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. B. s-cis — Only in s-cis do the two ends of the diene face the dienophile.
  2. A. [4+2], concertada — 4 π electrons of the diene and 2 of the dienophile participate in a single cyclic transition state.
  3. D. It lowers the LUMO and speeds up the reaction — The HOMO(diene)–LUMO(dienophile) gap is reduced.
  4. C. HOMO of the diene and LUMO of the dienophile — Both ends have constructive phase overlap between the two frontier orbitals.
  5. B. Cyclohexene — A six-membered ring with a single double bond (between C2 and C3 of the diene) is formed.
  6. A. under the diene, due to secondary orbital interactions — In the endo the π system of the substituent interacts with C2 and C3 of the diene.
  7. D. Two molecules join into one: freedom of movement is lost — The reduction in the number of particles decreases entropy.
  8. C. −TΔS becomes more positive and ΔG approaches 0 — ΔG = ΔH − TΔS with negative ΔS: at high T, ΔG rises until it becomes positive.
  9. B. endo (kinetic) — Kinetic control: secondary orbital interactions.
  10. A. 4π + 2π — [4+2] cycloaddition.
  11. B. T is high — −TΔS comes to dominate.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. Cyclohexene with two methyls at C4–C5 and a CHO at C1 (1,2 “ortho” regiochemistry).
  2. The reaction is concerted and suprafacial: the configuration of the dienophile is retained.
