R/S stereochemistry (CIP) 3D

Cahn–Ingold–Prelog priorities, R/S assignment viewing from the side opposite the lowest-priority group, mirror-image enantiomers and a configuration challenge.

Open the simulation

⏱ 45 minLevel: undergraduate, early years11 multiple-choice questions · 2 essay questions

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Identify stereogenic centers in a molecule.
  • Assign CIP priorities and R or S configuration to a chiral center.
  • Relate the exchange of two substituents to the inversion of configuration.

Prior knowledge

  • Lewis structure and tetrahedral geometry of the sp³ carbon.
  • Atomic number and position of the elements in the periodic table.

Suggested activity

Assign R/S to three molecules and check it in 3D

  1. Each student chooses an amino acid or a chiral alcohol and assigns R/S on paper.
  2. Check in the simulation by rotating the molecule until the lowest-priority group points away.
  3. Swap two substituents and record what happens to the configuration.
  4. Discuss in pairs why two enantiomers have the same melting point but rotate light in opposite directions.

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“R means dextrorotatory and S levorotatory.”R/S is a formal priority rule; the sign of the optical rotation (+/−) is measured in the polarimeter and is not deduced from R/S.
It is viewed from any side of the molecule.The lowest-priority group must point away from the observer; if it points toward the observer, the result is inverted.
Only the number of carbons in each branch is compared.The atomic number of the first atom is compared; in a tie, one moves on to the next set of atoms.

B. Essay

1. Assign CIP priorities to the four substituents of the chiral carbon of alanine (–NH₂, –COOH, –CH₃, –H) and deduce whether L-alanine is S or R.

2. Explain why two enantiomers share physical properties but may differ in biological activity.

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. D. OH > NH₂ > CH₃ > H — Z: O (8) > N (7) > C (6) > H (1).
  2. C. –CH₂OH — Both start with C; their sets are (O,H,H) and (C,C,H): O > C at the first point of difference.
  3. B. COOH > CHO > CH₂OH — (O,O,O) > (O,O,H) > (O,H,H).
  4. A. R — Clockwise = R (rectus).
  5. D. S — Swapping two substituents is equivalent to the mirror image: R ↔ S.
  6. C. All physical properties except the direction of optical rotation — They are non-superimposable mirror images; they rotate the plane of polarized light in opposite directions.
  7. B. I — Iodine (Z = 53) has the highest atomic number.
  8. A. No, because it has two identical substituents — A stereocenter requires four different substituents.
  9. C. 8 — 2ⁿ = 2³ = 8.
  10. B. enantiomers — They are nonsuperimposable mirror images.
  11. D. H — H has the lowest atomic number.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. Priorities: NH₂ (N) > COOH (C with O,O,O) > CH₃ > H. With H pointing back, NH₂→COOH→CH₃ turns counterclockwise in L-alanine: it is (S).
  2. Physical properties depend on intermolecular interactions with achiral environments, which are identical for both. Receptors and enzymes are chiral and recognize only one enantiomer (hand–glove analogy).
