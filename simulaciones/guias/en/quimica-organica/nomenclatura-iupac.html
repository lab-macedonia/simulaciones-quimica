IUPAC Organic Nomenclature

3D simulator for learning and practicing IUPAC organic nomenclature: main chain, numbering and locants, substituents in alphabetical order and functional-group priority, with 3D molecules and a practice mode.

⏱ 40 minLevel: undergraduate, early years11 multiple-choice questions · 2 essay questions

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Select the main chain and number it correctly.
  • Order substituents alphabetically.
  • Name compounds with a principal functional group and multiple bonds.

Prior knowledge

  • Structural formulas.
  • Numerical prefixes.

Simulation walkthrough

Suggested activity

Naming tournament

  1. Present 6 structures of increasing difficulty.
  2. Teams name them by hand and then check in the simulation.
  3. Each error is corrected by explaining which rule was violated.
  4. The team with the fewest rule errors wins.

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“The main chain is always the longest.”It must first include the principal functional group and the multiple bonds.
“Substituents are ordered by size.”They are ordered alphabetically, ignoring di-, tri-.
“Numbering starts from the end closest to a substituent.”Priority goes to the functional group, then the multiple bonds, and then the substituents.

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. A. 2-metilbutano — 4-carbon chain; the methyl gets the lowest locant (2).
  2. D. alphabetically: ethyl before methyl — e comes before m.
  3. C. -al — butanal, propanal…
  4. B. ketone — The ketone has higher priority: the OH is cited as hydroxy-.
  5. A. from the end closest to the double bond — Lowest locant to the double bond: pent-2-ene.
  6. D. pent- — meth, eth, prop, but, pent…
  7. C. 2-metilpropan-2-ol — 3-carbon chain with OH and CH₃ on C2.
  8. B. di-, tri-… — 2,2-dimethylbutane.
  9. B. 2-butanol — OH en C2.
  10. C. -oic acid — “…oic acid”.
  11. A. 2,2-dimethylbutane — 4-carbon chain, two methyls on C2.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. 4-chloro-2-methylpentan-1-ol (the OH defines the chain and the numbering).
  2. 6-carbon chain; methyls on C2 and C4, ethyl on C3.
