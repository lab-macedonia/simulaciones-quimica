Alcohols, Ethers and Amines

3D simulator of alcohols, ethers and amines: polarity, hydrogen bonds (dimers), boiling points, water solubility as a function of chain length, amine basicity (pKaH) and comparative reactivity.

⏱ 45 minLevel: undergraduate, early years11 multiple-choice questions · 2 essay questions

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Relate the ability to form hydrogen bonds to boiling point and solubility.
  • Compare the acidity and basicity of alcohols, ethers and amines.
  • Justify the basicity order of primary, secondary and tertiary amines.

Prior knowledge

  • Intermolecular forces.
  • Concept of Brønsted acid and base.

#StageCore idea
1PolarityC–O and C–N are polar bonds: alcohols, ethers and amines have a dipole moment
2Hydrogen bondsDonor: O–H or N–H · acceptor: lone pair on O or N
3Boiling pointsAlcohol ≫ amine ≫ ether ≈ alkane of similar mass
4Water solubilityThe –OH group is hydrophilic; the alkyl chain, hydrophobic
5Amine basicityThe N lone pair accepts H⁺: R₃N + H⁺ ⇌ R₃NH⁺
6Comparative reactivityAlcohol: weak acid, nucleophile, dehydratable · ether: inert · amine: base and nucleophile

Suggested activity

Rank boiling points

  1. Hand out ethanol, dimethyl ether, propane, ethanamine and trimethylamine.
  2. Predict the order of boiling point and solubility.
  3. Check in the simulation the number of possible H bonds.
  4. Write a three-line justification.

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“Ethers form H bonds with themselves.”They have no H bonded to O; they only accept H bonds.
“Tertiary amines are always the most basic.”In water the order depends on solvation and steric effects; it is not monotonic.
“Alcohol is a strong base.”It is a weak acid (pKa ≈ 16) and a very weak base.

B. Essay

1. Explain why ethanol (78 °C) boils higher than dimethyl ether (−24 °C) with the same formula.

2. Rank by basicity: aniline, methylamine, ammonia. Justify.

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. B. Ethanol — Ethanol: intermolecular H bonds.
  2. A. they have no O–H — They are acceptors only.
  3. D. aniline — The lone pair is delocalized into the ring (pKaH 4.6).
  4. C. decreases as the chain gets longer — The hydrophobic chain weighs more.
  5. B. weaker than O–H···O — N is less electronegative.
  6. A. polar — Electronegativity difference of 1.0.
  7. D. Trimethylamine — It has no N–H.
  8. C. the acidity of the conjugate acid of the amine — The higher the pKaH, the stronger the base.
  9. B. ethanol — Short chain: the hydrophilic OH predominates.
  10. B. weaker than O–H···O — Lower electronegativity of N.
  11. B. basic — It accepts an H⁺.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. Ethanol forms O–H···O hydrogen bonds; the ether only dipole–dipole.
  2. Methylamine > ammonia > aniline. The alkyl group donates; in aniline the lone pair is delocalized into the ring.
