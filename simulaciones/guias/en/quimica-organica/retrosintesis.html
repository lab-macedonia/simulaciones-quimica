Retrosynthesis and Multistep Synthesis

Retrosynthetic analysis in 3D: disconnection of C–C bonds, synthons and synthetic equivalents (Grignard), functional group interconversion and overall yield in linear and convergent syntheses.

Open the simulation

⏱ 55 minLevel: undergraduate, early years8 multiple-choice questions · 2 essay questions

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Make logical disconnections from a target molecule.
  • Match synthons with synthetic equivalents.
  • Calculate the overall yield of a route.

Prior knowledge

  • Grignard and addition to the carbonyl.
  • Functional groups and their interconversion.

Suggested activity

From target to reagent

  1. 1-Phenyl-1-propanol is given as the target.
  2. Propose two C–C disconnections and the reagents.
  3. With the simulation, build the route and calculate the yield.
  4. Compare a linear route with a convergent one.

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“The ⇒ arrow is a reaction.”It indicates the direction of the retrosynthetic analysis.
“Synthons exist as reagents.”They are theoretical fragments; synthetic equivalents are used.
“Overall yield = average.”It is the product of the yields.

B. Essay

1. Propose the retrosynthesis of 2-phenylpropanoic acid.

2. A linear 6-step route at 85 % versus a convergent 3 + 3 steps and a final coupling at 85 %. Compare.

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. D. “can be prepared from” — It indicates the disconnection backward.
  2. C. R–MgBr — Grignard reagent.
  3. B. Grignard + ketone — Ketone + R–MgX.
  4. A. 33 % — 0,8⁵ ≈ 0,33.
  5. D. higher overall yield — Fewer steps in the longest route.
  6. C. RMgX — RMgX is equivalent to R⁻, not to acyl.
  7. C. 65,6 % — 0,9⁴ = 0,656.
  8. A. C–C next to the C–OH — It is done with a Grignard reagent + aldehyde.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. Disconnect the C–COOH: PhCH(CH₃)MgBr + CO₂ (Grignard) or hydrolysis of the corresponding nitrile.
  2. Linear: 0.85⁶ = 37.7 %. Convergent: 0.85³ × 0.85 = 0.85⁴ = 52.2 %, better.
