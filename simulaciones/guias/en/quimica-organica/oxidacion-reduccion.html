Oxidation and reduction in organic chemistry

3D simulator of organic oxidation and reduction: carbon oxidation states, oxidation of primary, secondary and tertiary alcohols with PCC and Jones, reduction of carbonyls with NaBH₄ and LiAlH₄, hydride mechanism and reagent selectivity.

⏱ 45 minLevel: undergraduate, early years11 multiple-choice questions · 2 essay questions

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Assign oxidation states of carbon.
  • Predict oxidation products of alcohols with PCC and Jones reagent.
  • Choose the appropriate hydride (NaBH₄ or LiAlH₄) according to the functional group.

Prior knowledge

  • Alcohols, aldehydes, ketones and acids.
  • Oxidation states.

#StageCore idea
1Carbon oxidation statesMore C–O bonds (or fewer C–H) = more oxidized carbon
2Primary alcoholsPCC → aldehyde · Jones (aqueous Cr⁶⁺) → carboxylic acid
3Secondary and tertiary alcoholsThe 2° reaches a ketone; the 3° is not oxidized (it has no H on the carbinol carbon)
4Reduction of carbonylsNaBH₄ reduces aldehydes and ketones · LiAlH₄ also acids and esters
5Hydride mechanismH⁻ attacks the C=O (nucleophilic addition) and the alkoxide is protonated
6Reagent selectivityA mild hydride leaves acids and esters intact

Suggested activity

Oxidation route

  1. Start from 1-propanol and plan a route to propanal, propanoic acid and back.
  2. Choose the reagent at each step.
  3. Check the oxidation state of C with the simulation.
  4. Explain why the tertiary alcohol does not react.

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“PCC and Jones do the same thing.”PCC (anhydrous) stops at the aldehyde; Jones (aqueous) goes on to the acid.
“NaBH₄ reduces esters.”It is mild: it does not reduce them; LiAlH₄ does.
“Oxidizing is always adding O.”It is increasing the oxidation state: more C–O bonds or fewer C–H bonds.

B. Essay

1. Propose how to convert ethanol into acetic acid and into ethanal, indicating reagents.

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. B. aldehyde — In an anhydrous medium the oxidation stops at the aldehyde.
  2. A. it has no H on the carbinol carbon — Oxidation needs to remove an H from the carbon that bears the OH.
  3. D. NaBH₄ — NaBH₄ is a mild hydride.
  4. C. primary alcohol — R–CHO + H⁻ → R–CH₂–OH.
  5. B. +3 — Three bonds to O and one to C.
  6. A. the carbon of the C=O — The carbonyl carbon is electrophilic.
  7. D. orange Cr(VI) is reduced to green Cr(III) — Chromium is reduced while it oxidizes the alcohol.
  8. C. alcohol(s) — The primary alcohol from the acyl portion and the alcohol from the alkoxy group are obtained.
  9. B. propanone — Secondary → ketone.
  10. B. primary alcohol — It is fully reduced.
  11. B. −2 — Three H (−3) and one O (+1): −2.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. Ethanal: PCC/CH₂Cl₂. Acetic acid: Jones (CrO₃, H₂SO₄, acetone) or KMnO₄.
  2. It reacts violently with water releasing H₂; it is used in anhydrous ether.
