UV-Vis spectroscopy and conjugation

π → π* transition in polyenes: effect of conjugation on the HOMO–LUMO gap and the maximum wavelength, alkyl substituents, complementary color of the solution, absorption spectrum and the Beer–Lambert law, from ethene to β-carotene.

Open the simulation

⏱ 45 minLevel: undergraduate, early years9 multiple-choice questions · 2 essay questions

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Relate conjugation, HOMO–LUMO gap and λmax.
  • Predict the color of a substance from the wavelength absorbed.
  • Apply the Beer–Lambert law.

Prior knowledge

  • π molecular orbitals.
  • Electromagnetic spectrum.

Suggested activity

From ethene to β-carotene

  1. Increase the double bonds from 1 to 11.
  2. Note λmax and ΔE.
  3. Identify from which n the substance is colored.
  4. Add alkyl substituents and measure the shift.

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“More conjugation increases the gap.”It reduces it.
“The observed color is the one absorbed.”It is the complementary one.
“Absorbance does not depend on the path length.”A = ε·c·l.

B. Essay

1. Calculate the A of a 20 µM solution with ε = 30,000 and l = 1 cm.

2. Explain why β-carotene is orange.

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. A. increases — The HOMO–LUMO gap decreases.
  2. D. π → π* — From the π HOMO to the π* LUMO.
  3. C. orange — Complementary color.
  4. B. +5 nm — Woodward–Fieser rule.
  5. A. it doubles — A is proportional to c.
  6. D. E = hc/λ — The smaller λ, the greater E.
  7. A. 4,13 eV — 1239,84/300.
  8. A. reddish/purple — It absorbs green → complementary.
  9. A. 1,0 — Proportional to the path length.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. 30 000 × 2×10⁻⁵ = 0,60.
  2. It absorbs in the blue (~450 nm) because of 11 conjugated double bonds.
