Functional groups 3D

Twenty molecules with fifteen highlighted functional groups, characteristic IR bands, nomenclature priority and an identification challenge.

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Recognize the main functional groups in 3D structures.
  • Order groups by priority to name polyfunctional compounds.
  • Predict general properties (polarity, acidity) from the functional group.

Prior knowledge

  • Polar covalent bonds and electronegativity.
  • Condensed and line formulas.

Suggested activity

Functional group hunt

  1. Present a polyfunctional molecule (for example, aspirin, paracetamol, adrenaline).
  2. In pairs, they identify and label all the functional groups.
  3. They check each one in the simulation and note its nomenclature priority.
  4. They propose the group that would determine the suffix of the name.

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“Ketone and ester have the same group.”Both have C=O, but the ester also carries an O–R attached to the carbonyl.
“An amine and an amide are equivalent.”In the amide the N is bonded to C=O, its lone pair is delocalized and it is not basic.
“Phenol is an alcohol.”Its OH is attached to an aromatic sp² carbon: it is more acidic and behaves differently.

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. A. Aldehyde — R–CHO: carbonyl at the end of the chain, with an H on the carbonyl carbon.
  2. D. in the ketone the carbonyl is bonded to two carbons — Both have C=O; in the ketone the carbonyl is interior (R–CO–R′).
  3. C. Ester — A carbonyl bonded to an oxygen that carries another R′ group: ester.
  4. B. acid > ester > amide > nitrile > aldehyde > ketone > alcohol > amine — This is the IUPAC order of principal groups (suffix).
  5. A. 2-aminoethanoic acid — The acid is the principal group (suffix -oic); the amine becomes the prefix amino-.
  6. D. ≈ 2200–2260 cm⁻¹ — The C≡N triple bond absorbs at 2210–2260 cm⁻¹.
  7. C. 1 — DBE = (2·4 + 2 − 8)/2 = 1 (the C=O).
  8. B. a phenol — Ar–OH is a phenol: more acidic than an alcohol because of phenoxide stabilization.
  9. B. Amide — Carbonyl bonded to nitrogen.
  10. B. aldehyde — The C=O is at the end of the chain.
  11. C. Carboxylic acid — The carboxylic acid heads the priority list.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. Aromatic ring, carboxylic acid (–COOH) and ester (acetate, –OCOCH₃).
  2. They form dimers with two O–H···O=C hydrogen bonds; aldehydes only have dipole–dipole forces.
