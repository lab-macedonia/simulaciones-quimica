Alkynes: structure and reactions

Linear sp geometry, acidity of the terminal alkyne and acetylides, selective hydrogenation (Lindlar cis, Na/NH₃ trans, H₂/Pd alkane), hydration to ketones or aldehydes and HBr addition with regiochemistry, with substrate and product in 3D.

⏱ 55 minLevel: undergraduate, early years9 multiple-choice questions · 2 essay questions

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Describe sp hybridization and linear geometry.
  • Explain the acidity of the terminal alkyne.
  • Predict the products of hydrogenation, hydration and HX addition.

Prior knowledge

  • Alkenes and electrophilic addition.
  • Hybridization of carbon.

#StageCore idea
1sp structureThe triple-bond carbons are sp: linear geometry of 180°
2Acidity and acetylidesThe terminal H is acidic (pKa ≈ 25) because the sp carbanion has 50 % s character
3Selective hydrogenationLindlar → cis · Na/NH₃ → trans · H₂/Pd → alkane
4Hydration and tautomerismEnol → ketone (or aldehyde with hydroboration)
5HBr additionMarkovnikov regiochemistry

Suggested activity

Reaction map

  1. With 1-butyne, apply each reagent and note the product.
  2. Repeat with 2-butyne.
  3. Compare Lindlar and Na/NH₃.
  4. Explain why 2-butyne does not react with NaNH₂.

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“H₂/Pd stops at the alkene.”Without poisoning the catalyst, it goes to the alkane.
“Hydration always gives an aldehyde.”With Hg²⁺ it gives a ketone (Markovnikov); an aldehyde only by hydroboration.
“All alkynes are acidic.”Only the terminal ones.

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. C. linear, 180° — sp hybridization.
  2. B. has 50 % s character — The pair is closer to the nucleus.
  3. A. (Z)-2-butene — Syn addition on a poisoned catalyst.
  4. D. butanone — Markovnikov: enol → ketone.
  5. C. trans alkene — The radical anion is more stable trans.
  6. B. 2-butyne — It has no terminal H.
  7. A. 180° — sp.
  8. A. (E)-2-butene — Trans.
  9. A. 25 — Much more acidic than alkanes and alkenes.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. Butanone (via enol).
  2. H₂, Lindlar catalyst.
