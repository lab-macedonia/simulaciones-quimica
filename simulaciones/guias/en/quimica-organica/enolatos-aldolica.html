Enolates and Aldol Condensation

3D simulator of enolates and the aldol reaction: α-hydrogen acidity, enolate resonance, aldol addition, E1cB dehydration and Claisen–Schmidt crossed condensation, with electron arrows.

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Explain the acidity of the α hydrogen and the formation of the enolate.
  • Predict the product of aldol addition and condensation.
  • Choose base and conditions according to the synthetic goal.

Prior knowledge

  • Acidity (pKa) and resonance.
  • Nucleophilic addition to the carbonyl.

#StageCore idea
1Acidity of the α hydrogenThe H on the carbon next to the C=O is much more acidic than a normal C–H
2Enolate formationA base removes the α H; the charge is shared between C and O
3Aldol additionThe enolate attacks the carbonyl of another molecule: new C–C bond
4Protonation: the aldolThe alkoxide is protonated and gives a β-hydroxyaldehyde
5Dehydration (E1cB)With heat, the aldol loses water and forms a conjugated enal
6Crossed aldol (Claisen–Schmidt)An aldehyde without α H acts only as an electrophile
7Choosing conditionsWeak base: little enolate and reversible · strong base: complete enolate

Suggested activity

Crossed aldol

  1. Propose the reaction of benzaldehyde with acetone in NaOH.
  2. Draw enolate, addition, dehydration (E1cB).
  3. Use the simulation to compare a weak base and LDA.
  4. Explain why benzaldehyde does not self-condense.

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“The enolate attacks through oxygen.”It normally reacts through the α carbon (C-alkylation/addition).
“The aldol always gives the enal.”At low temperature it stops at the β-hydroxycarbonyl; heat dehydrates.
“Any carbonyl has α H.”Benzaldehyde and formaldehyde have no α H.

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. D. The enolate delocalizes the negative charge onto the oxygen — The conjugate base (enolate) is a C⁻/O⁻ hybrid: the charge is shared with the electronegative O.
  2. C. the α carbon — Although the charge is shared between C and O, the reaction with carbonyls forms the C–C bond through the α carbon.
  3. B. 3-hidroxibutanal — The addition product is the β-hydroxyaldehyde; dehydration to but-2-enal requires heat.
  4. A. E1cB (via enolate) — The α H is deprotonated first (carbanion/enolate) and then OH⁻ leaves from the β carbon.
  5. D. It has no α H: it can only act as an electrophile — Since it cannot form an enolate, it does not compete as a nucleophile and the mixture of products is avoided.
  6. C. LDA (pKa of HB ≈ 36) — The base must have a conjugate acid much less acidic than the ester; only LDA or NaH qualify.
  7. B. The CH₂ between two C=O groups (acetylacetone, pKa 8.9) — The carbanion charge is delocalized over two carbonyls.
  8. A. β — It is a β-hydroxyaldehyde: the OH is on the β carbon (C 3).
  9. B. form the enolate quantitatively — Strong, bulky, non-nucleophilic base.
  10. B. β — Two carbons from the C=O.
  11. B. E1cB — Enolate → expulsion of HO⁻.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. 2-methylpent-2-enal.
  2. LDA/−78 °C gives the less substituted enolate (kinetic); a weak base and equilibrium give the more substituted (thermodynamic).
