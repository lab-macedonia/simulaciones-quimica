Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Interpret the pKa scale and relate it to the stability of the conjugate base.
  • Evaluate the effect of hybridization, electronegativity, resonance and induction on acidity.
  • Calculate the constant and the direction of an acid–base equilibrium from pKa values.

Prior knowledge

  • Ka, pKa and pH.
  • Resonance and electronegativity.

Suggested activity

Rank and predict

  1. Hand out 8 compounds (ethane, ethyne, ethanol, phenol, acetic acid, ethanethiol, ethanamine, acetone).
  2. Rank them by increasing acidity without a table.
  3. Contrast with the pKa scale in the simulation.
  4. For three acid–base pairs, calculate K = 10^(pKa(HB) − pKa(HA)).

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“Higher pKa = more acidic.”The lower the pKa, the greater the acidity.
“The O–H of an alcohol is as acidic as that of a carboxylic acid.”The carboxylate is stabilized by resonance: pKa ≈ 4.8 versus ≈ 16 for the alcohol.
“S–H is less acidic than O–H because S is less electronegative.”The S⁻ anion is larger and more polarizable; size dominates down a column.

Question bank

A. Multiple choice

1. Which of these compounds is the strongest acid?

  1. Trichloroethanoic acid (pKa 0.65)
  2. Ethanoic acid (pKa 4.76)
  3. Phenol (pKa 10.0)
  4. Ethanol (pKa 15.9)

2. Why is ethyne (pKa 25) much more acidic than ethane (pKa 50)?

  1. The C–H bond of ethyne is longer
  2. Ethyne has more hydrogens
  3. Ethane is more polar
  4. The sp carbanion has 50 % s character: the pair is closer to the nucleus

3. Why is phenol (10.0) more acidic than ethanol (15.9)?

  1. Phenol has more carbon atoms
  2. Ethanol is a Lewis acid
  3. The phenoxide delocalizes the charge into the ring by resonance
  4. Phenol does not form hydrogen bonds

4. Phenol (pKa 10.0) + HO⁻ (pKa of H₂O = 15.7). What is K?

  1. ≈ 2 × 10⁻⁶ (favors reactants)
  2. ≈ 5 × 10⁵ (favors products)
  3. ≈ 1,6
  4. ≈ 5,7

5. At pH = pKa, what fraction of the acid is ionized?

  1. 50 %
  2. 10 %
  3. 90 %
  4. 99 %

6. Acetic acid (pKa 4.76) is in a solution of pH 6.76. What is [A⁻]/[HA]?

  1. 10
  2. 0,01
  3. 2
  4. 100

7. Which base can deprotonate ethyne (pKa 25) almost completely?

  1. NaOH (pKa of H₂O = 15.7)
  2. NaHCO₃ (pKa of H₂CO₃ = 6.35)
  3. NaNH₂ (pKa of NH₃ = 38)
  4. CH₃COONa (pKa 4.76)

8. Ethanethiol (10.6) is more acidic than ethanol (15.9), even though S is less electronegative than O. Why?

  1. The S–H bond is stronger
  2. S⁻ is larger and more polarizable: it spreads the charge better
  3. S forms more hydrogen bonds

9. Which base completely deprotonates an alcohol (pKa 16)?

  1. NaHCO₃
  2. NaH

10. Trichloroacetic acid is more acidic than acetic acid because of…

  1. resonance
  2. the inductive effect of the Cl atoms

  4. size

11. With pKa = 4.76 and pH = 4.76, the percentage ionized is…

  1. 0 %

  4. 100 %

B. Essay

1. Predict which way it shifts: phenol + CH₃O⁻ ⇌ phenoxide + CH₃OH, with pKa 10 and 15.5.

2. Explain why the pKa of the α proton of a ketone (≈ 19) is much lower than that of an alkane (≈ 50).

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. A. Trichloroethanoic acid (pKa 0.65) — The lower the pKa, the greater the acidity; the three chlorines stabilize the carboxylate by the inductive effect.
  2. D. The sp carbanion has 50 % s character: the pair is closer to the nucleus — The greater the s character, the more stable the orbital and the less energetic the conjugate base (sp > sp² > sp³).
  3. C. The phenoxide delocalizes the charge into the ring by resonance — The O⁻ charge is spread to the ortho and para positions of the ring, stabilizing the conjugate base.
  4. B. ≈ 5 × 10⁵ (favors products) — K = 10^(15,7 − 10,0) = 10^5,7 ≈ 5 × 10⁵.
  5. A. 50 % — At pH = pKa, [A⁻] = [HA] (Henderson–Hasselbalch).
  6. D. 100 — log([A⁻]/[HA]) = pH − pKa = 2, so the ratio is 10² = 100 (≈ 99 % ionized).
  7. C. NaNH₂ (pKa of NH₃ = 38) — The base must have a conjugate acid with a pKa well above 25; only the amide ion qualifies.
  8. B. S⁻ is larger and more polarizable: it spreads the charge better — Going down the group, size (polarizability) prevails over electronegativity.
  9. B. NaH — H⁻ is a much stronger base (pKaH ≈ 35).
  10. B. the inductive effect of the Cl atoms — The Cl atoms withdraw density and stabilize the carboxylate.
  11. C. 50 % — pH = pKa → [A⁻] = [HA].

Essay (marking guide)

  1. K = 10^(15.5 − 10) ≈ 3 × 10⁵; it shifts toward products, because the stronger acid (phenol) reacts with the stronger base.
  2. The conjugate base (enolate) delocalizes the charge onto oxygen by resonance.
