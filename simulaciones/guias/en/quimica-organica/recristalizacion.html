Recrystallization

Purification of solids by recrystallization: solubility versus temperature curves, minimum volume of hot solvent, cooling rate, crystal formation and yield.

Open the simulation

⏱ 45 minLevel: undergraduate, early years8 multiple-choice questions · 2 essay questions

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Choose a recrystallization solvent from a solubility curve.
  • Calculate the expected yield.
  • Relate the cooling rate to the size and purity of the crystals.

Prior knowledge

  • Solubility and saturation.
  • Soluble and insoluble impurities.

Suggested activity

Designing a recrystallization

  1. Given a solubility curve, calculate the minimum mass of solvent.
  2. Predict the yield at 5 °C.
  3. Simulate slow and fast cooling.
  4. Comment on what happens to the mother liquor.

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“More solvent = purer.”It dissolves more product when cold and lowers the yield.
“Cooling quickly is better.”It traps impurities in small crystals.
“Recrystallization recovers 100 %.”Some product always remains in the mother liquor.

B. Essay

1. S(100 °C) = 20 g/100 mL and S(0 °C) = 2 g/100 mL. For 10 g of solid, calculate the minimum volume and the mass recovered.

2. List three criteria for the ideal solvent.

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. C. soluble hot and insoluble cold — That way almost everything is recovered on cooling.
  2. B. reduces the yield — More solute stays dissolved when cold.
  3. A. cool slowly — Fast cooling occludes impurities.
  4. D. scratch the wall or seed — Nucleation is favored.
  5. C. dissolved solute and soluble impurities — It is the solution after filtering.
  6. B. hot — So that the product stays dissolved.
  7. B. scratch the glass or seed — They induce nucleation.
  8. A. m initial − m lost in the mother liquor — Mass recovered / initial mass.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. V = 10/20 × 100 = 50 mL. When cold 1 g remains; it recovers 9 g (90 %).
  2. High solubility when hot and low when cold; does not react with the solute; volatile and with a boiling point lower than the melting point of the solute.
