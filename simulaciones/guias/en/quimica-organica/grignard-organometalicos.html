Grignard and organometallic reagents

3D simulator of Grignard reagents: RMgX formation, polarity inversion of carbon, addition to aldehydes, ketones, CO₂ and esters, incompatibility with acidic protons and comparison with organolithiums and cuprates.

Open the simulation

⏱ 50 minLevel: undergraduate, early years9 multiple-choice questions · 2 essay questions

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Explain the polarity inversion of the carbon in RMgX.
  • Predict 1°, 2° and 3° alcohols from carbonyls.
  • Recognize incompatibilities due to acidic protons.

Prior knowledge

  • Nucleophilic addition to the carbonyl.
  • Acidity (pKa).

Simulation walkthrough

Use the lesson (bottom player) for the guided walkthrough and the Lab tab to explore freely; “Back to the lesson” resumes the script.

#StageCore idea
1Formation of the reagentR–Br + Mg → R–MgBr in anhydrous ether: the carbon goes from δ+ to δ−
2Addition to aldehydes and ketonesR⁻ attacks the C=O: formaldehyde → 1°, aldehyde → 2°, ketone → 3°
3CO₂ and estersCO₂ gives carboxylic acids (+1 C); esters react with 2 equivalents
4IncompatibilitiesRMgX is a strong base: it is protonated by water, alcohols, acids, amines and terminal alkynes
5Organolithiums and cupratesThe polarity of the C–M bond determines the reactivity

Suggested activity

Design a tertiary alcohol

  1. Given 2-phenyl-2-propanol, propose two routes with Grignard reagents.
  2. Order the steps and anhydrous conditions.
  3. With the simulation, verify the product.
  4. Calculate equivalents if starting from an ester.

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“The Grignard reagent can be prepared in ethanol.”It is destroyed by any acidic H; it requires dry ether.
“An ester gives a ketone with a Grignard reagent.”It reacts twice and gives a tertiary alcohol.
“The C of RMgX is electrophilic.”It is nucleophilic (δ−).

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. C. nucleophilic and basic — Cδ−: polarity inversion.
  2. B. tertiary alcohol — It adds to the C=O.
  3. A. carboxylic acid — Carboxylate and then acid.
  4. D. tertiary alcohol with two identical R groups — It passes through the ketone.
  5. C. dry ether — It must be aprotic and anhydrous.
  6. B. the alkane R–H — It is protonated.
  7. A. ethanol — Primary alcohol with one more C.
  8. B. alkyl halide — R–X.
  9. B. carboxylic acid — Carboxylation.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. CH₃CH₂MgBr + butan-2-one (or EtMgBr + 2-butanone), then H₃O⁺.
  2. The OH protonates the Grignard reagent; it must be protected as a silyl ether.
