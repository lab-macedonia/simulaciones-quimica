Carbohydrates: Fischer → Haworth → chair

From the Fischer projection to the Haworth projection: pyranoses and furanoses of aldoses and ketoses, anomeric carbon (α/β), chair or envelope conformation and 1,3-diaxial repulsions, with a guided lesson and challenge.

Open the simulation

⏱ 60 minLevel: undergraduate, early years3 multiple-choice questions · 2 essay questions

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Convert Fischer projections into Haworth structures.
  • Distinguish pyranoses and furanoses, and α and β anomers.
  • Predict the most stable chair conformation of a pyranose.

Prior knowledge

  • R/S stereochemistry.
  • Hemiacetals and cyclohexane conformations.

Suggested activity

From D-glucose to β-D-glucopyranose

  1. Draw D-glucose in the Fischer projection.
  2. Cyclize in the simulation and label the anomeric C1.
  3. Compare α and β in the chair.
  4. Explain why β is more abundant at equilibrium.

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“α and β are enantiomers.”They are diastereomers (anomers) that differ only at C1.
“Groups on the right in Fischer end up on top in Haworth.”They end up at the bottom.
“The chair is flat.”It is a puckered structure.

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. B. C1 of cyclic glucose — The one that was the carbonyl.
  2. B. equatorial — All its substituents are equatorial.
  3. A. interconversion of anomers in solution — Via the open-chain form.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. Its β-pyranose has all substituents in equatorial positions: it is the most stable.
  2. Aldose: terminal carbonyl (glucose). Ketose: internal carbonyl (fructose).
