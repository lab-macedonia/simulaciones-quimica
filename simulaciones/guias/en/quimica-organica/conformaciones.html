Conformations: ethane, butane and cyclohexane 3D

Newman projections with torsional energy, anti/gauche/eclipsed conformations, cyclohexane chair with ring flip, A values and 1,3-diaxial repulsion.

⏱ 50 minLevel: undergraduate, early years11 multiple-choice questions · 2 essay questions

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Draw Newman projections of ethane and butane and identify conformers.
  • Compare relative energies of staggered, eclipsed, anti and gauche conformations.
  • Predict the preferred equatorial or axial position of a substituent on cyclohexane.

Prior knowledge

  • Sigma bond and free rotation about single bonds.
  • Concept of steric strain and potential energy.

Suggested activity

Energy profile of butane

  1. Rotate the C2–C3 bond of butane in the simulation and note the energy every 60°.
  2. Plot energy versus dihedral angle and identify minima and maxima.
  3. Label anti, gauche, eclipsed and fully eclipsed.
  4. Repeat with methylcyclohexane and compare the two chairs.

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“The eclipsed conformation is a different molecule.”Conformers interconvert by rotation without breaking bonds; they are the same molecule in a different state.
“Axial is always more stable.”Bulky substituents prefer equatorial to avoid 1,3-diaxial interactions.
“The cyclohexane chair is flat.”It is a puckered structure that avoids angle and torsional strain.

B. Essay

1. Draw the two chairs of cis-1-tert-butyl-3-methylcyclohexane and indicate which predominates.

2. Explain why the rotational barrier of ethane is about 12 kJ/mol.

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. A. Staggered — In the staggered form the C–H bonds are at 60° (minimum torsional repulsion).
  2. D. 12 kJ/mol — It is ≈ 12 kJ/mol (≈ 3 kcal/mol), easy to overcome at room temperature.
  3. C. Anti — In the anti form the methyl groups are at 180°, with no steric strain.
  4. B. 3.8 kJ/mol — Steric strain between the CH₃ groups at 60°: ≈ 0.9 kcal/mol = 3.8 kJ/mol.
  5. A. Chair — The chair has angles ≈ 111° and all bonds staggered.
  6. D. axial — The flip exchanges axial and equatorial at every carbon.
  7. C. it avoids 1,3-diaxial interactions with axial H atoms — When axial, the CH₃ clashes with the axial H atoms on C3 and C5 (≈ 7.3 kJ/mol).
  8. B. tert-butyl — t-Bu (≈ 4.9 kcal/mol) is the bulkiest: it locks the conformation with itself equatorial.
  9. B. 1,3-Diaxial repulsion — It clashes with the axial H atoms on carbons 3 and 5.
  10. B. 3.8 kJ/mol — About 3.8 kJ/mol (0.9 kcal/mol).
  11. B. gauche — Gauche = 60°.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. In the cis-1,3 isomer one chair places both groups equatorial and the other both axial. The diequatorial chair predominates almost exclusively, because tert-butyl (A ≈ 21 kJ/mol) cannot go axial.
  2. It is due to torsional repulsion between pairs of eclipsed C–H bonds (≈ 4 kJ/mol per pair) and to favorable hyperconjugation effects in the staggered conformation.
