Electrophilic addition and SEAr 3D

Addition of HBr, HCl, H₂O and Br₂ to alkenes (Markovnikov, carbocation, bromonium ion) and electrophilic aromatic substitution with σ complex and substituent effects (o/m/p).

Open the simulation

⏱ 60 minLevel: undergraduate, early years11 multiple-choice questions · 2 essay questions

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Describe the mechanisms of electrophilic addition to alkenes and electrophilic aromatic substitution.
  • Apply Markovnikov's rule through carbocation stability.
  • Predict the orientation (ortho/meta/para) according to the substituent on the ring.

Prior knowledge

  • Carbocation stability and resonance.
  • Concept of nucleophile and electrophile; electron-movement arrows.

Suggested activity

Predict the product and the orientation

  1. Hand out 4 reactions: HBr + 2-methylpropene, Br₂ + cyclohexene, nitration of toluene and of nitrobenzene.
  2. Each pair draws the mechanism and predicts the major product.
  3. Contrast with the simulation, which shows the carbocation or the σ complex.
  4. Explain why nitrobenzene reacts more slowly than toluene.

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“Benzene gives addition reactions like an alkene.”It keeps its aromaticity: it reacts by substitution (SEAr).
“H always adds to the carbon with fewer hydrogens.”It is the other way around: H goes to the carbon with more H to generate the more stable carbocation.
“All substituents direct to meta.”Donors direct ortho/para and activate; withdrawers (except halogens) direct meta and deactivate.

B. Essay

1. Draw the mechanism of the bromination of benzene with Br₂/FeBr₃ indicating the σ complex.

2. Justify why the addition of Br₂ to cyclohexene gives the trans product.

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. C. It has an electron-rich π cloud — The π bond, weaker and more exposed than the σ bond, acts as a nucleophile.
  2. B. carbon with more hydrogens (CH₂) — Markovnikov's rule: the H goes to the carbon with more H atoms; the + charge stays on the 2° carbon.
  3. A. 2-bromopropane — It goes through the secondary carbocation, which is more stable.
  4. D. tertiary > secondary > primary > methyl — More alkyl groups: more hyperconjugation and donor inductive effect.
  5. C. anti, via a bromonium ion — Br⁻ attacks the bromonium from the opposite face (SN2).
  6. B. that way it preserves aromaticity — Addition would destroy the 6 delocalized π electrons (≈ 150 kJ/mol of resonance).
  7. A. activating and ortho/para directing — It donates electron density (inductive and hyperconjugation) and stabilizes the σ complexes of o/p attack.
  8. D. Meta (and deactivates the ring) — Strong withdrawer: it destabilizes the + charge on the C attached to it, which is present in the o/p complexes.
  9. B. 2-bromo-2-methylpropane — The tertiary carbocation is formed.
  10. B. NO₂⁺ — The nitronium ion is generated with H₂SO₄.
  11. C. –OCH₃ — The lone pair of O donates by resonance.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. FeBr₃ polarizes Br₂ and generates Br⁺; the ring attacks (slow step) forming the delocalized σ complex; loss of H⁺ restores aromaticity.
  2. A cyclic bromonium ion is formed; Br⁻ attacks from the opposite face (anti), giving trans-1,2-dibromocyclohexane.
