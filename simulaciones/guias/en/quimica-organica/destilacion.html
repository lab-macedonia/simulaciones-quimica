Simple and Fractional Distillation

3D setup for simple and fractional distillation of ideal binary mixtures (Raoult's law): temperature–composition diagram, theoretical plates, distillate composition and distillation curve.

⏱ 50 minLevel: undergraduate, early years9 multiple-choice questions · 2 essay questions

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Explain separation by volatility in simple and fractional distillation.
  • Interpret a T–x–y diagram and count theoretical plates.
  • Apply Raoult's law to an ideal mixture.

Prior knowledge

  • Vapor pressure and boiling point.
  • Mole fraction.

Suggested activity

Separate ethanol and water

  1. Predict what comes out first in each type of distillation.
  2. With the simulation, count plates using the staircase.
  3. Record T versus distilled volume.
  4. Explain why the azeotrope limits purity.

Common mistakes

MisconceptionCorrection
“The distillate has the composition of the mixture.”It is enriched in the more volatile component.
“More heat always separates better.”Reflux and equilibrium are lost with excessive heating.
“Boiling chips are unnecessary.”They prevent superheating and bumping.

B. Essay

1. With p°(A) = 100 kPa and p°(B) = 40 kPa at a given T, calculate the mole fraction of A in the vapor above an equimolar mixture.

2. Justify why simple distillation is enough if the boiling points differ by > 70 °C.

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. B. more volatile — It has a higher vapor pressure.
  2. A. one liquid–vapor equilibrium — One evaporation and condensation.
  3. D. bottom — Countercurrent: the condenser fills and cools better.
  4. C. rises — The residue becomes enriched in the less volatile component.
  5. B. avoid superheating and bumping — They provide bubble nuclei.
  6. A. fractional with many plates — Many equilibria are needed.
  7. B. More theoretical plates — Each plate repeats the equilibrium.
  8. B. bottom — Countercurrent, to fill the jacket.
  9. B. boils with constant composition — Vapor and liquid have the same composition.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. y_A = x_A p°_A / (x_A p°_A + x_B p°_B) = 50/(50 + 20) = 0,71.
  2. The first vapor is already almost pure in the volatile component.
