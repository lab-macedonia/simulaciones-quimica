Plate tectonics and earthquakes

3D plate tectonics simulator: convergent (Nazca subduction beneath Chile), divergent and transform boundaries, mantle convection, P and S seismic waves, seismogram, distance to the epicentre and magnitude.

Open the simulation

⏱ 45 minLevel: Grades 7 and 98 multiple-choice questions · 2 essay questions

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Explain plate motion and the features of each boundary type.
  • Describe the origin of an earthquake and how P and S waves travel.
  • Estimate the distance to the epicentre and compare earthquakes by magnitude, linking it to prevention in Chile.

Prior knowledge

  • Layers of the Earth.
  • Speed as distance/time.

Simulation walkthrough

Use the lesson (bottom player) for the guided walkthrough and the Lab tab to explore freely; “Back to the lesson” resumes the script.

#StageCore idea

2Convergent boundary: subductionAn oceanic plate sinks beneath a continental one: trench, mountains and volcanoes
3Divergent boundaryTwo plates move apart and magma forms new oceanic crust
4Transform boundaryThe plates slide sideways past each other

Suggested activity

We are the seismic network

  1. Trigger an earthquake and record the S − P difference at three different distances.
  2. Calculate each distance with d ≈ 8.4·(t_S − t_P).
  3. With a compass, draw three circles on a map and locate the epicentre.
  4. Find out what prevention measures your school has for earthquakes and tsunamis.

Common misconceptions

MisconceptionCorrection
“Continents float on lava.”The mantle is solid, although it deforms slowly; magma forms only in particular places.
“The epicentre is underground.”The focus is deep down; the epicentre is the point above it on the surface.
“A magnitude 8 earthquake is twice a magnitude 4 one.”The scale is logarithmic: it releases about 10⁶ times more energy.

B. Essay

1. Explain why Chile has so many earthquakes and volcanoes.

2. A station records S − P = 20 s. How far away was the earthquake? How many stations are needed to locate the epicentre?

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. C. a few centimetres a year — About as fast as fingernails grow.
  2. B. sinks beneath the South American plate — It is a subduction zone.
  3. D. new oceanic crust forms — Divergent boundary: magma rises.
  4. D. transform — The plates slide sideways.
  5. C. the point on the surface above the focus — The focus is deep underground.
  6. C. P — The P wave is the fastest.
  7. A. 84 km — d ≈ 8.4 km per second of difference.
  8. A. ≈ 32 times more energy — The scale is logarithmic.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. It lies over the subduction zone of the Nazca plate beneath the South American plate.
  2. ≈ 168 km; at least three stations are needed (triangulation).
