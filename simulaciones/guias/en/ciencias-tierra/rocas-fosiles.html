Rocks, strata and fossils

3D geology simulator: igneous, sedimentary and metamorphic rocks, the rock cycle, formation of strata, fossilisation, the principle of superposition, intrusions, index fossils, uplift and erosion.

Open the simulation

⏱ 45 minLevel: Grades 7 and 98 multiple-choice questions · 2 essay questions

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Classify rocks by origin and describe the rock cycle.
  • Explain how strata and fossils form.
  • Apply superposition, intrusions and index fossils to order geological events.

Prior knowledge

  • Plate tectonics and volcanoes.
  • Time scales (millions of years).

Suggested activity

Detectives of the past

  1. Look at the stratigraphic column and order the strata from oldest to youngest.
  2. Turn on the intrusion and place the dyke in your sequence.
  3. Link each index fossil to the environment it indicates (sea, swamp, dry land).
  4. Explain why there are marine fossils in the Andes mountains.

Common misconceptions

MisconceptionCorrection
“All living things become fossils.”Only a tiny fraction: quick burial and special conditions are needed.
“Rocks do not change.”They are constantly transformed in the rock cycle, over millions of years.
“A fossil is the original bone.”Usually the original material has been replaced by minerals.

B. Essay

1. Describe how a piece of granite could end up as part of a metamorphic rock.

2. A stratum with ammonites lies beneath one with trilobites in a mountain. What might have happened?

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. C. igneous, cooled slowly at depth — Slow cooling lets the crystals grow.
  2. B. sedimentary — Organisms are buried in sediment.
  3. D. heat and pressure — It is a metamorphic rock.
  4. D. the lower ones are older — Each layer settles on top of the previous one.
  5. C. younger than those strata — The layers had to exist for the magma to cut them.
  6. C. have lived for a short time in many places — That way it gives a precise age and allows correlation.
  7. A. marine strata were lifted by tectonics — Subduction lifted rocks that formed on the seabed.
  8. A. the Earth’s internal heat and the Sun’s energy — Tectonics and external agents (water, wind, ice).

Essay (marking guide)

  1. Weathering and erosion → sediment → compaction (sandstone) → heat and pressure → metamorphic rock.
  2. The strata were folded or overturned by tectonics: trilobites are older than ammonites.
