Volcanoes and eruptions

3D volcano simulator: magma chamber and vent, effusive and explosive eruptions depending on the magma’s silica and gas content, ash, lahars, technical alerts and Chile’s volcanoes, and benefits such as geothermal energy.

Open the simulation

⏱ 45 minLevel: Grade 78 multiple-choice questions · 2 essay questions

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Describe the structure of a volcano and the origin of magma.
  • Relate magma composition to the type of eruption.
  • Identify the hazards and benefits of volcanic activity and prevention measures in Chile.

Prior knowledge

  • Plate tectonics (subduction).
  • States of matter.

Suggested activity

Volcanic emergency plan

  1. Choose a Chilean volcano near you and find out its magma type and historical eruptions.
  2. Simulate it by setting the silica and gas content.
  3. Identify its main hazards (does it have a glacier? where would the ash fall?).
  4. Design a poster with the measures for each alert level.

Common misconceptions

MisconceptionCorrection
“All volcanoes explode.”Many have effusive eruptions with lava flows.
“Lava is the greatest danger.”In Chile, ash, pyroclastic flows and lahars are usually more dangerous.
“A volcano with no recent eruptions is extinct.”It may be dormant; that is why earthquakes, gases and deformation are monitored.

Classroom tools

The simulation’s «Class» button gathers these tools. None needs an account or a connection: everything stays in the student’s browser.

  • Practice: problems with new values or cases on every attempt, instant feedback and a worked solution. Types: Eruption type; Volcanic hazards; Explosivity index; Lahar distance. Use it as a lesson wrap-up (10 minutes) or homework: ask the class for a streak of 5 correct answers.
  • Predict → observe → explain: the guided lesson pauses at stages 3 and 5 and asks for a prediction before showing the result. Project it and take a show of hands before continuing.
  • Report: records practice, the challenge and predictions with time and result, plus a screenshot and the student’s notes. It can be printed or saved as PDF to hand in. It survives closing the tab and clears itself after 12 hours of inactivity; on shared computers, ask students to use «Start over» when they finish.
  • Shareable scenario and projector mode: copy a link that reopens the simulation with the same parameters (handy for assignments) and enlarge the text for projection.

B. Essay

1. Compare the eruptions of Kīlauea and Chaitén according to magma type.

2. Explain why a glacier-capped volcano is especially dangerous for towns downstream.

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. C. lava — Magma underground, lava at the surface.
  2. B. effusive, with lava flows — It is runny and the gases escape easily.
  3. D. viscous, gas-rich — The trapped gases expand suddenly.
  4. D. a mudflow of mud and meltwater — It forms when the summit glacier melts.
  5. C. the Nazca plate subducts beneath the South American plate — Subduction generates magma beneath the Andes.
  6. C. SERNAGEOMIN — It issues the technical volcanic alert.
  7. A. geothermal energy — Example: Cerro Pabellón, in Antofagasta.
  8. A. it harms lungs, engines, crops and roofs — Fine, abrasive particles of rock and glass.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. Kīlauea: runny basalt, effusive, lava flows. Chaitén: viscous, gas-rich rhyolite, explosive, ash column.
  2. The heat melts the ice and lahars form, rushing down the river channels.
