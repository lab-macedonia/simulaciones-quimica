Climate and climate change

3D simulator of the climate system: Earth’s energy balance, albedo, the natural greenhouse effect, ice and water-vapour feedbacks, rising CO₂ with real data and future scenarios.

Open the simulation

⏱ 50 minLevel: Grades 7 and 11–128 multiple-choice questions · 2 essay questions

Learning objectives

By the end, the student will be able to:

  • Describe climate as a dynamic system of interacting components.
  • Explain the energy balance, albedo and the greenhouse effect.
  • Analyse CO₂ and temperature data and evaluate future scenarios and mitigation measures.

Prior knowledge

  • Electromagnetic radiation (visible and infrared).
  • Reading time-series graphs.

Suggested activity

Reading the planet with data

  1. In the data graph, calculate how much CO₂ rose per decade since 1960.
  2. Compare it with the temperature anomaly: what relationship do you see?
  3. In the model, find which CO₂ level gives +1.5 °C and +2 °C (Paris Agreement targets).
  4. Propose three mitigation measures that are possible in your community.

Common misconceptions

MisconceptionCorrection
“The greenhouse effect is bad.”The natural one is essential for life; the problem is its intensification.
“If one day is cold, there is no global warming.”Climate is measured in averages over decades, not days.
“The ozone hole causes global warming.”They are different problems; warming is due to greenhouse gases.

Classroom tools

The simulation’s «Class» button gathers these tools. None needs an account or a connection: everything stays in the student’s browser.

  • Practice: problems with new values or cases on every attempt, instant feedback and a worked solution. Types: CO₂ forcing; Estimated warming; Temperature without atmosphere; Rate of CO₂ rise; Feedbacks. Use it as a lesson wrap-up (10 minutes) or homework: ask the class for a streak of 5 correct answers.
  • Predict → observe → explain: the guided lesson pauses at stages 3 and 5 and asks for a prediction before showing the result. Project it and take a show of hands before continuing.
  • Report: records practice, the challenge and predictions with time and result, plus a screenshot and the student’s notes. It can be printed or saved as PDF to hand in. It survives closing the tab and clears itself after 12 hours of inactivity; on shared computers, ask students to use «Start over» when they finish.
  • Shareable scenario and projector mode: copy a link that reopens the simulation with the same parameters (handy for assignments) and enlarge the text for projection.

B. Essay

1. Explain the ice–albedo feedback with an example from the Arctic.

2. CO₂ rose from 280 to 420 ppm. Calculate the forcing and the equilibrium warming with 0.8 K per W/m².

Answer key

Multiple choice

  1. C. is the average over many years — Climate: statistics over decades.
  2. B. ≈ −18 °C — Radiative equilibrium without an atmosphere.
  3. D. the fraction of sunlight reflected — Ice has a high albedo.
  4. D. absorbs more light and warms further — Ice–albedo feedback.
  5. C. 280 ppm — Today it exceeds 420 ppm.
  6. C. burning fossil fuels — Together with deforestation.
  7. A. absorb and re-emit part of the infrared downwards — They are almost transparent to visible light but absorb IR.
  8. A. generating electricity from renewables — It reduces CO₂ emissions.

Essay (marking guide)

  1. As sea ice melts, dark ocean is exposed that absorbs more light, warms and melts more ice.
  2. ΔF = 5.35·ln(1.5) = 2.17 W/m²; ΔT ≈ 1.7 °C (part of it has not yet appeared).
